\title{Asymptotically Optimal Cloning on Simple-Spectrum Unitary Orbits and Compact Spectral Sets}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let \(d\ge2\) and \(m_n/n\to\gamma>1\).  We determine the asymptotically
optimal global \(n\to m_n\) cloning fidelity for full-rank
\(d\)-dimensional states with simple spectrum, using root fidelity with the
ideal \(m_n\)-fold product output.  If the spectrum
\(p_1>\cdots>p_d>0\) is known and only the eigenbasis is unknown, the
minimax value on the unitary orbit is
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  =\prod_{i<j}
  \frac{\sqrt{\gamma}+\sqrt{q_{ij}(\gamma-1+q_{ij})}}
       {\gamma+q_{ij}},
  \qquad q_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]
We construct an explicit covariant Schur--Weyl channel that attains this
value.  If the spectrum is also unknown, a single spectrum-independent
sequence of covariant channels guarantees, uniformly on every compact subset
of the strict spectral chamber, asymptotic root fidelity at least
\[
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)
  =\left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2}
   \Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
Consequently, writing
\(\mathsf A_d:=\{x\in\R^d:\sum_i x_i=1\}\), on every nonempty compact
spectral set \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) satisfying
\(K=\overline{\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}K}^{\,\mathsf A_d}\), the
global minimax value is \(\inf_{p\in K}\Buniv(\gamma,p)\).  We also show that
\(\Buniv(\gamma,p)\) is the exact expanding-window local asymptotic minimax
value at each interior spectrum \(p\).

The factorization of \(\Buniv\) reflects two distinct tasks.  The Young label
carries the spectral fluctuation and must be dilated as a classical variable,
whereas the irreducible Schur sector carries the eigenbasis fluctuation and
must be amplified quantum mechanically.  Achievability combines exact
sampling or randomized dilation of Young labels with a multiplicity-one
Cartan-sector channel.  The converses use two-way finite-dimensional local
asymptotic normality and a flat-prior Gaussian amplification bound valid
against arbitrary channels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and results}\label{sec:introduction-results}

The no-cloning theorem excludes perfect copying of an arbitrary unknown
quantum state \cite{WoottersZurek1982,Dieks1982}.  Approximate cloning asks how
well a quantum channel can transform \(n\) identical inputs into an output
that resembles \(m\) ideal copies.  We compare the output with the entire
\(m\)-partite product state, rather than only with its one-site marginals.
For pure states, Werner's covariant channel solves the universal
finite-dimensional problem \cite{Werner1998}.  Here we study mixed states of
fixed finite dimension in the asymptotic regime \(m_n/n\to\gamma>1\).

A full-rank mixed state with simple spectrum contains two kinds of unknown
information: its eigenvalues and its eigenbasis.  This leads to two natural
problems.  In the known-spectrum problem, the eigenvalues are fixed and the
state ranges over one unitary orbit.  In the unknown-spectrum problem, both
the spectrum and the eigenbasis vary, with the spectrum restricted to a
compact subset of the strict spectral chamber.  Our goal is to determine the
best worst-case root fidelity in both problems and to give explicit channel
sequences that attain it.

The final unknown-spectrum value separates into a classical spectral factor
and a quantum orbital factor.  This factorization has a direct statistical
meaning.  On the \(n^{-1/2}\) local scale, spectral fluctuations converge to a
\((d-1)\)-dimensional classical Gaussian shift, while eigenbasis fluctuations
converge to \(\binom d2\) displaced thermal oscillator modes
\cite{GutaKahn2006LANQubits,KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}.  Producing
\(m_n\) rather than \(n\) copies therefore requires two simultaneous
operations: dilation of the classical shift and amplification of the quantum
displacements.  Their optimal fidelity contributions multiply.  If the
spectrum is known, the classical task is absent, leaving only the orbital
factor.

Schur--Weyl duality makes the same separation available directly in finite
dimension.  The law of the Young label depends only on the spectrum, while the
group action---and hence the eigenbasis dependence---resides in the
conditional irreducible
\(\U(d)\)-state.  For known spectrum, we sample the output Young label from
its exact target law.  For unknown spectrum, we obtain it from the observed
input label by a spectrum-independent randomized dilation.  Conditional on a
typical pair of input and output labels, their difference is a partition and
the multiplicity-one Cartan inclusion defines a covariant channel between the
two irreducible sectors.  The label affinity gives the classical factor, and
the sector fidelity gives the orbital factor.

To prove that no other channels do better, we use two-way local asymptotic
normality (LAN).  Any finite-dimensional cloning protocol can then be
transferred to an amplification protocol between the corresponding Gaussian
local models.  Gu\c{t}\u{a} and Matsumoto identified the least-noisy
phase-insensitive amplifier in the relevant covariant problem
\cite{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning}.  A weighted fidelity inequality
and averaging over growing parameter cubes give the required Gaussian upper
bound for arbitrary channels, without assuming Gaussianity or covariance.
The resulting local converse also supplies the global converse on compact
spectral sets.

We now state the results precisely.  Fix positive integers \(m_n\) such that
\[
  \gamma_n:=\frac{m_n}{n}\longrightarrow\gamma>1,
  \qquad r_d:=\binom d2.
\]
In particular, \(m_n>n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Let
\[
  \Delta_d^\circ
  :=\left\{p\in\mathsf A_d:p_1>\cdots>p_d>0\right\},\qquad
  \rho_p=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_d),\qquad
  \rho_{p,g}=g\rho_p g^*.
\]
We write \(B\) for root fidelity, so the target in every statement is the
entire product state \(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}\).  For
\(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\), define
\[
  q_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i},
  \qquad
  b_\gamma(q)
  =\frac{\sqrt\gamma+\sqrt{q(\gamma-1+q)}}{\gamma+q},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-values}
  \Borb(\gamma,p)=\prod_{i<j}b_\gamma(q_{ij}),
  \qquad
  \Bcl(\gamma,d)
  =\left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2},
  \qquad
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)=\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
The factor \(\Borb\) is the orbital fidelity contribution from amplifying the
unknown eigenbasis, while \(\Bcl\) is the additional classical fidelity
contribution from dilating the unknown spectral fluctuation.  The first
theorem treats a
fixed spectrum, so only the orbital factor appears.

\begin{theorem}[Known-spectrum optimum]\label{thm:known-optimum}
For every \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-optimum}
  \lim_{n\to\infty}
  \sup_{\cM_n}\inf_{g\in\U(d)}
  B\!\left(\cM_n(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),\rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}\right)
  =\Borb(\gamma,p),
\end{equation}
where the supremum is over all quantum channels from \(n\) to \(m_n\)
qudits.  The explicit sequence of channels \(\cC_n^p\) defined in
\cref{sec:known-label-kernel} asymptotically attains the displayed value.
\end{theorem}

When the spectrum is unknown, one universal construction guarantees
asymptotic fidelity at least \(\Buniv(\gamma,p)\), pointwise and uniformly on
compact spectral sets.  The same theorem records the resulting global minimax
value.

\begin{theorem}[Unknown-spectrum achievability and compact-set optimum]
\label{thm:unknown-optimum}
There exists a sequence of spectrum-independent, \(\U(d)\)-covariant channels
\(\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}\) with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every compact \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-uniform-achievability}
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}
  \inf_{\substack{p\in K\\g\in\U(d)}}
  \left[
  B\!\left(
    \cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),
    \rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}
  \right)-\Buniv(\gamma,p)
  \right]\ge0.
\end{equation}

\item[(b)] Let \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) be nonempty and
satisfy \(K=\overline{\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}K}^{\,\mathsf A_d}\).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-global-value}
  \lim_{n\to\infty}
  \sup_{\cM_n}
  \inf_{\substack{p\in K\\g\in\U(d)}}
  B\!\left(\cM_n(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),\rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}\right)
  =\inf_{p\in K}\Buniv(\gamma,p),
\end{equation}
where the supremum is over all quantum channels from \(n\) to \(m_n\)
qudits.  The same sequence \(\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}\), defined in
\cref{sec:unknown-label-kernel}, asymptotically attains the displayed value.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part~\textup{(a)} gives the achievability inequality in
part~\textup{(b)},
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}
  \sup_{\cM_n}\inf_{\substack{p\in K\\g\in\U(d)}}
  B\!\left(\cM_n(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),\rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}\right)
  \ge \inf_{p\in K}\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
The local characterization below gives the matching converse inequality,
obtained by replacing \(\liminf\) and \(\ge\) with \(\limsup\) and \(\le\),
respectively.

For the local minimax problem, let
\[
  \mathsf H=\left\{h\in\R^d:\sum_i h_i=0\right\},
  \qquad
  Y(z)=2\sum_{j<k}\left(\bar z_{jk}E_{jk}-z_{jk}E_{kj}\right),
  \quad z\in\C^{r_d},
\]
and define
\[
  g_{n,z}=\exp\!\left(\frac{Y(z)}{\sqrt n}\right),
  \qquad
  \rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}
  =g_{n,z}\diag\!\left(p+\frac h{\sqrt n}\right)g_{n,z}^*.
\]
Here \(h\) describes a local change of eigenvalues and \(z\) a local change
of eigenbasis.  Since \(Y(z)^*=-Y(z)\), one has
\(g_{n,z}\in\U(d)\).  For \(L>0\), set
\[
  \Omega_L
  =\left\{(h,z)\in\mathsf H\times\C^{r_d}:
  \|h\|_\infty\le L,\
  |\Re z_{ij}|,|\Im z_{ij}|\le L\ \text{for all }i<j\right\}.
\]
For every fixed \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\) and \(L>0\), the vector
\(p+h/\sqrt n\) belongs to \(\Delta_d^\circ\) for every
\((h,z)\in\Omega_L\) once \(n\) is sufficiently large.  For those \(n\),
set
\[
  v_{n,L}(p)
  =\sup_{\cM_n}\inf_{(h,z)\in\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(
    \cM_n\bigl((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}\bigr),
    (\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}
  \right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Local asymptotic minimax characterization]
\label{thm:unknown-local}
For every \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\),
\[
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)
  =
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)
  =\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus \(\Buniv(\gamma,p)\) is not only the performance of the explicit
universal channel; it is the intrinsic local asymptotic minimax value at each
interior spectrum.  Restricting a global protocol to such a local
neighborhood yields the upper bound in part~\textup{(b)} of
\cref{thm:unknown-optimum}.

\begin{corollary}[Uniform-gap chambers]\label{cor:compact-chamber}
Let \(0<\kappa<2/[d(d+1)]\), and set
\[
  \Delta_{d,\kappa}
  =\left\{p\in\mathsf A_d:p_d\ge\kappa,\
    p_i-p_{i+1}\ge\kappa\ (i<d)\right\}.
\]
Then \(\Delta_{d,\kappa}\) is a nonempty compact subset of
\(\Delta_d^\circ\), it satisfies
\(\Delta_{d,\kappa}
=\overline{\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}\Delta_{d,\kappa}}
^{\,\mathsf A_d}\),
and \eqref{eq:compact-global-value} holds with
\(K=\Delta_{d,\kappa}\).
\end{corollary}

At \(\kappa=2/[d(d+1)]\), the chamber is a singleton and the spectrum is
known; its optimum is therefore \(\Borb\), not \(\Buniv\).  No result below
is uniform at an eigenvalue collision or at the boundary of the probability
simplex, where the local Gaussian model changes.

\section{The common Schur--Cartan construction}\label{sec:schur-cartan}

Both cloning protocols use the same finite-dimensional architecture.  This
section first records the fidelity identities needed to compare block-diagonal
Schur states, then recalls the Schur--Weyl decomposition, and finally lifts an
arbitrary Markov kernel on Young labels to a covariant quantum channel.  The
choice of label kernel is postponed to \cref{sec:label-kernels}.

\subsection{Fidelity and block estimates}

For positive trace-class operators \(A,C\), not necessarily normalized, set
\[
  B(A,C)=\|\sqrt A\sqrt C\|_1.
\]
For states this is the unsquared Uhlmann fidelity.  For nonnegative functions
or measures on a countable set, the same symbol denotes Hellinger affinity;
in particular,
\[
  B(P,Q)=\sum_x\sqrt{P(x)Q(x)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Root-fidelity facts]\label{lem:fidelity-facts}
Root fidelity is monotone under quantum channels and jointly concave.  If
\(A,C,A',C'\) are positive trace-class operators with trace at most one, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fidelity-continuity}
  |B(A,C)-B(A',C')|
  \le \|A-A'\|_1^{1/2}+\|C-C'\|_1^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
If two positive operators are block diagonal over the same label, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-fidelity}
  B\!\left(\bigoplus_x A_x,\bigoplus_x C_x\right)
  =\sum_x B(A_x,C_x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity, joint concavity, and the direct-sum identity are standard.  For
\eqref{eq:fidelity-continuity}, the Powers--St\o rmer inequality gives
\(\|\sqrt A-\sqrt{A'}\|_2^2\le\|A-A'\|_1\), and similarly for \(C,C'\).
Therefore, by the reverse triangle inequality for the trace norm, the
triangle inequality, and Schatten H\"older,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  |B(A,C)-B(A',C')|
  &\le \|(\sqrt A-\sqrt{A'})\sqrt C\|_1
       +\|\sqrt{A'}(\sqrt C-\sqrt{C'})\|_1\\
  &\le \|\sqrt A-\sqrt{A'}\|_2\|\sqrt C\|_2
       +\|\sqrt{A'}\|_2\|\sqrt C-\sqrt{C'}\|_2\\
  &\le \|A-A'\|_1^{1/2}+\|C-C'\|_1^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last line uses \(\Tr C\le1\) and \(\Tr A'\le1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Block-fidelity lower bound]\label{lem:block-good-set}
Let \(\Pi(x,y)\) be a joint probability distribution with \(y\)-marginal
\(Q\), let \(P\) be a probability distribution on the same \(y\)-space,
and let \(\sigma_{x,y}\) and \(\tau_y\) be states.  If \(G\) is a set of
pairs and
\[
  B(\sigma_{x,y},\tau_y)\ge b\qquad((x,y)\in G)
\]
for some \(b\in[0,1]\), then
\[
  B\!\left(
    \bigoplus_y\sum_x \Pi(x,y)\sigma_{x,y},
    \bigoplus_y P(y)\tau_y
  \right)
  \ge b\left(B(Q,P)-\sqrt{\Pi(G^c)}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(Q(y)>0\), put
\[
  r(y)=\sum_{x:(x,y)\notin G}\frac{\Pi(x,y)}{Q(y)},
\]
and put \(r(y)=0\) otherwise.  Joint concavity,
\eqref{eq:block-fidelity}, and the hypothesis on \(G\) give
\[
  B\!\left(
    \bigoplus_y\sum_x\Pi(x,y)\sigma_{x,y},
    \bigoplus_y P(y)\tau_y
  \right)
  \ge b\left(B(Q,P)-\sum_y\sqrt{Q(y)P(y)}\,r(y)\right).
\]
Since \(0\le r\le1\) and \(\sum_yQ(y)r(y)=\Pi(G^c)\), Cauchy--Schwarz
yields
\[
  \sum_y\sqrt{Q(y)P(y)}\,r(y)
  \le
  \left(\sum_yQ(y)r(y)\right)^{1/2}
  \left(\sum_yP(y)r(y)\right)^{1/2}
  \le\sqrt{\Pi(G^c)}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Schur--Weyl decomposition}

Let
\[
  \mathsf Y_N=\{\lambda\vdash N:\ell(\lambda)\le d\}.
\]
Schur--Weyl duality gives
\[
  (\C^d)^{\ot N}
  \cong
  \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf Y_N}\cH_\lambda\ot\cS_\lambda,
\]
where \(\cH_\lambda\) is the irreducible \(\U(d)\)-module of highest
weight \(\lambda\) and \(\cS_\lambda\) is the corresponding Specht module.
For \(\rho_p=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_d)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-state}
  \rho_p^{\ot N}
  =\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf Y_N}
  P_{N,p}(\lambda)\,
  \rho_{p,\lambda}\ot\frac{I_{\cS_\lambda}}{\dim\cS_\lambda},
  \qquad
  P_{N,p}(\lambda)=\dim\cS_\lambda\,s_\lambda(p),
  \quad
  \rho_{p,\lambda}=\frac{\pi_\lambda(\rho_p)}{s_\lambda(p)}.
\end{equation}
For \(\rho_{p,g}=g\rho_p g^*\), replace \(\rho_{p,\lambda}\) by
\(\rho_{p,g,\lambda}=U_\lambda(g)\rho_{p,\lambda}U_\lambda(g)^*\).  The
Young law \(P_{N,p}\) depends on \(p\), but not on \(g\).

Define
\[
  \mathsf T_N(p)
  =\left\{\lambda\in\mathsf Y_N:
  \left\|\frac\lambda N-p\right\|_\infty\le N^{-1/3}\right\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Young concentration]\label{lem:young-concentration}
There is a constant \(c_d>0\), depending only on \(d\), such that for every
compact \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\),
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}P_{N,p}(\mathsf T_N(p)^c)
  \le e^{-c_d N^{1/3}}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(q=\lambda/N\).  The Weyl dimension formula gives
\(\dim\cH_\lambda\le(N+1)^{d(d-1)/2}\).  Every weight \(\xi\) of
\(\cH_\lambda\) has \(\lambda-\xi\) equal to a nonnegative integral
combination of positive roots.  Since \(p_j/p_i<1\) for \(i<j\), this gives
\(p^\xi\le p^\lambda\).  Hence
\(s_\lambda(p)\le \dim\cH_\lambda\,p^\lambda\).
The standard bounds
\(\dim\cS_\lambda\le\binom{N}{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d}\) and
\(\binom{N}{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d}p^\lambda
\le \exp[-ND(q\|p)]\) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-Young-large-deviation}
  P_{N,p}(\lambda)
  \le (N+1)^{d(d-1)/2}\exp[-ND(q\|p)],
\end{equation}
which is the finite-sample estimate underlying the Keyl--Werner spectrum
large-deviation theorem \cite{KeylWerner2001Spectrum}.

There are at most \((N+1)^{d-1}\) partitions of \(N\) with at most \(d\)
rows.  If \(\lambda\notin\mathsf T_N(p)\), then
\(\|q-p\|_1>N^{-1/3}\), and Pinsker's inequality gives
\(D(q\|p)\ge\tfrac12N^{-2/3}\).  Therefore
\[
  P_{N,p}(\mathsf T_N(p)^c)
  \le (N+1)^{d(d-1)/2+d-1}e^{-N^{1/3}/2}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), the polynomial factor is at most
\(e^{N^{1/3}/4}\).  Hence the asserted bound holds with \(c_d=1/4\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cartan-sector channel and lifting of a label kernel}

Let \(\lambda,\theta\) be dominant weights, with unit highest-weight vectors
\(v_\lambda,v_\theta\).  The Cartan component \(\cH_{\lambda+\theta}\)
occurs with multiplicity one in \(\cH_\lambda\ot\cH_\theta\).  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-inclusion}
  V_{\lambda,\theta}:\cH_{\lambda+\theta}
  \longrightarrow\cH_\lambda\ot\cH_\theta,
  \qquad
  V_{\lambda,\theta}v_{\lambda+\theta}=v_\lambda\ot v_\theta,
\end{equation}
be the normalized intertwining isometry.  For \(\mu\in\mathsf Y_n\) and
\(\nu\in\mathsf Y_m\), write \(\nu-\mu\vdash m-n\) when the coordinatewise
difference \(\nu-\mu\) is a partition of \(m-n\).  If this holds, put
\(\omega=\nu-\mu\) and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-channel-formula}
  \cC_{\mu,\nu}(X)
  =\frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
  V_{\mu,\omega}^*(X\ot I_{\cH_\omega})V_{\mu,\omega}.
\end{equation}
The partial trace
\(\Tr_{\cH_\omega}(V_{\mu,\omega}V_{\mu,\omega}^*)\) commutes with the
irreducible \(\U(d)\)-action on \(\cH_\mu\), so Schur's lemma makes it a
scalar multiple of the identity.  Taking its trace determines the scalar:
\[
  \Tr_{\cH_\omega}(V_{\mu,\omega}V_{\mu,\omega}^*)
  =\frac{\dim\cH_\nu}{\dim\cH_\mu}I_{\cH_\mu}.
\]
The map in \eqref{eq:sector-channel-formula} is completely positive,
since it is the composition of \(X\mapsto X\ot I_{\cH_\omega}\) with
compression by \(V_{\mu,\omega}\), followed by multiplication by a positive
scalar.  The displayed partial-trace identity shows that it is trace
preserving.  Intertwining gives \(\U(d)\)-covariance.

For every pair \((\mu,\nu)\in\mathsf Y_n\times\mathsf Y_m\), define a
covariant channel \(\cE_{\mu,\nu}:\cT_1(\cH_\mu)\to\cT_1(\cH_\nu)\) by
\[
  \cE_{\mu,\nu}(X)=
  \begin{cases}
    \cC_{\mu,\nu}(X),&\nu-\mu\vdash m-n,\\[1mm]
    \Tr(X)I_{\cH_\nu}/\dim\cH_\nu,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Let \(K_n:\mathsf Y_n\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Y_{m_n}\), with transition
probabilities \(K_n(\nu\mid\mu)\), be any Markov kernel.  Its Schur--Cartan
lifting is the following channel,
denoted by \(\mathcal Q_n[K_n]\): apply the Schur transform, measure
\(\mu\), discard the input Specht factor, sample \(\nu\) from
\(K_n(\cdot\mid\mu)\), apply \(\cE_{\mu,\nu}\), adjoin
\(I_{\cS_\nu}/\dim\cS_\nu\), and apply the inverse Schur transform.  Each
step is completely positive and trace preserving, so their composition is a
channel.  Because the label measurement and sampling are invariant and every
map \(\cE_{\mu,\nu}\) is covariant, \(\mathcal Q_n[K_n]\) is
\(\U(d)\)-covariant.

\begin{proposition}[Fidelity of a lifted label kernel]
\label{prop:lifted-kernel-bound}
Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  Let
\[
  \Pi_{n,p}(\mu,\nu)=P_{n,p}(\mu)K_n(\nu\mid\mu),
  \qquad
  Q_{m_n,p}(\nu)=\sum_\mu\Pi_{n,p}(\mu,\nu).
\]
Suppose \(G\subset\mathsf Y_n\times\mathsf Y_{m_n}\) satisfies
\(\nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n\) for every \((\mu,\nu)\in G\), and
\[
  B\!\left(\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),\rho_{p,\nu}\right)\ge b
  \qquad((\mu,\nu)\in G)
\]
for some \(b\in[0,1]\).  Then, for every \(g\in\U(d)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lifted-kernel-bound}
  B\!\left(
    \mathcal Q_n[K_n](\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),
    \rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}
  \right)
  \ge b\left(B(Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})-\sqrt{\Pi_{n,p}(G^c)}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Covariance makes every sector fidelity independent of \(g\).  In the output
Schur decomposition, the actual state in the \(\nu\)-block is the mixture of
\(\cE_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,g,\mu})\) with weights
\(\Pi_{n,p}(\mu,\nu)\), whereas the ideal block is
\(P_{m_n,p}(\nu)\rho_{p,g,\nu}\).  The Specht factors are identical.
Apply \cref{lem:block-good-set} with \(x=\mu\), \(y=\nu\).
\end{proof}

\section{The two label kernels and achievability}\label{sec:label-kernels}

After the common lifting has been isolated, the two protocols differ only in
how they choose the output Young label.  We first define the exact target-law
kernel for known spectrum and the randomized dilation kernel for unknown
spectrum.  We then combine their label estimates with the common sector
fidelity to prove the two achievability statements.

\subsection{Known spectrum: exact target-label sampling}
\label{sec:known-label-kernel}

Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  For every \(n\) such that \(m_n>n\), define
\[
  K_n^p(\nu\mid\mu)=P_{m_n,p}(\nu).
\]
Thus \(\nu\) is independent of \(\mu\) and has the exact target law.  Define
the full sequence of channels by
\[
  \cC_n^p(X):=
  \begin{cases}
    \Tr_{m_n+1,\ldots,n}(X),&m_n\le n,\\[1mm]
    \mathcal Q_n[K_n^p](X),&m_n>n.
  \end{cases}
\]
For \(m_n\le n\), the partial trace (the identity when
\(m_n=n\)) is \(\U(d)\)-covariant and sends \(\rho^{\ot n}\) to
\(\rho^{\ot m_n}\).  For \(m_n>n\), pairs with
\(\nu-\mu\not\vdash m_n-n\) use
\(\cE_{\mu,\nu}(X)=\Tr(X)I_{\cH_\nu}/\dim\cH_\nu\) without changing the
sampled label \(\nu\).  Hence \(\cC_n^p\) is \(\U(d)\)-covariant, and its
output Young law on \(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}\) is \(P_{m_n,p}\) for every
\(n\) and \(g\).

\subsection{Unknown spectrum: randomized rounding}
\label{sec:unknown-label-kernel}

For \(N\in\mathbb N\), let
\[
  \mathsf L_N=\left\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:\sum_i x_i=N\right\}.
\]
For every \(n\) such that \(m_n>n\), set
\(\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}=(m_n,0,\ldots,0)\).
Given \(\mu\in\mathsf Y_n\), draw independent variables
\(U_1,\ldots,U_{d-1}\), each uniform on \([-1/2,1/2)\), and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:randomized-rounding}
  \widetilde\nu_i
  =\left\lfloor\gamma_n(\mu_i+U_i)+\frac12\right\rfloor
  \quad(1\le i<d),
  \qquad
  \widetilde\nu_d=m_n-\sum_{i<d}\widetilde\nu_i.
\end{equation}
Then \(\widetilde\nu\in\mathsf L_{m_n}\).  Let
\(D_n(\widetilde\nu\mid\mu)\) denote its distribution.  Define
\(K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}:\mathsf Y_n\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Y_{m_n}\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-kernel}
  K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}(\nu\mid\mu)=
  \begin{cases}
    D_n(\nu\mid\mu),
      &\nu\ne\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}
       \text{ and }\nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n,\\[1mm]
    1-\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{\lambda\in\mathsf Y_{m_n},\\
            \lambda\ne\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}\\
            \lambda-\mu\vdash m_n-n}}
       D_n(\lambda\mid\mu),
      &\nu=\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}},\\[4mm]
    0,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus every rounded label outside \(\mathsf Y_{m_n}\), and every
\(\widetilde\nu\in\mathsf Y_{m_n}\) for which
\(\widetilde\nu-\mu\not\vdash m_n-n\), is replaced by
\(\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}\).  Define the full sequence of channels by
\[
  \cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}(X):=
  \begin{cases}
    \Tr_{m_n+1,\ldots,n}(X),&m_n\le n,\\[1mm]
    \mathcal Q_n[K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}](X),&m_n>n.
  \end{cases}
\]
For \(m_n\le n\), the partial trace (the identity when
\(m_n=n\)) is spectrum independent and \(\U(d)\)-covariant, and sends
\(\rho^{\ot n}\) to \(\rho^{\ot m_n}\).  For \(m_n>n\), the rounding
variables, \eqref{eq:rounding-kernel}, and the maps \(\cE_{\mu,\nu}\) are
spectrum independent, while \(\mathcal Q_n[K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}]\) is
\(\U(d)\)-covariant.  Thus the full sequence
\(\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}\) is spectrum independent and
\(\U(d)\)-covariant.

\subsection{Uniform estimates and achievability}

The following proposition is the only representation-theoretic asymptotic
estimate used in the cloning protocols.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform Cartan-sector fidelity]
\label{prop:sector-fidelity}
Let \(\mu_n\vdash n\) and \(\nu_n\vdash m_n\), where
\(m_n/n\to\gamma>1\), and suppose
\[
  \frac{\mu_n}{n}\longrightarrow p,
  \qquad
  \frac{\nu_n}{m_n}\longrightarrow p,
  \qquad
  p\in\Delta_d^\circ.
\]
Then \(\nu_n-\mu_n\vdash m_n-n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-fidelity-limit}
  B\!\left(
    \cC_{\mu_n,\nu_n}(\rho_{p,\mu_n}),
    \rho_{p,\nu_n}
  \right)
  \longrightarrow\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}

More precisely, let \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) and \(\delta_n\to0\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\), every tuple in the supremum below satisfies
\(\nu-\mu\vdash m-n\), and
\[
  \sup_{\substack{p\in K,\ m\in\mathbb N,\
                   \mu\in\mathsf Y_n,\ \nu\in\mathsf Y_m\\
                   |m/n-\gamma|\le\delta_n\\
                   \|\mu/n-p\|_\infty\le\delta_n\\
                   \|\nu/m-p\|_\infty\le\delta_n}}
  \left|
  B\!\left(\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),\rho_{p,\nu}\right)
  -\Borb(\gamma,p)
  \right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
An empty supremum is understood to be zero.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is an intrinsic fidelity statement; it does not assert
convergence of the entire sector channel to a Gaussian channel.  The
proof uses finite-dimensional coordinate maps indexed by \(\nu\) and a fixed
height cutoff; they are not restrictions of the LAN channel.
Their precise relation to fixed-spectrum LAN is stated after
\cref{cor:LAN-orb}.

The second estimate concerns only \(K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}\).

\begin{proposition}[Randomized Young-label dilation]
\label{prop:young-rounding}
There is a constant \(c_{\mathrm{rnd}}<\infty\) such that, for every compact
\(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
  \left.
  \begin{gathered}
    p\in K,\quad \mu\in\mathsf T_n(p),\\
    \nu\in\mathsf Y_{m_n},\quad
    K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}(\nu\mid\mu)>0
  \end{gathered}
  \right\}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n,\quad
  \|\nu-\gamma_n\mu\|_\infty\le c_{\mathrm{rnd}}.
\]
For
\[
  Q_{m_n,p}(\nu)
  :=\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf Y_n}
    P_{n,p}(\mu)K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}(\nu\mid\mu),
  \quad \nu\in\mathsf Y_{m_n},
\]
one has, for every compact \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-label-fidelity}
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left|B(Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})-\Bcl(\gamma,d)\right|
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proofs of \cref{prop:sector-fidelity,prop:young-rounding} are given in
\cref{app:sector-fidelity,app:young-rounding}, respectively.

\begin{proof}[Known-spectrum achievability in \cref{thm:known-optimum}]
Since \(m_n>n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), it suffices to consider
those \(n\).  By covariance, take \(g=I\).  For \(K_n^p\),
\[
  \Pi_{n,p}(\mu,\nu)=P_{n,p}(\mu)P_{m_n,p}(\nu),
  \qquad Q_{m_n,p}=P_{m_n,p}.
\]
Let \(G_{n,p}=\mathsf T_n(p)\times\mathsf T_{m_n}(p)\).  Young
concentration gives \(\Pi_{n,p}(G_{n,p}^c)=o(1)\).  If
\((\mu,\nu)\in G_{n,p}\), then, for every \(i<d\),
\[
  (\nu_i-\mu_i)-(\nu_{i+1}-\mu_{i+1})
  \ge (m_n-n)(p_i-p_{i+1})-2m_n^{2/3}-2n^{2/3}>0,
\]
and
\[
  \nu_d-\mu_d
  \ge(m_n-n)p_d-m_n^{2/3}-n^{2/3}>0
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus
\(\nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n\) for every \((\mu,\nu)\in G_{n,p}\).  Apply the
uniform part of
\cref{prop:sector-fidelity} with \(K=\{p\}\) and
\[
  \delta_n=|\gamma_n-\gamma|+n^{-1/3}+m_n^{-1/3}.
\]
It gives \(\eta_n\to0\) such that, uniformly on \(G_{n,p}\),
\[
  B\!\left(\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),\rho_{p,\nu}\right)
  \ge\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n.
\]
Applying \cref{prop:lifted-kernel-bound} and using
\(B(Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})=1\) yields
\[
  B\!\left(\cC_n^p(\rho_p^{\ot n}),\rho_p^{\ot m_n}\right)
  \ge
  \bigl(\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n\bigr)
  \bigl(1-\sqrt{\Pi_{n,p}(G_{n,p}^c)}\bigr)
  =\Borb(\gamma,p)-o(1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of part~\textup{(a)} of \cref{thm:unknown-optimum}]
Since \(m_n>n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), it suffices to consider
those \(n\).  By covariance, take \(g=I\), and fix
\(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\).
Uniformly for \(p\in K\), Young concentration gives
\(P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)=o(1)\).  By \cref{prop:young-rounding}, for all
sufficiently large \(n\), every pair \((\mu,\nu)\) with
\(\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)\) and \(K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}(\nu\mid\mu)>0\) satisfies
both \(\nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n\) and
\[
  \left\|\frac\nu{m_n}-p\right\|_\infty
  \le
  \frac{\|\nu-\gamma_n\mu\|_\infty}{m_n}
  +\left\|\frac\mu n-p\right\|_\infty
  \le \frac{c_{\mathrm{rnd}}}{m_n}+n^{-1/3}.
\]
Apply the uniform part of \cref{prop:sector-fidelity} with the compact set
\(K\) and
\[
  \delta_n=|\gamma_n-\gamma|+n^{-1/3}
           +\frac{c_{\mathrm{rnd}}}{m_n}.
\]
Then, for some \(\eta_n\to0\), uniformly in
\(p,\mu,\nu\) as above,
\[
  B\!\left(\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),\rho_{p,\nu}\right)
  \ge\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n.
\]
The joint label law has bad-pair probability
\(P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)\), and
\eqref{eq:rounding-label-fidelity} gives
\(B(Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})=\Bcl(\gamma,d)+o(1)\) uniformly on \(K\).
Applying \cref{prop:lifted-kernel-bound} therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  B\!\left(\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}(\rho_p^{\ot n}),
            \rho_p^{\ot m_n}\right)
  &\ge
  \bigl(\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n\bigr)
  \left(
    B(Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})
    -\sqrt{P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)}
  \right)\\
  &=\Buniv(\gamma,p)-o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly for \(p\in K\).
\end{proof}

\section{Gaussian converses and LAN transfer}\label{sec:gaussian-LAN}

The constructions above give lower bounds on the minimax fidelities.  To show
that they are optimal, we pass to the local Gaussian models in which cloning
becomes amplification.  We first solve the Gaussian problems, then state the
two-way LAN approximation, and finally transfer the resulting upper bounds
back to the finite-dimensional models.

\subsection{Gaussian amplification}

Let \(\cF\) be the one-mode Fock space with number basis
\(\{\ket{k}:k\ge0\}\), creation and annihilation operators \(a^*,a\), and
number operator \(\widehat N=a^*a\).  We use the Weyl convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Weyl-convention}
  D(z)=\exp(z a^*-\bar z a).
\end{equation}
For \(0<q<1\), set
\[
  \tau_q=(1-q)\sum_{k\ge0}q^k\proj{k}.
\]
Two thermal states commute, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermal-root-fidelity}
  B(\tau_q,\tau_{q'})
  =\frac{\sqrt{(1-q)(1-q')}}{1-\sqrt{qq'}}.
\end{equation}
For \(G\ge1\), put \(q^{(G)}=1-(1-q)/G\).  The quantum-limited
phase-insensitive amplifier \(\cA_G\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplifier-action}
  \cA_G\!\left(D(z)\tau_qD(z)^*\right)
  =D(\sqrt G\,z)\tau_{q^{(G)}}D(\sqrt G\,z)^*.
\end{equation}

For \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\), define on
\(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}=\cF^{\ot r_d}\)
\[
  \tau_p=\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}},
  \qquad
  \tau_p^{(\gamma)}
  =\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}},
\]
and
\begin{align}
  \Phi_z^{(p)}
  &=D(z)\tau_pD(z)^*,
  \label{eq:Phi-family-def}\\
  \Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}
  &=D(\sqrt\gamma\,z)\tau_pD(\sqrt\gamma\,z)^*.
  \label{eq:Psi-family-def}
\end{align}
Here \(D(z)=\bigotimes_{i<j}D(z_{ij})\).  The three relevant states are
\begin{align*}
  \text{input:}\quad
  &\Phi_z^{(p)}=D(z)\tau_pD(z)^*,\\
  \text{ideal gain-\(\sqrt\gamma\) target:}\quad
  &\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}=D(\sqrt\gamma z)\tau_pD(\sqrt\gamma z)^*,\\
  \text{amplifier output:}\quad
  &\cA_\gamma^{\ot r_d}(\Phi_z^{(p)})
    =D(\sqrt\gamma z)\tau_p^{(\gamma)}D(\sqrt\gamma z)^*.
\end{align*}
Thus the ideal target retains the thermal state \(\tau_p\); only the actual
amplifier output contains the additional noise \(\tau_p^{(\gamma)}\).  By
\eqref{eq:thermal-root-fidelity},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-mode-b}
  B(\tau_{q^{(\gamma)}},\tau_q)=b_\gamma(q),
\end{equation}
Thus, for every \(z\in\C^{r_d}\), the product-amplifier output and the
corresponding ideal state satisfy
\[
  B\!\left(\cA_\gamma^{\ot r_d}(\Phi_z^{(p)}),
           \Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\right)
  =\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]

Equip \(\mathsf H\) with its induced Euclidean measure and let
\[
  \Sigma_p=\left.(\diag(p)-pp^{\mathsf T})\right|_{\mathsf H}>0.
\]
Write \(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\) for the Gaussian law on \(\mathsf H\)
with mean \(h\) and covariance \(\Sigma_p\).  A classical--quantum state is
a positive trace-class-valued \(L^1\) density, and a channel between such
state spaces is a completely positive trace-preserving bounded map.  Root
fidelity is the integral of the pointwise root fidelities.  The statements in
\cref{lem:fidelity-facts} remain valid for these direct-integral state spaces.
Set
\[
  \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}
  =\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)},
  \qquad
  \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}
  =\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}.
\]
Let
\[
  \mathsf Z_L
  =\{z\in\C^{r_d}:|\Re z_{ij}|,|\Im z_{ij}|\le L\ \text{for all }i<j\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian amplification optimum]
\label{thm:gaussian-amplification}
For every \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{align}
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda
  \frac1{|\mathsf Z_L|}\int_{\mathsf Z_L}
  B\!\left(\Lambda(\Phi_z^{(p)}),\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\right)\,\dd z
  &=\Borb(\gamma,p),
  \label{eq:gaussian-orbital-optimum}\\
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda
  \frac1{|\Omega_L|}\int_{\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(\Lambda(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
            \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}\right)\,\dd h\,\dd z
  &=\Buniv(\gamma,p).
  \label{eq:gaussian-full-optimum}
\end{align}
The first supremum is over all quantum channels on
\(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}\); the second is over all classical--quantum channels.
The upper bounds hold without assuming Gaussianity, covariance, or a product
structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The product amplifier gives the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:gaussian-orbital-optimum}.  For the full model, combine it with the
deterministic classical map \(x\mapsto\sqrt\gamma x\).  This sends
\(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\) to
\(\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma h,\gamma\Sigma_p}\), whose fidelity with
\(\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma h,\Sigma_p}\) is
\[
  \left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2}
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\]
Multiplicativity gives \(\Buniv(\gamma,p)\).  Both constructions have
these fidelities for every parameter value and every \(L\).  The matching
upper bounds are proved together in \cref{prop:flat-prior-upper}, so the two
limits in the theorem exist and have the stated values.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local asymptotic normality}

The rotation coordinate in \cite{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional} differs
from the oscillator displacement coordinate used here.  For
\(\zeta\in\C^{r_d}\), set
\[
  X_p(\zeta)
  =\sum_{j<k}
    \frac{\zeta_{jk}E_{kj}-\bar\zeta_{jk}E_{jk}}
         {\sqrt{p_j-p_k}},
  \qquad
  (L_p\zeta)_{jk}=-\frac{\zeta_{jk}}{2\sqrt{p_j-p_k}}.
\]
Then \(L_p\) is invertible and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LAN-coordinate-map}
  X_p(L_p^{-1}z)=Y(z).
\end{equation}
In the convention of
\cite[Eqs.~(4.4) and~(4.19)]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}, the physical
unitary generated by \(X_p(\zeta)\) produces the oscillator state
\(W(w)^*\tau W(w)\), where \(W\) denotes their Weyl operator and
\(w_{jk}=\zeta_{jk}/(2\sqrt{p_j-p_k})\).  Since our convention is
\(D(z)\tau D(z)^*\) with \(D=W\), the corresponding coordinate is
\(z=-w=L_p\zeta\).  Linearity of \(L_p\) implies that multiplication of the
physical coordinate by \(\sqrt\gamma\) multiplies \(z\) by the same factor.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-dimensional LAN and target-sample scaling]
\label{thm:LAN-full}
Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  There are channels
\[
  T_N^{\mathrm{full}}:
  \cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot N})
  \longrightarrow L^1(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})),
  \qquad
  S_N^{\mathrm{full}}:
  L^1(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))
  \longrightarrow\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot N})
\]
such that, for every compact
\(C\subset\mathsf H\times\C^{r_d}\),
\begin{align}
  \sup_{(h,z)\in C}
  \left\|
    T_n^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}\right)
    -\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}
  \right\|_1&\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-full-forward}\\
  \sup_{(h,z)\in C}
  \left\|
    S_n^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}\right)
    -(\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}
  \right\|_1&\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-full-reverse}\\
  \sup_{(h,z)\in C}
  \left\|
    T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}\right)
    -\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}
  \right\|_1&\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-full-target}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward and reverse assertions
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-forward}--\eqref{eq:LAN-full-reverse} are
\cite[Theorem~4.3]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}, after identifying their
\((d-1)\)-dimensional spectral coordinate with \(\mathsf H\) and applying
the coordinate change \eqref{eq:LAN-coordinate-map}.  For the classical
factor, let
\[
  \iota:\R^{d-1}\longrightarrow\mathsf H,
  \qquad
  \iota(u_1,\ldots,u_{d-1})
  =\left(u_1,\ldots,u_{d-1},-\sum_{i<d}u_i\right),
\]
and let \(A\) be the matrix of \(\iota\).  In the coordinates of
\cite[Eq.~(3.3)]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}, the classical covariance
is the \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) matrix
\[
  V(p)_{ij}=\delta_{ij}p_i-p_ip_j,
  \qquad i,j<d.
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
  A V(p)A^{\mathsf T}=\diag(p)-pp^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Hence, when \(h=\iota(u)\), the pushforward of their classical Gaussian is
\(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\).  The coordinate change
\eqref{eq:LAN-coordinate-map} identifies their oscillator state with
\(\Phi_z^{(p)}\).  Their Schr\"odinger-picture maps are completely positive
and trace preserving on the trace-class and classical--quantum \(L^1\)
spaces shown above.

The target-sample assertion \eqref{eq:LAN-full-target} is a consequence
of the first assertion.  Indeed, the identity
\[
  \rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}
  =\rho_{p,\sqrt{\gamma_n}h,\sqrt{\gamma_n}z}^{(m_n)}
\]
follows from \(\sqrt{\gamma_n}/\sqrt{m_n}=1/\sqrt n\) and the real linearity
of \(Y\).  Apply the forward LAN statement at sample size \(m_n\) on one
compact set containing all scaled parameters.  Since \(\gamma_n\to\gamma\),
translation of Gaussian densities and Weyl conjugation are uniformly
trace-norm continuous there, which gives \eqref{eq:LAN-full-target}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-spectrum LAN]\label{cor:LAN-orb}
Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  There are channels
\[
  T_N^{\mathrm{orb}}:\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot N})\to\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}),
  \qquad
  S_N^{\mathrm{orb}}:\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\to\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot N})
\]
such that, uniformly for \(z\) in compact sets,
\begin{align}
  \|T_n^{\mathrm{orb}}(\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot n})-\Phi_z^{(p)}\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-known-forward}\\
  \|S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Phi_z^{(p)})-\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot n}\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-known-reverse}\\
  \|T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot m_n})
       -\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-known-target}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Restrict \cref{thm:LAN-full} to \(h=0\).  The classical Gaussian factor
\(\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p}\) is independent of \(z\).  Discard it in the
forward channel and adjoin it before the reverse channel.  Contractivity of
trace norm gives the three assertions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The sector calculation and fixed-spectrum LAN.}
Consider the label pairs \(\mu,\nu\) in the uniform family of
\cref{prop:sector-fidelity}.  For a fixed height cutoff \(L\), let
\(P_{\nu,L}\) project onto the sum of the exact weight spaces of height at most
\(L\) in \(\cH_\nu\), and let \(P_L^{\cF}\) project onto the Fock number
vectors whose total root height is at most \(L\).  The unitary
\(J_{\nu,L}\) in \eqref{eq:finite-coordinate-unitary} maps the first space to
the second.  Uniformly for those pairs as \(n\to\infty\), the proof of
\cref{prop:sector-fidelity} establishes
\begin{align*}
  J_{\nu,L}P_{\nu,L}\rho_{p,\nu}P_{\nu,L}J_{\nu,L}^*
  &\longrightarrow P_L^{\cF}\tau_pP_L^{\cF},\\
  J_{\nu,L}P_{\nu,L}
  \cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu})
  P_{\nu,L}J_{\nu,L}^*
  &\longrightarrow P_L^{\cF}\tau_p^{(\gamma)}P_L^{\cF}.
\end{align*}
Thus the ideal target sector approaches \(\tau_p\), whereas the actual
Cartan-sector output approaches the noisier state \(\tau_p^{(\gamma)}\) on
each fixed collection of exact weight spaces.  The maps \(J_{\nu,L}\)
depend on \(\nu\) and \(L\); they compare finite matrices and do not define
a channel on the full Schur direct sum.

The LAN channel acts on the complete tensor-power experiment.  In the exact
decomposition \eqref{eq:schur-state}, the Young label is classical and the
conditional quantum state is \(\rho_{p,\mu}\).  At the centered state, the
full LAN limit is
\[
  \mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p}\ot\tau_p.
\]
The first factor describes the rescaled Young-label fluctuation.  For fixed
spectrum it is independent of the unknown eigenbasis, so
\cref{cor:LAN-orb} discards it and retains the orbital state \(\tau_p\).
Therefore the label-conditioned finite-matrix comparison and fixed-spectrum
LAN concern different maps and different levels of the Schur decomposition;
there is no second mapping of the same state to an independent Gaussian
state.

\begin{lemma}[LAN fidelity transfer]\label{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer}
Let \(\{\rho_{n,\theta}\}_{\theta\in C}\) and
\(\{\sigma_{n,\theta}\}_{\theta\in C}\) be input and target models on a
compact parameter set \(C\).  Let \(\{\Phi_\theta\}\) and
\(\{\Psi_\theta\}\) be limiting models, and suppose channels \(S_n,T_n\)
satisfy
\[
  \sup_{\theta\in C}\|S_n(\Phi_\theta)-\rho_{n,\theta}\|_1\to0,
  \qquad
  \sup_{\theta\in C}\|T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta})-\Psi_\theta\|_1\to0.
\]
For every channel \(\cM_n\), put \(\Gamma_n=T_n\circ\cM_n\circ S_n\).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LAN-transfer}
  \inf_{\theta\in C}B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_\theta),\Psi_\theta)
  \ge
  \inf_{\theta\in C}B(\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),\sigma_{n,\theta})-o(1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the two uniform approximation errors be \(\delta_n\) and \(\eta_n\).
Trace-norm contractivity and \eqref{eq:fidelity-continuity} give, uniformly in
\(\theta\),
\[
  B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_\theta),\Psi_\theta)
  \ge
  B(T_n\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta}))
  -\sqrt{\delta_n}-\sqrt{\eta_n}.
\]
Monotonicity under \(T_n\) proves \eqref{eq:LAN-transfer}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completion of the minimax proofs}

We now apply the same transfer principle in three increasingly broad settings.
The fixed-spectrum Gaussian bound completes \cref{thm:known-optimum}; the full
Gaussian bound gives the pointwise local result \cref{thm:unknown-local}; and
that local result, together with uniform achievability, yields the compact-set
statement in \cref{thm:unknown-optimum}.

\begin{proof}[Known-spectrum converse and completion of \cref{thm:known-optimum}]
Let \(u_n(p)\) be the finite-\(n\) minimax value in
\eqref{eq:known-optimum}, and choose \(\cM_n\) with worst-case fidelity at
least \(u_n(p)-1/n\).  For fixed \(L\), set
\[
  \Gamma_n=T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{orb}}\circ\cM_n\circ S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}.
\]
The local orbit \(\{g_{n,z}:z\in\mathsf Z_L\}\) is contained in the full
unitary orbit.  Hence \cref{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer} gives
\[
  u_n(p)
  \le
  \inf_{z\in\mathsf Z_L}
  B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_z^{(p)}),\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)})+o(1).
\]
The infimum is no larger than the average.  Taking \(\limsup_n\), then the
supremum over Gaussian-model channels, and finally \(L\to\infty\),
\eqref{eq:gaussian-orbital-optimum} gives
\(\limsup_n u_n(p)\le\Borb(\gamma,p)\).  Together with the lower bound in
\cref{sec:known-label-kernel}, this proves \cref{thm:known-optimum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:unknown-local}]
Fix \(L\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), the spectra
\(p+h/\sqrt n\), \((h,z)\in\Omega_L\), lie in one compact subset of
\(\Delta_d^\circ\).  Since
\(\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}=\rho_{p+h/\sqrt n,g_{n,z}}\), part~\textup{(a)} of
\cref{thm:unknown-optimum} and continuity of \(\Buniv(\gamma,\cdot)\) give
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)\ge\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]

For the converse, choose \(\cM_n\) with worst-case fidelity at least
\(v_{n,L}(p)-1/n\), and set
\[
  \Gamma_n=T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{full}}\circ\cM_n\circ S_n^{\mathrm{full}}.
\]
By \cref{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer},
\[
  v_{n,L}(p)
  \le
  \inf_{(h,z)\in\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(
    \Gamma_n(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
    \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}
  \right)+o(1).
\]
Average over \(\Omega_L\), take \(\limsup_n\), and then use
\eqref{eq:gaussian-full-optimum} as \(L\to\infty\).  This gives the matching
upper bound.  Since both \(\limsup_n v_{n,L}(p)\) and
\(\liminf_n v_{n,L}(p)\) are nonincreasing in \(L\), the two outer limits
exist and equal \(\Buniv(\gamma,p)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of part~\textup{(b)} of \cref{thm:unknown-optimum}]
Let \(w_n(K)\) be the finite-\(n\) minimax value on the
left of \eqref{eq:compact-global-value}.  Part~\textup{(a)} of
\cref{thm:unknown-optimum} gives
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}w_n(K)
  \ge\inf_{p\in K}\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
For the reverse inequality, fix
\(p_0\in\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}K\).  For every fixed \(L\), all local
spectra \(p_0+h/\sqrt n\), \((h,z)\in\Omega_L\), lie in \(K\) for
sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus \(w_n(K)\le v_{n,L}(p_0)\).  Letting first
\(n\to\infty\) and then \(L\to\infty\), \cref{thm:unknown-local} gives
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}w_n(K)\le\Buniv(\gamma,p_0).
\]
Take the infimum over
\(\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}K\).  Its closure in \(\mathsf A_d\) is
\(K\), and \(\Buniv(\gamma,\cdot)\) is continuous, so the resulting upper
bound is \(\inf_{p\in K}\Buniv(\gamma,p)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:compact-chamber}]
For every \(p\in\Delta_{d,\kappa}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chamber-threshold}
  1=dp_d+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}i(p_i-p_{i+1})
  \ge\frac{\kappa d(d+1)}2.
\end{equation}
If \(\kappa<2/[d(d+1)]\), choose
\(\kappa'\in(\kappa,2/[d(d+1)])\) and set
\[
  p_i=\frac1d+\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-i\right)\kappa'.
\]
Then every adjacent gap exceeds \(\kappa\) and
\[
  p_d=\frac1d-\frac{d-1}{2}\kappa'>\frac{2}{d(d+1)}>\kappa.
\]
Thus
\(\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}\Delta_{d,\kappa}\ne\varnothing\).
It is convex and closed in the simplex, so it is compact and
satisfies
\[
  \Delta_{d,\kappa}
  =\overline{\operatorname{int}_{\mathsf A_d}\Delta_{d,\kappa}}
             ^{\,\mathsf A_d}.
\]
Apply part~\textup{(b)} of \cref{thm:unknown-optimum}.

If \(\kappa>2/[d(d+1)]\), \eqref{eq:chamber-threshold} makes the chamber
empty.  At equality, every inequality in \eqref{eq:chamber-threshold} is an
equality, so the chamber is a singleton.  The corresponding problem has
known spectrum and is governed by \cref{thm:known-optimum}.
\end{proof}

\section{Comparison and discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Pure-state cloning and random purification.}
For a \(D\)-dimensional pure state, Werner's universal \(n\to m\) cloner has
root fidelity
\[
  \left(
  \frac{\binom{n+D-1}{D-1}}
       {\binom{m+D-1}{D-1}}
  \right)^{1/2}.
\]
The purify-and-clone construction of
\cite{LiTheilHarrowChuang2026} first applies an \(n\to n\)
random-purification channel and, when \(m>n\), then applies the optimal
pure-state \(n\to m\) cloner on the \(dr\)-dimensional purification space.
Its Eq.~(1a) and Theorem~S3.1 give, for rank-\(r\) inputs, the all-output
squared-fidelity guarantee
\[
  F_{\mathrm{all}}^*
  \ge
  \frac{\binom{n+dr-1}{dr-1}}
       {\binom{m+dr-1}{dr-1}}.
\]
Here \(F\) is squared Uhlmann fidelity, consistently with the convention
that squared Bures distance is \(2(1-\sqrt F)\).  Tracing out the purifying
registers gives the same lower bound for all-output mixed-state cloning.
Thus, in the root-fidelity convention of this paper, the guarantee is
\[
  \left(
  \frac{\binom{n+dr-1}{dr-1}}
       {\binom{m+dr-1}{dr-1}}
  \right)^{1/2}
  \longrightarrow\gamma^{-(dr-1)/2}.
\]
This is a guarantee for a larger universal rank-\(r\) model, not an exact
fixed-orbit cloning value.

For \(0<q<1\), \(b_\gamma(q)>\gamma^{-1/2}\).  Indeed, after multiplying by
the positive denominator and cancelling \(\sqrt\gamma\), the inequality is
equivalent to
\(\sqrt{q(\gamma-1+q)}>q/\sqrt\gamma\); the difference of the squares is
\(q(\gamma-1)(1+q/\gamma)>0\).  Therefore
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)>\gamma^{-d(d-1)/4}.
\]
For full rank, this is strictly larger than the quoted universal guarantee
\(\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/2}\).  Since
\(2\sqrt\gamma/(1+\gamma)>\gamma^{-1/2}\), also
\[
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)>\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/4}
  >\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/2}.
\]

\paragraph{Broadcasting versus cloning.}
The target here is the full product \(\rho^{\ot m}\).  Broadcasting requires
only selected output marginals to reproduce the input and permits
correlations.  Exact broadcasting and superbroadcasting results therefore do
not conflict with the product-state bounds proved here
\cite{BarnumCavesFuchsJozsaSchumacher1996,
DArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2005,
BuscemiDArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2005Optimal,
BuscemiDArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2006,
DArianoPerinottiSacchi2006Continuous}.  Earlier mixed-state cloning bounds
and restricted constructions also use different model classes or figures of
merit \cite{Rastegin2003Upper,Rastegin2002Relative,
Rastegin2003StateDependent,Fan2003,FanLiuShi2007,Kazakov2007}.

\paragraph{A shorter existence proof.}
Preliminary localization followed by forward LAN, Gaussian amplification,
and reverse LAN would give the same asymptotic values if the LAN errors were
uniform jointly over estimated centers and the required growing local
windows.  This route is conceptually shorter but does not give the explicit
finite-\(n\) Schur--Cartan channel or the spectrum-independent rounding
kernel.  Related localization--LAN constructions were used for mixed-qubit
purification and dilution and for asymptotic reversal of qudit channels
\cite{BowlesGutaAdesso2011Purification,BowlesGutaAdesso2012Reversal}.

\paragraph{Limiting regimes.}
For fixed \(p\), as \(\gamma\to\infty\),
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  =\gamma^{-d(d-1)/4}
  \prod_{i<j}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{p_j}{p_i}}\right)
  \left(1+O(\gamma^{-1})\right).
\]
If \(\gamma=1+\eps\), then
\[
  b_{1+\eps}(q)=1-\frac{\eps^2}{8q}+O(\eps^3),
\]
so the known-spectrum loss is quadratic in \(\gamma-1\).  In contrast, the
quoted full-rank random-purification guarantee has a linear loss.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Natural extensions include quantitative finite-\(n\) bounds, degenerate or
rank-deficient spectra, a finite-dimensional covariant converse, and
efficient implementation of the Cartan intertwiners.  Associativity of the
normalized Cartan inclusions gives, whenever \(\omega_1,\omega_2\) are
partitions,
\[
  \cC_{\mu+\omega_1,\,\mu+\omega_1+\omega_2}
  \circ\cC_{\mu,\,\mu+\omega_1}
  =\cC_{\mu,\,\mu+\omega_1+\omega_2}.
\]
Decomposing a partition into columns therefore factors every sector channel
into column-addition channels.  Controlling a long product of these
channels may provide quantitative estimates.  Degenerate and boundary
spectra require separate analysis because stabilizers appear and some
oscillator modes disappear.

\section{Exact-weight PBW calculation of the sector fidelity}
\label{app:sector-fidelity}

This appendix proves \cref{prop:sector-fidelity}.  The argument compares the
sector-channel output and the target only on finitely many complete weight
spaces at a time.  It does not extend those comparisons to a single global
isometry from every irreducible sector into a common Fock space.

\subsection{PBW vectors on complete weight spaces}

Identify the positive roots of \(\U(d)\) with
\[
  \mathsf R_+=\{e_i-e_j:1\le i<j\le d\}.
\]
For \(\alpha=e_i-e_j\), set
\[
  F_\alpha=E_{ji},
  \qquad E_\alpha=E_{ij},
  \qquad H_\alpha=[E_\alpha,F_\alpha]=E_{ii}-E_{jj},
  \qquad \Delta_\alpha^\lambda=\lambda_i-\lambda_j.
\]
Fix a total order on \(\mathsf R_+\).  For
\(\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}\), write
\[
  F^{\mathbf r}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}^{\prec}F_\alpha^{r_\alpha},
  \qquad |\mathbf r|=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha.
\]
For a nonnegative simple-root combination \(\eta\), let
\(\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\) be its simple-root height and put
\[
  \mathsf P(\eta)
  =\left\{\mathbf r:\sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}r_\alpha\alpha=\eta\right\}.
\]
The set \(\mathsf P(\eta)\) is finite, and the PBW theorem implies that
\(\{F^{\mathbf r}v_\lambda:\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)\}\) spans
\(\cH_\lambda[\lambda-\eta]\) \cite{FultonHarris1991}.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-height-subspace}
  \mathcal K_{\lambda,L}
  =\bigoplus_{\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\le L}
    \cH_\lambda[\lambda-\eta].
\end{equation}
Let \(P_{\lambda,L}\) denote the orthogonal projection onto
\(\mathcal K_{\lambda,L}\).

\begin{lemma}[PBW Gram matrices at fixed height]
\label{lem:PBW-fixed-height}
Let \(\lambda=\lambda^{(N)}\) range over dominant weights satisfying
\[
  cN\le\Delta_\alpha^\lambda\le CN
  \qquad(\alpha\in\mathsf R_+)
\]
for fixed \(0<c<C<\infty\).  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-PBW-vectors}
  \widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda
  =\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}^{\prec}
   \frac{F_\alpha^{r_\alpha}}
        {\sqrt{r_\alpha!}(\Delta_\alpha^\lambda)^{r_\alpha/2}}
   v_\lambda.
\end{equation}
For every fixed \(L\), uniformly over
\(\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\le L\) and
\(\mathbf r,\mathbf s\in\mathsf P(\eta)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-Gram-fixed-height}
  \langle\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda,
         \widetilde e_{\mathbf s}^\lambda\rangle
  =\delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}+O_L(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(N\), the full Gram matrix
\(G_{\lambda,\eta}\) on every such exact-weight block is invertible.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-block-orthonormalization}
  (e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda)_{\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)}
  =
  (\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda)_{\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)}
  G_{\lambda,\eta}^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
then these vectors form an orthonormal basis of
\(\cH_\lambda[\lambda-\eta]\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-orthonormal-close}
  e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda
  =\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda+O_L(N^{-1/2})
\end{equation}
uniformly for \(\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\le L\) and
\(\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)\), then
\(|\mathbf r|\le\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\), because every positive root has
positive simple-root height.  Thus only words of bounded length occur at a
fixed \(L\).

Put
\[
  \widehat F_\alpha=\frac{F_\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda}},
  \qquad
  \widehat E_\alpha=\frac{E_\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda}}.
\]
Fix \(L\).  For every \(R\ge0\) and \(\alpha\in\mathsf R_+\),
\(\widehat F_\alpha\) maps \(\mathcal K_{\lambda,R}\) into
\(\mathcal K_{\lambda,R+\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)}\), while
\(\widehat E_\alpha\) maps \(\mathcal K_{\lambda,R}\) into
\(\mathcal K_{\lambda,R}\).  Every matrix coefficient under consideration
contains a word of total length at most \(2L\).  Commuting two root operators
replaces them by a root operator or a Cartan operator and therefore never
increases this length.  Since every positive root has height at most
\(d-1\), every intermediate vector lies in the fixed cutoff
\[
  \mathcal K_{\lambda,R_L},
  \qquad R_L:=2L(d-1).
\]

For every fixed \(R\), the restrictions
\[
  \widehat E_\alpha:\mathcal K_{\lambda,R}\to\mathcal K_{\lambda,R},
  \qquad
  \widehat F_\alpha:\mathcal K_{\lambda,R}
  \to\mathcal K_{\lambda,R+d-1}
\]
have operator norms bounded uniformly in \(N\) and \(\lambda\).  Indeed,
restrict to \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\alpha)\) and decompose orthogonally into
irreducible strings.  A weight \(\lambda-\eta\) with
\(\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\le R\) has \(H_\alpha\)-eigenvalue
\(\Delta_\alpha^\lambda+O_R(1)\).  If a component at this weight lies \(t\)
steps below the top of its string, then \(\lambda-\eta+t\alpha\) is also a
weight, so \(t\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)\le\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\) and
\(t=O_R(1)\).  The string highest weight is therefore
\(\Delta_\alpha^\lambda+O_R(1)\).  The standard
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-coefficients and \(E_\alpha=F_\alpha^*\) give the claimed
uniform bounds.  Applying this observation to the finitely many cutoffs
with \(R\le R_L\) controls every intermediate operator below.

On the same fixed collection of cutoffs, the matrix-unit commutator formula
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-root-commutator}
  [\widehat E_\alpha,\widehat F_\beta]
  =\delta_{\alpha\beta}I+O_L(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
For \(\alpha=\beta\), use
\([E_\alpha,F_\alpha]=H_\alpha\) and
\(H_\alpha/\Delta_\alpha^\lambda=I+O_L(N^{-1})\).  For
\(\alpha\ne\beta\), the commutator is zero or one root operator of norm
\(O_L(\sqrt N)\), divided by a quantity of order \(N\).  Here and below,
operator-norm estimates are taken between fixed enlarged height cutoffs,
whose sizes depend only on \(L\) and \(d\).

Move the normalized raising operators in
\(\langle v_\lambda,(\widehat F^{\mathbf r})^*
\widehat F^{\mathbf s}v_\lambda\rangle\) successively to the right using
\eqref{eq:normalized-root-commutator}.  A term containing only the scalar
parts \(\delta_{\alpha\beta}I\) can survive only if every raising operator is
paired with a lowering operator of the same root.  This is possible exactly
when \(\mathbf r=\mathbf s\), and then the number of pairings is
\(\prod_\alpha r_\alpha!\).  Every remaining term either contains at least
one \(O_L(N^{-1/2})\) commutator remainder, leaves an unpaired raising
operator that annihilates \(v_\lambda\), or leaves a nonzero lowering weight
orthogonal to \(v_\lambda\).  The number of terms and all intervening
operator norms are bounded in terms of \(L,d,c,C\).  Dividing by the
factorials in \eqref{eq:normalized-PBW-vectors} proves
\eqref{eq:PBW-Gram-fixed-height}.

Each exact-weight Gram matrix is therefore
\(I+O_L(N^{-1/2})\).  Functional calculus gives
\(G_{\lambda,\eta}^{-1/2}=I+O_L(N^{-1/2})\), which proves
\eqref{eq:PBW-orthonormal-close}.  Since the PBW vectors span the weight
space and their Gram matrix is invertible, the orthonormalized vectors form a
basis of the entire weight space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Character normalization and fixed-height matrices}

For \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)\), write \(p^a=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}\).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform character normalization]
\label{lem:uniform-character-normalization}
Let \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\), let \(\delta_N\to0\), and suppose
\(p\in K\), \(\lambda\vdash N\), and
\(\|\lambda/N-p\|_\infty\le\delta_N\).  Then, uniformly over this family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-character-normalization}
  \frac{p^\lambda}{s_\lambda(p)}
  =\prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)+o(1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the numerator in the Weyl character formula as
\[
  \sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_d}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)A_\sigma,
  \qquad
  A_\sigma=\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+d-\sigma(i)}.
\]
For \(\sigma\ne\mathrm{id}\),
\[
  \frac1N\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{id}}}{A_\sigma}
  =\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i}{N}\log p_i
   -\sum_i\frac{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}{N}\log p_i+O(N^{-1}).
\]
At \(\lambda/N=p\), strict rearrangement makes the limit positive.
Compactness of \(K\) and finiteness of \(\mathfrak S_d\) give a uniform
positive lower bound when \(\lambda/N\) is sufficiently close to \(p\).
Thus every nonidentity term is exponentially smaller than the identity term.
Dividing that term by
\[
  \det(p_i^{d-j})_{i,j=1}^d
  =\prod_i p_i^{d-i}
   \prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)
\]
proves \eqref{eq:uniform-character-normalization}.
\end{proof}

For the remainder of the appendix, index the number basis of
\(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}\) by
\(\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}\), and write it as
\(\{\ket{\mathbf r}\}\).  Define the finite-rank projection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fock-height-projection}
  P_L^{\cF}
  =\sum_{\operatorname{ht}(\sum_\alpha r_\alpha\alpha)\le L}
    \proj{\mathbf r}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed \(L\) and all sufficiently large \(N\), each highest
weight \(\lambda\) satisfying the gap bounds in
\cref{lem:PBW-fixed-height} has the orthonormal basis
\(e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda\) of \(\mathcal K_{\lambda,L}\) constructed there.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-coordinate-unitary}
  J_{\lambda,L}:\mathcal K_{\lambda,L}\longrightarrow
  P_L^{\cF}\cF_{\mathrm{orb}},
  \qquad
  J_{\lambda,L}e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda=\ket{\mathbf r}.
\end{equation}
This is a unitary between the two finite-dimensional spaces.  It is defined
only on \(\mathcal K_{\lambda,L}\), and it depends on \(\lambda\) and \(L\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:sector-fidelity}]
It suffices to prove the compact-uniform assertion.  For the pointwise
assertion, choose a compact neighborhood \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) of
\(p\) and set
\[
  \delta_n=\left|\frac{m_n}{n}-\gamma\right|
  +\left\|\frac{\mu_n}{n}-p\right\|_\infty
  +\left\|\frac{\nu_n}{m_n}-p\right\|_\infty.
\]
Then \(\delta_n\to0\), and the compact-uniform assertion applies.

Fix \(K\) and \(\delta_n\) as in the proposition, and put
\(\omega=\nu-\mu\).  Uniformly over the allowed parameters,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \left\|\frac\omega{m-n}-p\right\|_\infty
  &=\left\|
    \frac{m/n}{m/n-1}\left(\frac\nu m-p\right)
    -\frac1{m/n-1}\left(\frac\mu n-p\right)
  \right\|_\infty\\
  &\le\frac{m/n+1}{m/n-1}\,\delta_n=o(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every coordinate and every adjacent gap is uniformly positive on \(K\).
Hence \(\omega\vdash m-n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  For
\(\alpha=e_i-e_j\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:splitting-fraction}
  s_{\alpha,n}
  =\frac{\Delta_\alpha^\mu}{\Delta_\alpha^\nu}
  =\frac1\gamma+o(1),
  \qquad
  1-s_{\alpha,n}
  =\frac{\Delta_\alpha^\omega}{\Delta_\alpha^\nu}.
\end{equation}
There are constants \(0<c_K<C_K<\infty\) such that, for all large \(n\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-sector-gaps}
  c_Kn
  \le\Delta_\alpha^\mu,\Delta_\alpha^\nu,\Delta_\alpha^\omega
  \le C_Kn
  \qquad(\alpha\in\mathsf R_+).
\end{equation}

Let
\(V=V_{\mu,\omega}:\cH_\nu\to\cH_\mu\ot\cH_\omega\).  Intertwining and the
highest-weight normalization give
\[
  VF_\alpha^{(\nu)}
  =\bigl(F_\alpha^{(\mu)}\ot I+I\ot F_\alpha^{(\omega)}\bigr)V.
\]
Expanding one root block at a time in the fixed PBW order yields the exact
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-PBW-exact}
  V\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu}
  =\sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
    \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
    \widetilde e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}
    \ot\widetilde e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-coefficients}
  \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
  =\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}
   \sqrt{\binom{r_\alpha}{a_\alpha}
   s_{\alpha,n}^{a_\alpha}
   (1-s_{\alpha,n})^{r_\alpha-a_\alpha}}.
\end{equation}
The two tensor-leg operators commute within each root block; multiplying the
expanded blocks in the fixed order preserves the PBW order in each tensor
factor.  By \cref{lem:PBW-fixed-height}, for every fixed \(L\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-orthonormal-fixed-height}
  Ve_{\mathbf r}^{\nu}
  =\sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
    \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
    e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}\ot e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega}
    +o(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly whenever
\(\operatorname{ht}(\sum_\alpha r_\alpha\alpha)\le L\), where the norm of
the error tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\).

Put
\[
  \sigma_{\mu,\nu}=\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),
  \qquad
  c_\lambda(p)=\frac{p^\lambda}{s_\lambda(p)},
  \qquad
  q_\alpha=\frac{p_j}{p_i}\quad(\alpha=e_i-e_j).
\]
Because \(e_{\mathbf a}^\mu\) has weight
\(\mu-\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\alpha\),
\[
  \rho_{p,\mu}e_{\mathbf a}^\mu
  =c_\mu(p)\prod_\alpha q_\alpha^{a_\alpha}e_{\mathbf a}^\mu.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:cartan-orthonormal-fixed-height} into
\eqref{eq:sector-channel-formula} gives, uniformly at fixed \(L\),
\begin{align}
  \langle e_{\mathbf r}^\nu,
    \sigma_{\mu,\nu}e_{\mathbf s}^\nu\rangle
  &={}
  \delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}
  \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}c_\mu(p)
  \prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}
  \bigl(1-s_{\alpha,n}+s_{\alpha,n}q_\alpha\bigr)^{r_\alpha}
  +o(1),
  \label{eq:sector-output-fixed-matrix}\\
  \langle e_{\mathbf r}^\nu,
    \rho_{p,\nu}e_{\mathbf s}^\nu\rangle
  &=\delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}
    c_\nu(p)\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{r_\alpha}.
  \label{eq:sector-target-fixed-matrix}
\end{align}
For \eqref{eq:sector-output-fixed-matrix}, the inner products in the two
tensor factors force \(\mathbf a=\mathbf b\) and
\(\mathbf r-\mathbf a=\mathbf s-\mathbf b\), hence
\(\mathbf r=\mathbf s\); the remaining sum factors into binomial sums.

By \cref{lem:uniform-character-normalization},
\[
  c_\mu(p)=\prod_\alpha(1-q_\alpha)+o(1),
  \qquad
  c_\nu(p)=\prod_\alpha(1-q_\alpha)+o(1).
\]
The Weyl dimension formula and \eqref{eq:splitting-fraction} give
\[
  \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
  =\prod_{\alpha=e_i-e_j}
    \frac{\Delta_\alpha^\mu+j-i}{\Delta_\alpha^\nu+j-i}
  =\gamma^{-r_d}+o(1).
\]
The identities
\[
  q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}
  =1-\frac{1-q_\alpha}{\gamma},
  \qquad
  1-q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}=\frac{1-q_\alpha}{\gamma},
\]
give
\[
  \gamma^{-r_d}\prod_\alpha(1-q_\alpha)
  \prod_\alpha\bigl(q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}\bigr)^{r_\alpha}
  =\prod_\alpha(1-q_\alpha^{(\gamma)})
   \bigl(q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}\bigr)^{r_\alpha}.
\]
Because there are only finitely many indices at height at most \(L\),
\eqref{eq:sector-output-fixed-matrix}--\eqref{eq:sector-target-fixed-matrix}
imply convergence in trace norm, for every fixed \(L\):
\begin{align}
  \left\|
  J_{\nu,L}P_{\nu,L}\sigma_{\mu,\nu}P_{\nu,L}J_{\nu,L}^*
  -P_L^{\cF}\tau_p^{(\gamma)}P_L^{\cF}
  \right\|_1&\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:output-fixed-height-convergence}\\
  \left\|
  J_{\nu,L}P_{\nu,L}\rho_{p,\nu}P_{\nu,L}J_{\nu,L}^*
  -P_L^{\cF}\tau_pP_L^{\cF}
  \right\|_1&\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:target-fixed-height-convergence}
\end{align}
uniformly over the allowed family, where
\[
  \tau_p=\bigotimes_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha},
  \qquad
  \tau_p^{(\gamma)}
  =\bigotimes_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}}.
\]

Both \(\rho_{p,\nu}\) and \(\sigma_{\mu,\nu}\) commute with the diagonal
torus.  The first assertion is immediate; the second follows from torus
invariance of \(\rho_{p,\mu}\) and covariance of \(\cC_{\mu,\nu}\).
Consequently, \(P_{\nu,L}\) reduces both states, and the direct-sum identity
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  B(\sigma_{\mu,\nu},\rho_{p,\nu})
  ={}&B(P_{\nu,L}\sigma_{\mu,\nu}P_{\nu,L},
        P_{\nu,L}\rho_{p,\nu}P_{\nu,L})\\
  &+B((I-P_{\nu,L})\sigma_{\mu,\nu}(I-P_{\nu,L}),
       (I-P_{\nu,L})\rho_{p,\nu}(I-P_{\nu,L})).
\end{aligned}
\]
For positive trace-class operators \(A,C\),
\(B(A,C)\le\sqrt{\Tr A\,\Tr C}\).  Hence the second term is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-tail-fidelity-bound}
  \sqrt{\Tr[(I-P_{\nu,L})\rho_{p,\nu}]},
\end{equation}
because \(\Tr[(I-P_{\nu,L})\sigma_{\mu,\nu}]\le1\).

For fixed \(L\), \eqref{eq:target-fixed-height-convergence} gives
\[
  \Tr[(I-P_{\nu,L})\rho_{p,\nu}]
  =\Tr[(I-P_L^{\cF})\tau_p]+o(1)
\]
uniformly as \(n\to\infty\) for the fixed \(L\).  Since \(K\) is
compact in \(\Delta_d^\circ\), there is \(b_K<1\) such that
\(q_\alpha\le b_K\) for every \(p\in K\) and every positive root
\(\alpha\).  The occupation numbers under \(\tau_p\) are independent
geometric variables with parameters at most \(b_K\).  If their total
occupation is \(s\), then their total root height is at most \((d-1)s\).
Moreover, the probability of any fixed occupation vector of total occupation
\(s\) is at most \(b_K^s\), and there are
\(\binom{s+r_d-1}{r_d-1}\) such vectors.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-thermal-height-tail}
\begin{aligned}
  \sup_{p\in K}\Tr[(I-P_L^{\cF})\tau_p]
  &\le
  \sum_{s>L/(d-1)}\binom{s+r_d-1}{r_d-1}b_K^s\\
  &\longrightarrow0
  \qquad(L\to\infty).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The same direct-sum decomposition for
\(B(\tau_p^{(\gamma)},\tau_p)\) bounds its complementary part by the square
root of the same target thermal tail.  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\left|B(\sigma_{\mu,\nu},\rho_{p,\nu})
       -B(\tau_p^{(\gamma)},\tau_p)\right|\\
 &\quad\le
 \left|B(P_{\nu,L}\sigma_{\mu,\nu}P_{\nu,L},
         P_{\nu,L}\rho_{p,\nu}P_{\nu,L})
       -B(P_L^{\cF}\tau_p^{(\gamma)}P_L^{\cF},
          P_L^{\cF}\tau_pP_L^{\cF})\right|\\
 &\qquad+
 \sqrt{\Tr[(I-P_{\nu,L})\rho_{p,\nu}]}
 +\sqrt{\Tr[(I-P_L^{\cF})\tau_p]}.
\end{aligned}
\]

By \eqref{eq:fidelity-continuity} and
\eqref{eq:output-fixed-height-convergence}--\eqref{eq:target-fixed-height-convergence},
for every fixed \(L\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &B(P_{\nu,L}\sigma_{\mu,\nu}P_{\nu,L},
     P_{\nu,L}\rho_{p,\nu}P_{\nu,L})\\
  &\qquad\longrightarrow
  B(P_L^{\cF}\tau_p^{(\gamma)}P_L^{\cF},
    P_L^{\cF}\tau_pP_L^{\cF})
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly.  First let \(n\to\infty\) at fixed \(L\), and then let
\(L\to\infty\) using
\eqref{eq:sector-tail-fidelity-bound}--\eqref{eq:uniform-thermal-height-tail}.
This proves
\[
  B(\sigma_{\mu,\nu},\rho_{p,\nu})
  -B(\tau_p^{(\gamma)},\tau_p)\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly.  Finally,
\[
  B(\tau_p^{(\gamma)},\tau_p)
  =\prod_{i<j}B(\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}},\tau_{q_{ij}})
  =\Borb(\gamma,p)
\]
by \eqref{eq:thermal-root-fidelity} and \eqref{eq:one-mode-b}.
\end{proof}

\section{Young local limit and randomized rounding}
\label{app:young-rounding}

This appendix proves \cref{prop:young-rounding}.  Define the half-open
fundamental cell
\[
  \mathsf C
  =\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}t_i(e_i-e_d):-\frac12\le t_i<\frac12\right\}
  \subset\mathsf H.
\]
Volumes are taken with the induced \((d-1)\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
The rounding rule \eqref{eq:randomized-rounding} is equivalent, outside a
null set of cell boundaries, to choosing the unique
\(\widetilde\nu\in\mathsf L_{m_n}\) for which
\[
  \gamma_n(\mu+U)\in\widetilde\nu+\mathsf C,
  \qquad
  U=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}U_i(e_i-e_d).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell-kernel}
  D_n(\widetilde\nu\mid\mu)
  =\frac{|\gamma_n(\mu+\mathsf C)\cap(\widetilde\nu+\mathsf C)|}
         {\gamma_n^{d-1}|\mathsf C|}.
\end{equation}
All label laws below are viewed on the full affine lattice
\(\mathsf L_N\); a Young law is extended by zero away from partitions.
For \(\lambda\in\mathsf L_N\), define
\[
  \mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda-Np+\mathsf C}{\sqrt N},
  \qquad
  (\mathcal E_{N,p}R)(x)
  =\frac{N^{(d-1)/2}}{|\mathsf C|}R(\lambda)
  \quad(x\in\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)).
\]
The cells tile \(\mathsf H\) up to null boundaries, and
\(\mathcal E_{N,p}\) preserves both \(\ell^1\)-distance and Hellinger
affinity.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Young local limit]\label{lem:young-local-limit}
For every compact \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:young-local-limit}
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|\mathcal E_{N,p}P_{N,p}-\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\right\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(M_{N,p}\) be the multinomial law on nonnegative integer compositions
of \(N\), extended by zero to \(\mathsf L_N\).  For
\(\lambda\in\mathsf Y_N\),
\[
  P_{N,p}(\lambda)=\dim\cS_\lambda\,s_\lambda(p),
  \qquad
  \dim\cS_\lambda
  =N!\frac{\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i)}
  {\prod_i(\lambda_i+d-i)!},
\]
and the Weyl character formula gives
\[
  s_\lambda(p)
  =\frac{\det[p_i^{\lambda_j+d-j}]_{i,j}}
  {\prod_{i<j}(p_i-p_j)}.
\]
Set
\(\mathsf W_{N,p}=\{\lambda\in\mathsf L_N:
\|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{2/3}\}\).  Uniform strict separation on
\(K\) implies that every point of
\(\mathsf W_{N,p}\cap\operatorname{supp}M_{N,p}\) is a partition for all
large \(N\).

On this set, both \((\lambda_i+d-i)/N\) and \(\log p_i\) are uniformly
strictly decreasing.  Strict rearrangement, compactness of \(K\), and
finiteness of \(\mathfrak S_d\) imply that every nonidentity term in the
character determinant is exponentially smaller than the identity term,
uniformly.  Thus
\[
  s_\lambda(p)
  =\frac{\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_i+d-i}}
  {\prod_{i<j}(p_i-p_j)}\bigl(1+O(e^{-cN})\bigr)
\]
for one \(c>0\).  Comparing with
\[
  M_{N,p}(\lambda)=\frac{N!}{\prod_i\lambda_i!}\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_i}
\]
gives
\[
  \frac{P_{N,p}(\lambda)}{M_{N,p}(\lambda)}
  =\bigl(1+O(e^{-cN})\bigr)
  \prod_{i<j}\frac{\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i}{N(p_i-p_j)}
  \prod_i\frac{\lambda_i!(Np_i)^{d-i}}{(\lambda_i+d-i)!}.
\]
Indeed,
\[
  \frac{\lambda_i!(Np_i)^{d-i}}{(\lambda_i+d-i)!}
  =\prod_{a=1}^{d-i}\frac{Np_i}{\lambda_i+a},
\]
and every displayed factor equals \(1+O_K(N^{-1/3})\) on
\(\mathsf W_{N,p}\).  Young concentration and Hoeffding's inequality
control the two laws outside this set, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:young-multinomial-l1}
  \sup_{p\in K}\|P_{N,p}-M_{N,p}\|_{\ell^1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Fix \(\beta_0\in(1/2,2/3)\) and put
\(x_\lambda=(\lambda-Np)/\sqrt N\).  Uniform Stirling expansion and a
third-order Taylor expansion give, on
\(\|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{\beta_0}\),
\[
  \log M_{N,p}(\lambda)
  =-\frac{d-1}{2}\log(2\pi N)-\frac12\sum_i\log p_i
   -\frac12\sum_i\frac{x_{\lambda,i}^2}{p_i}
   +O_K\!\left(N^{3\beta_0-2}+N^{\beta_0-1}+N^{-1}\right).
\]
For \(x\in\mathsf H\),
\(\langle x,\Sigma_p^{-1}x\rangle=\sum_i x_i^2/p_i\).  If
\(b_i=e_i-e_d\), then
\(\det[\langle b_i,b_j\rangle]_{i,j<d}=d\), so
\(|\mathsf C|=\sqrt d\).  A principal cofactor of
\(\diag(p)-pp^{\mathsf T}\) is \(\prod_i p_i\), while its kernel is
\(\operatorname{span}\{(1,\ldots,1)\}\); hence
\(\det_{\mathsf H}\Sigma_p=d\prod_i p_i\).  Because
\(\beta_0>1/2\), the term \(N^{3\beta_0-2}\) dominates the other two terms
in the remainder.  Since \(3\beta_0-2<0\),
\[
  \frac{N^{(d-1)/2}}{|\mathsf C|}M_{N,p}(\lambda)
  =\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x_\lambda)(1+o(1))
\]
uniformly on that window.

Let
\[
  V_{N,p}=\{\lambda\in\mathsf L_N:
  \|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{\beta_0}\},
  \qquad
  U_{N,p}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in V_{N,p}}\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda).
\]
Hoeffding's inequality and the uniform eigenvalue bounds for \(\Sigma_p\)
give constants \(c_K,C_K>0\) such that
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}\left[
  M_{N,p}(V_{N,p}^c)
  +\int_{U_{N,p}^c}\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\,\dd x
  \right]
  \le C_K e^{-c_K N^{2\beta_0-1}}.
\]
The rescaled cells have diameter \(O(N^{-1/2})\).  On the window,
\(\|\nabla\log\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\|=O_K(1+\|x\|)\), so the relative
oscillation of the Gaussian density across one cell is
\[
  \exp\bigl(O_K(N^{-1/2}+N^{\beta_0-1})\bigr)-1=o(1).
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \|\mathcal E_{N,p}M_{N,p}-\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\|_1
  \le{}&M_{N,p}(V_{N,p}^c)
  +\int_{U_{N,p}^c}\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\,\dd x\\
  &+\sup_{\substack{\lambda\in V_{N,p}\\
                     x\in\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)}}
  \left|
  \frac{N^{(d-1)/2}M_{N,p}(\lambda)}
       {|\mathsf C|\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)}-1
  \right|,
\end{aligned}
\]
which tends uniformly to zero.  Combine this with
\eqref{eq:young-multinomial-l1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Continuous limit of randomized dilation]
\label{lem:randomized-dilation}
Let \(\widetilde Q_{m_n,p}=P_{n,p}D_n\) be the rounded label law before the
replacement in \eqref{eq:rounding-kernel}.  For every compact
\(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{align}
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|\mathcal E_{m_n,p}\widetilde Q_{m_n,p}
  -\varphi_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}\right\|_1&\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:rounded-density-limit}\\
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left|B(\widetilde Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})-\Bcl(\gamma,d)\right|
  &\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:raw-rounded-affinity}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a density \(f\) on \(\mathsf H\), set
\[
  (\operatorname{dil}_a f)(y)=a^{-(d-1)/2}f(y/\sqrt a),
\]
and let \(\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}\) replace a density on each target cell
by its average there.  For \(u\in\mathsf C\), the centered coordinates satisfy
\[
  \frac{\gamma_n(\mu+u)-m_np}{\sqrt{m_n}}
  =\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,\frac{\mu+u-np}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Thus multiplication by \(\gamma_n\) in the unscaled affine hyperplane becomes
multiplication by \(\sqrt{\gamma_n}\) in central-limit coordinates, and
\eqref{eq:cell-kernel} records the target cell containing the dilated point.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rounding-intertwining}
  \mathcal E_{m_n,p}\widetilde Q_{m_n,p}
  =\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}\operatorname{dil}_{\gamma_n}
   (\mathcal E_{n,p}P_{n,p}).
\end{equation}
The averaging operator is an \(L^1\)-contraction, and dilation is an
\(L^1\)-isometry.  By \cref{lem:young-local-limit}, the expression before
cell averaging converges uniformly to
\(\varphi_{0,\gamma_n\Sigma_p}\).  This compact Gaussian family converges
uniformly in \(L^1\) to \(\varphi_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}\).  The target cells
have diameter \(O(m_n^{-1/2})\), and the same Gaussian family is uniformly
translation-continuous in \(L^1\), so cell averaging changes the limit by
\(o(1)\).  This proves \eqref{eq:rounded-density-limit}.

Applying \cref{lem:young-local-limit} at sample size \(m_n\) gives
\[
  \mathcal E_{m_n,p}P_{m_n,p}\longrightarrow\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}
\]
uniformly in \(L^1\).  The embedding preserves Hellinger affinity, and
\eqref{eq:fidelity-continuity} also holds for nonnegative \(L^1\) densities.
Therefore
\[
  B(\widetilde Q_{m_n,p},P_{m_n,p})
  \longrightarrow
  B(\mathsf N_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p},\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p})
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d)
\]
uniformly on \(K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:young-rounding}]
For \(i<d\), the nearest-integer rule gives
\[
  |\widetilde\nu_i-\gamma_n\mu_i|
  \le\frac{\gamma_n+1}{2}.
\]
Since the coordinate sums of \(\widetilde\nu\) and \(\gamma_n\mu\) are both
\(m_n\),
\[
  |\widetilde\nu_d-\gamma_n\mu_d|
  \le\frac{(d-1)(\gamma_n+1)}2.
\]
Thus one may take
\[
  c_{\mathrm{rnd}}
  =\frac{d-1}{2}\left(1+\sup_n\gamma_n\right)<\infty.
\]

Compactness of \(K\) gives \(c_0>0\) such that, for all large \(n\), every
\(p\in K\) and \(\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)\) satisfy
\[
  \mu_d\ge c_0n,
  \qquad
  \mu_i-\mu_{i+1}\ge c_0n\quad(i<d).
\]
For every rounded label in the support of \(D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)\),
\[
  (\widetilde\nu-\mu)_d
  \ge(\gamma_n-1)\mu_d-c_{\mathrm{rnd}},
\]
and
\[
  (\widetilde\nu_i-\mu_i)
  -(\widetilde\nu_{i+1}-\mu_{i+1})
  \ge(\gamma_n-1)(\mu_i-\mu_{i+1})-2c_{\mathrm{rnd}}.
\]
These quantities are positive for all large \(n\), uniformly on \(K\).
Hence \(\widetilde\nu-\mu\) is a partition; since \(\mu\) is a partition,
so is \(\widetilde\nu\).
Moreover, \(\widetilde\nu_d>\mu_d>0\), so
\(\widetilde\nu\ne\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}\).  Hence, for
\(\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)\), the law \(D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)\) is supported on
\(\mathsf Y_{m_n}\), assigns no mass to \(\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}\), and
undergoes no replacement in \eqref{eq:rounding-kernel}.  Consequently,
\[
  K_n^{\mathrm{rnd}}(\nu\mid\mu)=D_n(\nu\mid\mu),
  \qquad \nu\in\mathsf Y_{m_n},
\]
and every \(\nu\) in this support satisfies
\(\nu-\mu\vdash m_n-n\) and
\(\|\nu-\gamma_n\mu\|_\infty\le c_{\mathrm{rnd}}\).

Let \(\eps_{n,p}\) be the probability, under \(\mu\sim P_{n,p}\) and
\(\widetilde\nu\sim D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)\), that \(\widetilde\nu\) is
replaced in \eqref{eq:rounding-kernel}.  The preceding argument gives
\[
  \eps_{n,p}\le P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c),
\]
which tends uniformly to zero by \cref{lem:young-concentration}.

By \eqref{eq:raw-rounded-affinity}, the unreplaced output law has affinity
\(\Bcl(\gamma,d)+o(1)\) with \(P_{m_n,p}\), uniformly on \(K\).  Moving the
rejected mass to \(\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}\) changes the law by at most
\(2\eps_{n,p}\) in \(\ell^1\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:fidelity-continuity} for classical distributions therefore gives
\eqref{eq:rounding-label-fidelity}.
\end{proof}

\section{Unified Gaussian flat-prior upper bound}
\label{app:gaussian-converse}

This appendix proves the two upper bounds in
\cref{thm:gaussian-amplification}.  We average over large parameter sets and
test the oscillator output against a compact product operator before taking
a limit.  In the classical--quantum case, we also use the scalar measures
obtained by tracing out the oscillator.  The classical parameter space has
dimension \(k=0\) for the orbital problem and \(k=d-1\) for the full
problem.  All trace-class-valued integrals below are Bochner integrals in
trace norm.

\subsection{Weighted fidelity and compact limits}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted root-fidelity bound]\label{lem:weighted-fidelity}
Let \(R,T\) be positive trace-class operators, and let \(W\) be bounded,
positive, and injective.  Define
\[
  \Tr(TW^{-1})
  :=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
  \Tr\bigl(T(W+\varepsilon I)^{-1}\bigr)\in[0,\infty].
\]
If this quantity is finite, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-quantum-fidelity}
  B(R,T)^2\le\Tr(RW)\Tr(TW^{-1}).
\end{equation}

Let \(\mathsf E\) be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space,
let \(R\in L^1(\mathsf E;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))\) be positive, let
\(g\) be a probability density on \(\mathsf E\), and let \(\chi>0\) be
measurable.  The notation \(gT\) means the density \(y\mapsto g(y)T\).
Whenever the right-hand side below is finite,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-hybrid-fidelity}
  B(R,gT)^2
  \le
  \left(\int_{\mathsf E}\chi(y)\Tr[R(y)W] \,\dd y\right)
  \left(\int_{\mathsf E}\frac{g(y)}{\chi(y)}\,\dd y\right)
  \Tr(TW^{-1}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(W_\varepsilon=W+\varepsilon I\), apply the Schatten H\"older
inequality to
\[
  R^{1/2}T^{1/2}
  =(R^{1/2}W_\varepsilon^{1/2})
   (W_\varepsilon^{-1/2}T^{1/2}).
\]
This gives
\[
  B(R,T)
  \le\sqrt{\Tr(RW_\varepsilon)}
       \sqrt{\Tr(TW_\varepsilon^{-1})}.
\]
Squaring and letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), using monotone convergence
for the second trace, proves \eqref{eq:weighted-quantum-fidelity}.  For the
hybrid state,
\[
  B(R,gT)
  =\int_{\mathsf E}\sqrt{g(y)}\,B(R(y),T)\,\dd y.
\]
Apply the first inequality pointwise and then Cauchy--Schwarz to the factors
\(\sqrt{\chi(y)\Tr[R(y)W]}\) and
\(\sqrt{g(y)/\chi(y)}\).  This gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-hybrid-fidelity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-observable limits of completely positive maps]
\label{lem:compact-cp-limit}
Let \(\cH_0,\cH_1\) be separable Hilbert spaces and let
\(\Gamma_j:\cT_1(\cH_0)\to\cT_1(\cH_1)\) be completely positive maps with
uniformly bounded trace-norm operator norms.  There are a subsequence and a
completely positive map
\(\Gamma:\cT_1(\cH_0)\to\cT_1(\cH_1)\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-map-convergence}
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma(X)K]
\end{equation}
for every \(X\in\cT_1(\cH_0)\) and every compact
\(K\in\mathcal K(\cH_1)\).  If
\(\Tr\Gamma_j(X)\le c\Tr X\) for every \(X\ge0\), then
\(\Tr\Gamma(X)\le c\Tr X\).

Suppose in addition that
\(U:G\to\mathcal U(\cH_0)\) and
\(V:G\to\mathcal U(\cH_1)\) are unitary representations and that, for every
fixed \(g\in G\) and \(X\in\cT_1(\cH_0)\),
\[
  \|\Gamma_j(U_g X U_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
Then
\[
  \Gamma(U_g X U_g^*)=V_g\Gamma(X)V_g^*
\]
for every \(g\in G\) and \(X\in\cT_1(\cH_0)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(M=\sup_j\|\Gamma_j\|_{1\to1}\), and let
\(\mathcal D\subset\mathcal K(\cH_1)\) be a countable norm-dense
\(\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\)-linear \(*\)-algebra of finite-rank operators.  A
diagonal weak-* compactness argument gives a subsequence for which
\(\Gamma_j^*(K)\) converges weak-* in \(\mathcal B(\cH_0)\) for every
\(K\in\mathcal D\).  The bound
\(\|\Gamma_j^*(K)\|\le M\|K\|\) extends both the limit and the convergence
to every compact \(K\), producing a bounded map
\(\Gamma^*:\mathcal K(\cH_1)\to\mathcal B(\cH_0)\).

To check complete positivity, fix \(r\) and \(A\ge0\) in
\(M_r(\mathcal K(\cH_1))\), and choose
\(B_\ell\in M_r(\mathcal D)\) with \(B_\ell\to A^{1/2}\).  Since
\(\Gamma_j^{*(r)}(B_\ell^*B_\ell)\ge0\), first letting \(j\to\infty\) and
then \(\ell\to\infty\) gives \(\Gamma^{*(r)}(A)\ge0\).

Since \(\mathcal K(\cH_1)^*=\cT_1(\cH_1)\), define \(\Gamma(X)\) by
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma(X)K]=\Tr[X\Gamma^*(K)]
  \qquad(K\in\mathcal K(\cH_1)).
\]
Then \(\Gamma\) is completely positive and
\eqref{eq:compact-map-convergence} holds.  If \(P_N\uparrow I\) are
finite-rank projections and \(X\ge0\), then
\[
  \Tr\Gamma(X)
  =\sup_N\lim_j\Tr[\Gamma_j(X)P_N]
  \le c\Tr X.
\]
Finally, for fixed \(g,X\) and compact \(K\), the operator
\(V_g^*KV_g\) is compact, and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\Tr\!\left[
    \bigl(\Gamma(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma(X)V_g^*\bigr)K
  \right]\\
  &\qquad=\lim_j\Tr\!\left[
    \bigl(\Gamma_j(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\bigr)K
  \right]=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Compact operators separate trace-class operators, so the limiting map is
covariant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The least-noise oscillator moment}

Fix \(\gamma>1\).  For \(s\ge1\) and
\(q_1,\ldots,q_s\in(0,1)\), put
\[
  q_\ell^{(\gamma)}=1-\frac{1-q_\ell}{\gamma},
  \qquad
  \tau=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^s\tau_{q_\ell},
  \qquad
  \tau^{(\gamma)}
  =\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^s\tau_{q_\ell^{(\gamma)}}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Least-noise moment]
\label{prop:least-noise-moment}
Let \(\Gamma\) be a completely positive trace-nonincreasing map on the
\(s\)-mode trace class satisfying
\[
  \Gamma(D(z)XD(z)^*)
  =D(\sqrt\gamma\,z)\Gamma(X)D(\sqrt\gamma\,z)^*
  \qquad(z\in\mathbb C^s).
\]
If
\[
  W(\widehat{\mathbf N})
  =\prod_{\ell=1}^s w_\ell(\widehat N_\ell),
\]
where every \(w_\ell\) is bounded, nonnegative, and decreasing on
\(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:least-noise-moment}
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W(\widehat{\mathbf N})]
  \le\Tr[\Gamma(\tau)]
       \Tr[\tau^{(\gamma)}W(\widehat{\mathbf N})].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Averaging \(\Gamma\) over independent phase rotations preserves its
trace bound and displacement covariance, and it does not change either side
of \eqref{eq:least-noise-moment}.  We may therefore assume that \(\Gamma\)
is phase covariant in every mode.

Consider one mode.  Write \(\Tr\Gamma(X)=\Tr(AX)\) with \(0\le A\le I\).
Displacement covariance implies \(D(z)^*AD(z)=A\); hence irreducibility of
the Weyl representation gives \(A=cI\), where
\(c=\Tr\Gamma(\tau_q)\).  If \(c>0\), then
\(\Lambda=c^{-1}\Gamma\) is a phase-covariant amplifier of amplitude gain
\(\sqrt\gamma\).  The Heisenberg-picture idler representation writes such a
channel as
\[
  a_{\mathrm{out}}
  =\sqrt\gamma\,a_{\mathrm{in}}+\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,b^*,
\]
where \(b\) is an idler mode; phase covariance allows the idler state to
be chosen number diagonal.  The stochastic-order result of
\cite[Sec.~2.C, Lem.~2.3, and Sec.~3]
{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning} therefore implies that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imported-stochastic-order}
  \sum_{n=0}^m(1-q^{(\gamma)})(q^{(\gamma)})^n
  \ge
  \sum_{n=0}^m\langle n|\Lambda(\tau_q)|n\rangle
  \qquad(m\ge0).
\end{equation}
The distribution on the left is the vacuum-idler output, namely that of
\(\tau_{q^{(\gamma)}}\), and both output states are number diagonal.  For a
bounded decreasing \(w\), summation by parts writes its expectation as a
nonnegative linear combination of these cumulative probabilities; hence
\[
  \Tr[\Lambda(\tau_q)w(\widehat N)]
  \le\Tr[\tau_{q^{(\gamma)}}w(\widehat N)].
\]
Multiplication by \(c\) proves the one-mode case; the case \(c=0\) is
immediate.

For the multimode case, the claim is immediate if some \(w_\ell\)
vanishes identically; otherwise, divide each \(w_\ell\) by
\(w_\ell(0)\) and assume \(0\le w_\ell\le1\).  Put
\[
  W_{\ge\ell}
  =I_1\ot\cdots\ot I_{\ell-1}\ot
   \bigotimes_{j=\ell}^s w_j(\widehat N_j),
  \qquad W_{\ge s+1}=I,
\]
and, for an operator \(X\) on mode \(\ell\), define
\[
  \Gamma_\ell(X)
  =\Tr_{\ne\ell}\!\left[
    W_{\ge\ell+1}^{1/2}
    \Gamma(\tau_{q_1}\ot\cdots\ot\tau_{q_{\ell-1}}\ot X\ot
           \tau_{q_{\ell+1}}\ot\cdots\ot\tau_{q_s})
    W_{\ge\ell+1}^{1/2}
  \right].
\]
Because \(0\le W_{\ge\ell+1}\le I\) and this operator is the identity on
mode \(\ell\), each \(\Gamma_\ell\) is completely positive, trace
nonincreasing, and displacement covariant in that mode.  Moreover,
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma_\ell(\tau_{q_\ell})w_\ell(\widehat N_\ell)]
  =\Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W_{\ge\ell}],
  \qquad
  \Tr\Gamma_\ell(\tau_{q_\ell})
  =\Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W_{\ge\ell+1}].
\]
Applying the one-mode inequality to \(\Gamma_\ell\) and iterating over
\(\ell=1,\ldots,s\) gives \eqref{eq:least-noise-moment}.
\end{proof}

Specialize to \(s=r_d\), \(q_\ell=q_{ij}\),
\(\tau=\tau_p\), and \(\tau^{(\gamma)}=\tau_p^{(\gamma)}\).  Let
\(t_{\mathbf k}\) and \(t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}\) be the corresponding
number-basis eigenvalues, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantum-witness}
  W_\gamma
  =\sum_{\mathbf k}
  \sqrt{\frac{t_{\mathbf k}}{t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}}
  \proj{\mathbf k}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
  W_\gamma=\prod_{i<j}w_{ij}(\widehat N_{ij}),
  \qquad
  w_{ij}(n)=
  \sqrt{\frac{1-q_{ij}}{1-q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}}
  \left(\frac{q_{ij}}{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}\right)^{n/2}.
\]
Since \(q_{ij}<q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}\), these factors are positive, bounded,
and decreasing, and the eigenvalues of \(W_\gamma\) tend to zero with the
total occupation number.  Thus \(W_\gamma\) is bounded,
injective, and compact.  Its definition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantum-witness-identities}
  \Tr(\tau_p^{(\gamma)}W_\gamma)
  =\Tr(\tau_p W_\gamma^{-1})
  =\sum_{\mathbf k}\sqrt{t_{\mathbf k}t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}
  =\Borb(\gamma,p),
\end{equation}
where the inverse is understood as in \cref{lem:weighted-fidelity}.

\subsection{Classical trace kernels}

\begin{lemma}[Translation-covariant scalar kernels]
\label{lem:scalar-convolution}
Let \(\mathsf E\) be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and let
\(\mathcal R:L^1(\mathsf E)\to C_0(\mathsf E)^*\) be a positive
contraction.  For a finite Radon measure \(\lambda\), define
\(T_a\lambda(E)=\lambda(E-a)\).  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-measure-covariance}
  \mathcal R(u(\,\cdot-h))=T_{\sqrt\gamma h}\mathcal R(u)
  \qquad(h\in\mathsf E)
\end{equation}
as an equality of finite Radon measures.  Then there is a finite positive
measure \(\mu\), with \(\mu(\mathsf E)\le1\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-convolution}
  \mathcal R(u)(E)
  =\int_{\mathsf E}u(x)\,\mu(E-\sqrt\gamma x)\,\dd x
\end{equation}
for every \(u\in L^1(\mathsf E)\) and every Borel set \(E\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The one-point case is immediate.  Otherwise choose a nonnegative
approximate identity \(u_j\in C_c(\mathsf E)\) with \(\int u_j=1\).
Since \(C_0(\mathsf E)\) is separable, after passing to a subsequence the
positive measures \(\mathcal R(u_j)\) converge weak-* to a positive measure
\(\mu\); the contraction bound gives \(\mu(\mathsf E)\le1\).

Covariance gives, as an equality of finite measures,
\[
  \mathcal R(u*u_j)
  =\int_{\mathsf E}u(x)T_{\sqrt\gamma x}\mathcal R(u_j)\,\dd x.
\]
For \(u\in L^1(\mathsf E)\) and \(\phi\in C_0(\mathsf E)\), use
\(u*u_j\to u\) in \(L^1\), pair this identity with \(\phi\), and pass to
the limit to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \langle\phi,\mathcal R(u)\rangle
  &=\int_{\mathsf E}u(x)
      \int_{\mathsf E}\phi(y+\sqrt\gamma x)\,\mu(\dd y)\,\dd x.
\end{aligned}
\]
Dominated convergence applies because the integrand is bounded by
\(|u(x)|\|\phi\|_\infty\).  This is precisely the pairing of \(\phi\)
with the measure in \eqref{eq:scalar-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One flat-prior bound for both Gaussian models}

\begin{proposition}[Flat-prior upper bound]\label{prop:flat-prior-upper}
Let \(k\in\{0,d-1\}\).  For \(k=0\), let \(\mathsf E=\{0\}\) and omit the
classical factor.  For \(k=d-1\), let \(\mathsf E=\mathsf H\), equipped with
its induced Euclidean measure, and use the covariance \(\Sigma_p\).  Let
\[
  \mathsf E_L=\{h\in\mathsf E:\|h\|_\infty\le L\},
  \qquad
  \Xi_L=\mathsf E_L\times\mathsf Z_L,
\]
with \(|\mathsf E_L|=1\) when \(k=0\).  Define
\[
  R_{0,z}^{(0)}=\Phi_z^{(p)},
  \qquad S_{0,z}^{(0)}=\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)},
\]
\[
  R_{h,z}^{(d-1)}=\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}},
  \qquad S_{h,z}^{(d-1)}=\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}},
  \qquad
  c_k=\left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{k/2}.
\]
For \(k=0\), the supremum below is over quantum channels on
\(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}\); for \(k=d-1\), it is over classical--quantum
channels.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-flat-prior-upper}
  \limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda
  \frac1{|\Xi_L|}\int_{\Xi_L}
  B\!\left(\Lambda(R_{h,z}^{(k)}),S_{h,z}^{(k)}\right)
  \,\dd h\,\dd z
  \le c_k\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
For \(k=0\), integration in \(h\) means evaluation at the unique point of
\(\mathsf E\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(k=0\), set \(f_1=f_\gamma=\chi=1\) on the one-point space.  For
\(k=d-1\), let \(f_1\) and \(f_\gamma\) be the centered Gaussian densities
with covariances \(\Sigma_p\) and \(\gamma\Sigma_p\), and set
\[
  \chi(y)=\sqrt{\frac{f_1(y)}{f_\gamma(y)}}
  =\gamma^{k/4}
   \exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}
     \langle y,\Sigma_p^{-1}y\rangle\right].
\]
This function is bounded and strictly positive; for \(k=d-1\), it also
belongs to \(C_0(\mathsf E)\).  In both cases,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-witness-identity}
  \int_{\mathsf E}\frac{f_1(y)}{\chi(y)}\,\dd y
  =\int_{\mathsf E}\sqrt{f_1(y)f_\gamma(y)}\,\dd y
  =c_k.
\end{equation}

For \(\xi=(h,z)\), define
\[
  (\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}R)(x)
  =D(z)R(x-h)D(z)^*,
  \qquad
  (\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}R)(y)
  =D(\sqrt\gamma z)R(y-\sqrt\gamma h)D(\sqrt\gamma z)^*.
\]
Then
\(R_\xi^{(k)}=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}(f_1\tau_p)\) and
\(S_\xi^{(k)}=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}(f_1\tau_p)\).  For a channel
\(\Lambda\), let
\[
  \overline\Lambda_L
  =\frac1{|\Xi_L|}\int_{\Xi_L}
   \mathcal U_{-\eta}^{\mathrm{out}}\circ\Lambda\circ
   \mathcal U_\eta^{\mathrm{in}}\,\dd\eta.
\]
Translation invariance and concavity of root fidelity give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-folner-concavity}
  F_L(\Lambda)
  =\frac1{|\Xi_L|}\int_{\Xi_L}
  B\!\left(\Lambda(R_\xi^{(k)}),S_\xi^{(k)}\right)\,\dd\xi
  \le B(\overline\Lambda_L(f_1\tau_p),f_1\tau_p).
\end{equation}

Let \(F_*=\limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda F_L(\Lambda)\).  Choose
\(L_j\to\infty\) and nearly optimal channels
\(\widetilde\Lambda_j\), and set
\[
  \Lambda_j=\overline{\widetilde\Lambda_j}_{L_j},
  \qquad R_j=\Lambda_j(f_1\tau_p).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-maximizing-sequence}
  F_{L_j}(\widetilde\Lambda_j)\longrightarrow F_*,
  \qquad
  F_{L_j}(\widetilde\Lambda_j)\le B(R_j,f_1\tau_p).
\end{equation}
For \(\eta\in\mathsf E\times\mathbb C^{r_d}\), write
\[
  A_{j,\eta}
  =\mathcal U_{-\eta}^{\mathrm{out}}\circ
   \widetilde\Lambda_j\circ\mathcal U_\eta^{\mathrm{in}},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_j=\frac1{|\Xi_{L_j}|}\int_{\Xi_{L_j}}A_{j,\eta}\,\dd\eta.
\]
The group law and the change of variables \(u=\eta+\xi\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Lambda_j(\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}R)
  &=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}
    \frac1{|\Xi_{L_j}|}\int_{\Xi_{L_j}+\xi}A_{j,u}(R)\,\dd u,\\
  \mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}\Lambda_j(R)
  &=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}
    \frac1{|\Xi_{L_j}|}\int_{\Xi_{L_j}}A_{j,u}(R)\,\dd u.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two integrals cancel on the intersection of the two averaging sets,
and \(\|A_{j,u}\|_{1\to1}\le1\).  Therefore, for fixed \(\xi\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-approx-covariance}
  \|\Lambda_j(\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}R)
    -\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}\Lambda_j(R)\|_1
  \le
  \frac{|(\Xi_{L_j}+\xi)\mathbin\triangle\Xi_{L_j}|}{|\Xi_{L_j}|}
  \|R\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\Xi_1\) is a bounded polytope and \(\Xi_L=L\Xi_1\), so the
ratio is \(O_\xi(L^{-1})\).

Define
\[
  \Gamma_j^\chi(X)
  =\int_{\mathsf E}\chi(y)[\Lambda_j(f_1X)](y)\,\dd y.
\]
These maps are completely positive and satisfy
\(\|\Gamma_j^\chi\|_{1\to1}\le\|\chi\|_\infty\).  Taking
\(\xi=(0,z)\) in \eqref{eq:unified-approx-covariance}, multiplying by
\(\chi\), and integrating gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\|\Gamma_j^\chi(D(z)XD(z)^*)
   -D(\sqrt\gamma z)\Gamma_j^\chi(X)D(\sqrt\gamma z)^*\|_1\\
 &\qquad\le\|\chi\|_\infty
   \frac{|(\Xi_{L_j}+(0,z))\mathbin\triangle\Xi_{L_j}|}{|\Xi_{L_j}|}
   \|X\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence \cref{lem:compact-cp-limit} gives, after passing to a subsequence, a
completely positive displacement-covariant limit \(\Gamma^\chi\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-map-limit}
  \Tr[\Gamma_j^\chi(X)K]
  \longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(X)K]
\end{equation}
for trace-class \(X\) and compact \(K\).  With
\[
  M_j
  =\int_{\mathsf E}\chi(y)\Tr[R_j(y)W_\gamma] \,\dd y
  =\Tr[\Gamma_j^\chi(\tau_p)W_\gamma],
\]
compactness of \(W_\gamma\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-limit}
  M_j\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)W_\gamma].
\end{equation}
Moreover, \cref{lem:weighted-fidelity},
\eqref{eq:classical-witness-identity}, and
\eqref{eq:quantum-witness-identities} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-fidelity-to-moment}
  B(R_j,f_1\tau_p)^2
  \le M_j\,c_k\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}

If \(k=0\), then \(\Gamma_j^\chi=\Lambda_j\) are channels, so the limit is
trace nonincreasing.  By \cref{prop:least-noise-moment},
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_j M_j
  &=\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)W_\gamma]\\
  &\le\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)\,
       \Tr(\tau_p^{(\gamma)}W_\gamma)
  \le\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:unified-maximizing-sequence} and
\eqref{eq:unified-fidelity-to-moment} now yield
\(F_*\le\Borb(\gamma,p)\).

Assume henceforth that \(k=d-1\).  For \(u\in L^1(\mathsf E)\), define
\[
  \mathcal R_j(u)(E)
  =\int_E\Tr\bigl([\Lambda_j(u\tau_p)](y)\bigr)\,\dd y.
\]
The maps \(\mathcal R_j:L^1(\mathsf E)\to C_0(\mathsf E)^*\) are positive
contractions.  Using countable dense subsets of \(L^1(\mathsf E)\) and
\(C_0(\mathsf E)\), pass to a subsequence converging weak-* on every \(u\)
to a positive contraction \(\mathcal R\).  Taking \(z=0\) in
\eqref{eq:unified-approx-covariance}, pairing with a function in
\(C_0(\mathsf E)\), and passing to the limit gives
\[
  \mathcal R(u(\,\cdot-h))
  =T_{\sqrt\gamma h}\mathcal R(u).
\]
Let \(\mu\) be the measure from \cref{lem:scalar-convolution}; then
\(\mu(\mathsf E)\le1\).

Finite-rank projections, \eqref{eq:weighted-map-limit}, the weak-*
convergence of \(\mathcal R_j(f_1)\), and
\(\chi\in C_0(\mathsf E)\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)
  &\le\liminf_j\Tr\Gamma_j^\chi(\tau_p)\\
  &=\lim_j\int_{\mathsf E}\chi(y)\,\mathcal R_j(f_1)(\dd y)
   =\int_{\mathsf E}\chi(y)\,\mathcal R(f_1)(\dd y).
\end{aligned}
\]
By \eqref{eq:scalar-convolution}, the last integral equals
\[
  \int_{\mathsf E}\mu(\dd r)
  \int_{\mathsf E}f_1(x)\chi(r+\sqrt\gamma x)\,\dd x.
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
  \int_{\mathsf E}f_1(x)\chi(r+\sqrt\gamma x)\,\dd x
  =c_k\exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma(\gamma+1)}
    \langle r,\Sigma_p^{-1}r\rangle\right].
\]
Since \(\mu(\mathsf E)\le1\), it follows that
\(\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)\le c_k\).

Finally write \(\Tr\Gamma^\chi(X)=\Tr(AX)\).  Covariance implies that \(A\)
commutes with every Weyl displacement, so \(A=cI\), with
\[
  c=\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)\le c_k\le1.
\]
Thus \(\Gamma^\chi\) is trace nonincreasing, and
\cref{prop:least-noise-moment} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \lim_j M_j
  &=\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)W_\gamma]\\
  &\le\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau_p)\,
       \Tr(\tau_p^{(\gamma)}W_\gamma)
  \le c_k\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:unified-maximizing-sequence} and
\eqref{eq:unified-fidelity-to-moment} yields
\[
  F_*\le c_k\Borb(\gamma,p),
\]
which proves the full classical--quantum case.
\end{proof}
