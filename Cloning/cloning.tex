\title{Asymptotically Optimal Global Cloning of Full-Rank Mixed States with Simple Spectrum}
\author{}
\date{}


\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Let \(d\ge2\), and let \(m_n\) be positive integers with
\(m_n/n\to\gamma>1\).  For global \(n\to m_n\) cloning, measured by root
fidelity with the ideal product target, we determine the asymptotic minimax
value on every unitary orbit of a full-rank state with simple spectrum
\(p_1>\cdots>p_d>0\).  The value is
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  =
  \prod_{i<j}
  \frac{\sqrt{(1-q_{ij})(1-q_{ij}^{(\gamma)})}}
       {1-\sqrt{q_{ij}q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}},
  \qquad
  q_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i},
  \quad
  q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}=1-\frac{1-q_{ij}}{\gamma}.
\]
A covariant Schur--Weyl protocol attains this value: it measures the input
Young label, samples the output label from the exact target law, and applies
the multiplicity-one Cartan-sector channel on the asymptotically certain
admissible pairs.  In Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt
(PBW) coordinates, the Cartan
inclusion has asymptotic beam-splitter coefficients, and its centered
thermal-sector output converges to that of the quantum-limited Gaussian
amplifier.  Local asymptotic normality and the corresponding Gaussian
amplification bound give the converse.  When the spectrum is unknown, a
spectrum-independent dithered dilation of the Young label yields one channel
sequence whose asymptotic root fidelity, uniformly on compact subsets of the
strict spectral chamber, is at least
\[
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)
  =
  \left(\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2}
  \Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
This value is sharp in the expanding-window local asymptotic minimax sense.
For every \(0<\kappa<2/[d(d+1)]\), its infimum over the compact chamber
\(\Delta_{d,\kappa}\) in \cref{cor:compact-global-unknown} is also the sharp global worst-case
value.  The additional factor is the unavoidable loss from amplifying the
classical spectral component of the local Gaussian model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The no-cloning theorem excludes perfect copying of an arbitrary unknown
quantum state \cite{WoottersZurek1982,Dieks1982}.  Approximate cloning asks
for a channel that maps $n$ copies of a state to an output close to $m$ ideal
copies.  We use the global product target $\rho^{\ot m}$, rather than a
criterion involving only one-site marginals.  For pure states, Werner's
covariant channel solves the universal finite-dimensional problem
\cite{Werner1998}.  We fix \(d\ge2\) and positive integers \(m_n\), and write
\[
  \gamma_n:=\frac{m_n}{n}\longrightarrow\gamma>1,
  \qquad
  r_d:=\binom d2.
\]
Thus \(r_d\) is the number of pairs \(i<j\), equivalently the number of
off-diagonal oscillator modes in the LAN model.  We study
full-rank mixed states with simple spectrum.  Since \(m_n>n\)
eventually, the nontrivial constructions are defined for all
sufficiently large \(n\).  For the finitely many remaining values,
we use an arbitrary invariant preparation channel; this does not affect any
asymptotic assertion.

Throughout, $B$ denotes the unsquared Uhlmann fidelity.  This root
convention is convenient because $B$ is jointly concave and has an exact
linear direct-sum formula over Schur blocks; precise conventions for states,
subnormalized operators, and classical measures are fixed in
\cref{sec:preliminaries}.
Let
\[
  \Delta_d^\circ
  =\left\{p\in\R^d:
  p_1>\cdots>p_d>0,\ \sum_i p_i=1\right\},
  \qquad
  \rho_p=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_d),
  \qquad
  \rho_{p,g}=g\rho_p g^*.
\]
For $p\in\Delta_d^\circ$ and $i<j$, put
\[
  q_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i},
  \qquad
  q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}=1-\frac{1-q_{ij}}{\gamma}.
\]
When the spectrum is known and only $g\in\U(d)$ is unknown, the asymptotic
minimax root fidelity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-known-answer}
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  =
  \prod_{i<j}
  \frac{\sqrt{(1-q_{ij})(1-q_{ij}^{(\gamma)})}}
       {1-\sqrt{q_{ij}q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}}
  =
  \prod_{i<j}
  \frac{\sqrt{\gamma}+\sqrt{q_{ij}(\gamma-1+q_{ij})}}
       {\gamma+q_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
The achievability protocol measures the input Schur--Weyl label and samples
the output label directly from its ideal target distribution.  For typical
input and output labels $\mu\vdash n$ and $\nu\vdash m_n$, the difference
$\nu-\mu$ is dominant.  The multiplicity-one Cartan inclusion
$\cH_\nu\hookrightarrow\cH_\mu\ot\cH_{\nu-\mu}$ then defines the
Cartan-sector channel used in the protocol.  For highest weights of order
$n$, its PBW coproduct coefficients converge to those of a beam splitter,
and its centered thermal-sector output has the least-noise amplifier
parameter $q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}$.  This gives
\eqref{eq:intro-known-answer}.

When the spectrum is unknown, the Young label also carries the classical
spectral fluctuation in the local asymptotic normality (LAN) model.  A
deterministic lattice dilation generally has insufficient overlap with the
target Young law.  We instead use a bounded cell-overlap dither, independent
of $p$, whose Gaussian limit is the optimal classical dilation.  The resulting
local asymptotic minimax value is
\[
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p),
  \qquad
  \Bcl(\gamma,d)
  =\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2}.
\]
The same spectrum-independent channel guarantees at least this value,
uniformly on every compact subset of $\Delta_d^\circ$.  We prove that the
value is sharp
in the expanding-window local minimax sense and deduce the global worst-case
value on each compact chamber $\Delta_{d,\kappa}$ defined in
\cref{cor:compact-global-unknown}.  These chambers are bounded away from
spectral collisions and the simplex boundary.  No claim is made uniformly
across those singular strata, where the LAN model changes.

Broadcasting is a different task: it constrains selected output marginals but
allows correlations among the outputs.  Consequently, exact broadcasting or
superbroadcasting results do not solve the product-target problem considered
here \cite{BarnumCavesFuchsJozsaSchumacher1996,
DArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2005,BuscemiDArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2006}.
The Gaussian limit relevant to our converse was analyzed by Gu\c{t}\u{a} and
Matsumoto, who identified the least-noise amplifier for displaced thermal
states \cite{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning}.  Finite-dimensional LAN
then transfers the resulting upper bound on Gaussian amplification fidelity
to the original cloning problem
\cite{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}.

\section{Fidelity, Schur--Weyl duality, and the Cartan channel}\label{sec:preliminaries}

For positive trace-class operators $A,C$, not necessarily normalized, set
\[
  B(A,C)=\|\sqrt A\sqrt C\|_1.
\]
For states this is the unsquared Uhlmann fidelity.  When one argument is
subnormalized, we use this homogeneous expression without adding a separate
failure term; this is the convention needed for trace-nonincreasing limits in
the Gaussian converses.  A quantum channel is a completely positive
trace-preserving map between trace-class operator spaces.

For finite positive classical measures $P,Q$, the same symbol denotes their
Hellinger affinity,
\[
  B(P,Q)=\int
  \sqrt{\frac{\dd P}{\dd\lambda}\frac{\dd Q}{\dd\lambda}}\,\dd\lambda,
\]
where $\lambda$ is any common dominating measure.  The value is independent
of this choice.  On a countable space,
$B(P,Q)=\sum_x\sqrt{P(x)Q(x)}$.  More generally, for positive
trace-class-valued densities $R,S$ on the same classical space, set
\[
  B(R,S)=\int B(R(x),S(x))\,\dd x.
\]
This is the continuous direct-sum formula and is unchanged by replacing the
reference measure and the two densities simultaneously.  Given measure spaces
$X,Y$ and Hilbert spaces $\cH_{\mathrm{in}},\cH_{\mathrm{out}}$, a
classical--quantum channel is a completely positive, trace-preserving linear
map $\Lambda:L^1(X;\cT_1(\cH_{\mathrm{in}}))\to
L^1(Y;\cT_1(\cH_{\mathrm{out}}))$.
We use $\|\cdot\|_1$ for trace norm, $\ell^1$-distance, or $L^1$-distance
according to context.

\begin{lemma}[Basic properties of root fidelity]\label{lem:fid-cont}
The following statements hold both for quantum states and for
classical--quantum states, with
$B(R,S)=\int B(R(x),S(x))\,\dd x$ in the latter case.  Let
$A,C$ be positive elements of the corresponding state space and let
$\Lambda$ be a channel.  Then
\[
  B(\Lambda(A),\Lambda(C))\ge B(A,C),
\]
and $B$ is jointly concave on the positive cone of that state space.  If
$\rho,\sigma,\rho',\sigma'$ are states, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-continuity}
  |B(\rho,\sigma)-B(\rho',\sigma')|
  \le
  \sqrt2\left(
  \|\rho-\rho'\|_1^{1/2}+\|\sigma-\sigma'\|_1^{1/2}
  \right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity and joint concavity of root fidelity are standard for positive
trace-class operators and positive trace-class-valued $L^1$ densities.  For
\eqref{eq:B-continuity}, let
$d_B(\rho,\sigma)=\sqrt{2(1-B(\rho,\sigma))}$ be the Bures distance.  The
Fuchs--van de Graaf inequalities give
$d_B(\rho,\rho')\le\|\rho-\rho'\|_1^{1/2}$ and likewise for
$\sigma,\sigma'$.  The triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
  |d_B(\rho,\sigma)-d_B(\rho',\sigma')|
  \le
  \|\rho-\rho'\|_1^{1/2}+\|\sigma-\sigma'\|_1^{1/2}.
\]
Since $0\le d_B\le\sqrt2$ and $B=1-d_B^2/2$, the stated estimate follows.
\end{proof}

If two states are block diagonal over the same classical label,
\[
  \rho=\bigoplus_xP_x\rho_x,
  \qquad
  \sigma=\bigoplus_xQ_x\sigma_x,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-fidelity}
  B(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_x\sqrt{P_xQ_x}\,B(\rho_x,\sigma_x).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Block-fidelity lower bound]\label{lem:block-good-set}
Let $\Pi(x,y)$ be a joint probability distribution with $y$-marginal $Q$,
let $P$ be a probability distribution on the same $y$-space, and let
$\sigma_{x,y}$ and $\tau_y$ be states.  If $G$ is a set of pairs and
\[
  B(\sigma_{x,y},\tau_y)\ge b\qquad((x,y)\in G)
\]
for some $b\in[0,1]$, then
\[
  B\!\left(
    \bigoplus_y\sum_x \Pi(x,y)\sigma_{x,y},
    \bigoplus_y P(y)\tau_y
  \right)
  \ge b\left(B(Q,P)-\sqrt{\Pi(G^c)}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $Q(y)>0$, put
\[
  r(y)=\sum_{x:(x,y)\notin G}\frac{\Pi(x,y)}{Q(y)}
\]
and put $r(y)=0$ otherwise.  Joint concavity,
\eqref{eq:block-fidelity}, and the hypothesis on $G$ give
\[
  B\!\left(
    \bigoplus_y\sum_x\Pi(x,y)\sigma_{x,y},
    \bigoplus_yP(y)\tau_y
  \right)
  \ge b\left(B(Q,P)-\sum_y\sqrt{Q(y)P(y)}\,r(y)\right).
\]
Since $0\le r\le1$ and $\sum_yQ(y)r(y)=\Pi(G^c)$, Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
  \sum_y\sqrt{Q(y)P(y)}\,r(y)
  \le
  \left(\sum_yQ(y)r(y)\right)^{1/2}
  \left(\sum_yP(y)r(y)\right)^{1/2}
  \le\sqrt{\Pi(G^c)}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Schur--Weyl decomposition and Weyl-sector states}
Schur--Weyl duality gives
\[
  (\C^d)^{\ot n}
  \cong
  \bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n,\,\ell(\lambda)\le d}
  \cH_\lambda\ot\cS_\lambda,
\]
where \(\cH_\lambda\) is the irreducible \(\U(d)\)-module of highest
weight \(\lambda\), and \(\cS_\lambda\) is the corresponding Specht module.
For a diagonal density matrix \(\rho_p=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_d)\),
\[
  \rho_p^{\ot n}
  =
  \bigoplus_{\substack{\lambda\vdash n\\\ell(\lambda)\le d}}
  P_{n,p}(\lambda)
  \rho_{p,\lambda}\ot\frac{I_{\cS_\lambda}}{\dim\cS_\lambda},
\]
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Young-prob-and-Weyl-state}
  P_{n,p}(\lambda)=\dim\cS_\lambda\,s_\lambda(p),
  \qquad
  \rho_{p,\lambda}=\frac{\pi_\lambda(\rho_p)}{s_\lambda(p)}.
\end{equation}
Here \(s_\lambda\) is the Schur polynomial and \(\pi_\lambda\) is the
polynomial representation.  For an orbit point
\(\rho_{p,g}=g\rho_p g^*\), the same decomposition holds with
\[
  \rho_{p,g,\lambda}=U_\lambda(g)\rho_{p,\lambda}U_\lambda(g)^*.
\]
The Young distribution \(P_{n,p}\) depends on the spectrum \(p\), but not
on the eigenbasis \(g\).

For \(n\ge1\), define the spectral typical set
\[
  \mathsf T_n(p)
  =\left\{\lambda\vdash n:\ell(\lambda)\le d,
  \ \left\|\frac{\lambda}{n}-p\right\|_\infty\le n^{-1/3}\right\}.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Uniform Young concentration]\label{lem:young-concentration}
For every compact $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$, there is $c_K>0$ such that
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)
  \le e^{-c_K n^{1/3}}
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing $q=\lambda/n$, the finite-$n$ Keyl--Werner estimate gives the
concrete bound \cite{KeylWerner2001Spectrum}
\[
  P_{n,p}(\lambda)
  \le (n+1)^{d(d-1)/2}\exp[-nD(q\|p)].
\]
Moreover, there are at most $(n+1)^{d-1}$ partitions of $n$ with at most
$d$ rows.  If $\lambda\notin\mathsf T_n(p)$, then
$\|q-p\|_1>n^{-1/3}$, and Pinsker's inequality gives
$D(q\|p)\ge \tfrac12n^{-2/3}$.  Thus
\[
  P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)
  \le (n+1)^{d(d-1)/2+d-1}e^{-n^{1/3}/2}.
\]
Since $n(n^{-1/3})^2=n^{1/3}$ and polynomial factors are
$e^{o(n^{1/3})}$, this proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Cartan component and its sector channel}

Let \(\lambda\) and \(\theta\) be dominant weights of \(\U(d)\), with
unit highest-weight vectors \(v_\lambda\) and \(v_\theta\).  The vector
\(v_\lambda\ot v_\theta\) is a highest-weight vector of weight
\(\lambda+\theta\).  The corresponding weight space in
\(\cH_\lambda\ot\cH_\theta\) is one-dimensional, so the irreducible Cartan
component \(\cH_{\lambda+\theta}\) occurs with multiplicity one.  We denote
its uniquely normalized intertwining isometry by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-inclusion}
  V_{\lambda,\theta}:\cH_{\lambda+\theta}\hookrightarrow
  \cH_\lambda\ot\cH_\theta,
  \qquad
  V_{\lambda,\theta}v_{\lambda+\theta}=v_\lambda\ot v_\theta.
\end{equation}
If \(\mu\vdash n\), \(\nu\vdash m\), and
\(\omega:=\nu-\mu\) is dominant, then \(\nu=\mu+\omega\), and
\eqref{eq:cartan-inclusion} gives
\[
  V_{\mu,\omega}:\cH_\nu\hookrightarrow\cH_\mu\ot\cH_\omega.
\]
The associated sector channel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-channel-formula}
  \cC_{\mu,\nu}(X)
  =
  \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
  V_{\mu,\omega}^*(X\ot I_{\cH_\omega})V_{\mu,\omega},
  \qquad X\in\cT_1(\cH_\mu).
\end{equation}
It is completely positive and trace preserving, since Schur's lemma and a
trace comparison give
\(\Tr_{\cH_\omega}(V_{\mu,\omega}V_{\mu,\omega}^*)
=(\dim\cH_\nu/\dim\cH_\mu)I_{\cH_\mu}\).  The intertwining identity also gives
\[
  \cC_{\mu,\nu}(U_\mu(g)XU_\mu(g)^*)
  =U_\nu(g)\cC_{\mu,\nu}(X)U_\nu(g)^*,
\]
so the sector channel is \(\U(d)\)-covariant.



\section{Common asymptotic ingredients}\label{sec:LAN-Gaussian}

Two asymptotic ingredients are used in both cloning problems.  The
Cartan-sector channel has a thermal bosonic limit, which gives achievability.
Local asymptotic normality (LAN) reduces the converses to amplification of the
corresponding Gaussian models.

\subsection{Bosonic Gaussian model}

Let
\(\cF=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\ket{k}:k\ge0\}\) be the one-mode
Fock space, with annihilation operator \(a\), creation operator \(a^*\), and
number operator \(\widehat N=a^*a\).  Thus
\[
  a\ket{k}=\sqrt{k}\ket{k-1},
  \qquad
  a^*\ket{k}=\sqrt{k+1}\ket{k+1},
  \qquad
  [a,a^*]=I.
\]
For \(z\in\C\), write
\[
  D(z)=\exp(za^*-\bar z a),
  \qquad
  D(z)^*aD(z)=a+z.
\]
For \(0<q<1\), the thermal state is
\[
  \tau_q=(1-q)\sum_{k\ge0}q^k\proj{k}.
\]
Its mean occupation number is \(q/(1-q)\).  Since two thermal states
commute, their root fidelity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermal-root-fid}
  B(\tau_q,\tau_{q'})
  =\frac{\sqrt{(1-q)(1-q')}}{1-\sqrt{qq'}}.
\end{equation}
For \(s\ge1\) and \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_s)\in\C^s\), use the product
notation
\[
  D(z)=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^s D(z_\ell).
\]
The orbital Gaussian model has
\[
  \cF_{\mathrm{orb}}:=\cF^{\ot r_d}
\]
as its multimode Fock space.

The quantum-limited phase-insensitive amplifier of intensity gain
\(G\ge1\) is obtained by coupling the input mode to a vacuum environment
mode through a two-mode squeezing unitary satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplifier-heisenberg}
  U_G^*(a\ot I)U_G
  =\sqrt{G}\,a\ot I+\sqrt{G-1}\,I\ot b^*.
\end{equation}
Tracing out the environment defines a channel \(\cA_G\).  It is
phase covariant and displacement covariant with amplitude gain \(\sqrt{G}\):
\[
  \cA_G\bigl(D(z)\rho D(z)^*\bigr)
  =D(\sqrt{G}\,z)\cA_G(\rho)D(\sqrt{G}\,z)^*.
\]
The vacuum environment is the least-noise choice compatible with
\eqref{eq:amplifier-heisenberg}.  For $0<q<1$, write
$q^{(G)}:=1-(1-q)/G$.  On a displaced thermal input,
\[
  \cA_G\bigl(D(z)\tau_q D(z)^*\bigr)
  =D(\sqrt{G}\,z)\tau_{q^{(G)}}D(\sqrt{G}\,z)^*.
\]

Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\), put
\[
  q_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i}\quad(i<j),
\]
and index \(\C^{r_d}\) by the pairs \(i<j\).  The Gaussian input and ideal
target families are
\begin{align}
  \Phi_z^{(p)}
  &=\bigotimes_{i<j}D(z_{ij})\tau_{q_{ij}}D(z_{ij})^*,
  \label{eq:Phi-family-def}\\
  \Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}
  &=\bigotimes_{i<j}
    D(\sqrt{\gamma}\,z_{ij})\tau_{q_{ij}}
    D(\sqrt{\gamma}\,z_{ij})^*.
  \label{eq:Psi-family-def}
\end{align}
When \(p\) and \(\gamma\) are fixed, we abbreviate these as \(\Phi_z\) and
\(\Psi_z\).  The product amplifier \(\cA_\gamma^{\ot r_d}\) has the desired
displacement gain, but replaces each $\tau_{q_{ij}}$ by
$\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-mode-b}
  b_\gamma(q)
  :=B\bigl(\tau_{q^{(\gamma)}},\tau_q\bigr)
  =
  \frac{\sqrt{(1-q)(1-q^{(\gamma)})}}
       {1-\sqrt{q q^{(\gamma)}}}
  =
  \frac{\sqrt{\gamma}+\sqrt{q(\gamma-1+q)}}{\gamma+q}.
\end{equation}
Thus, for every $z$, the root fidelity between
$\cA_\gamma^{\ot r_d}(\Phi_z^{(p)})$ and $\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}$ is the
$z$-independent quantity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Borb-def}
  \Borb(\gamma,p)=\prod_{i<j}b_\gamma(q_{ij}).
\end{equation}

Gu\c{t}\u{a} and Matsumoto prove the decreasing photon-number moment
bound that characterizes the vacuum-idler amplifier as least noisy among
phase-insensitive displacement-covariant amplifiers of the same gain
\cite{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning}.  This moment bound yields the
Gaussian converse bounds for known- and unknown-spectrum cloning in
\cref{prop:orbital-flat-prior-upper,prop:hybrid-flat-prior-upper}, respectively.

\subsection{Thermal Cartan-sector limit}

The next proposition is the representation-theoretic input used by both
cloning protocols.  Its proof, including the cutoff-independent PBW basis,
Gram estimates, and trace-norm tail control, is given in
\cref{app:sector-limit}.

\begin{proposition}[Thermal Cartan-sector limit]
  \label{prop:thermal-sector}
  Let $\mu_n\vdash n$ and $\nu_n\vdash m_n$, where
  $m_n/n\to\gamma>1$, and suppose that
  \[
    \frac{\mu_n}{n}\longrightarrow p,
    \qquad
    \frac{\nu_n}{m_n}\longrightarrow p,
    \qquad
    p\in\Delta_d^\circ.
  \]
  Then $\nu_n-\mu_n\vdash m_n-n$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
  For the PBW isometries
  $J_{\nu_n}:\cH_{\nu_n}\to\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}$ constructed in
  \cref{lem:sector-thermal},
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thermal-sector-limit}
    \left\|
      J_{\nu_n}\cC_{\mu_n,\nu_n}(\rho_{p,\mu_n})J_{\nu_n}^*
      -\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}
    \right\|_1
    \longrightarrow0.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-fidelity-limit}
    B\!\left(
      \cC_{\mu_n,\nu_n}(\rho_{p,\mu_n}),
      \rho_{p,\nu_n}
    \right)
    \longrightarrow\Borb(\gamma,p).
  \end{equation}

  The convergence is uniform in the following form.  Fix
  $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$ and $\delta_n\to0$.  Uniformly over
  $p\in K$, integers $m$, and partitions $\mu\vdash n$, $\nu\vdash m$
  satisfying
  \[
    \frac\mu n,\frac\nu m\in K,
    \qquad
    \left|\frac mn-\gamma\right|\le\delta_n,
    \qquad
    \left\|\frac\mu n-p\right\|_\infty\le\delta_n,
    \qquad
    \left\|\frac\nu m-p\right\|_\infty\le\delta_n,
  \]
  one has $\nu-\mu\vdash m-n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, and the
  errors in \eqref{eq:thermal-sector-limit} and
  \eqref{eq:sector-fidelity-limit} tend to zero.
\end{proposition}

Thus the PBW isometry asymptotically identifies the target irrep state
$\rho_{p,\nu_n}$ with $\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}}$, and the sector-channel
output $\cC_{\mu_n,\nu_n}(\rho_{p,\mu_n})$ with
$\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}$.

\subsection{Local asymptotic normality}

Let
\[
  \mathsf H=\left\{h\in\R^d:\sum_{i=1}^d h_i=0\right\},
\]
and equip \(\mathsf H\) with its Euclidean structure and induced
$(d-1)$-dimensional Lebesgue measure; on
$\C^{r_d}\cong\mathbb R^{2r_d}$ we use standard Lebesgue measure.  If
\(\Sigma\) is positive definite on \(\mathsf H\), let
\(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma}\) denote the Gaussian law on \(\mathsf H\) with mean
\(h\) and covariance \(\Sigma\), and let \(\varphi_{h,\Sigma}\) denote its
density on \(\mathsf H\), so that
\(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma}(\dd x)=\varphi_{h,\Sigma}(x)\,\dd x\).  A
classical--quantum state is identified with its Bochner density
\[
  R\in L^1\!\left(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\right),
  \qquad
  \|R\|_1=\int_{\mathsf H}\|R(x)\|_1\,\dd x,
\]
where $x\in\mathsf H$ is the classical variable and
$R(x)\in\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})$ the quantum trace-class density.

Here LAN is the two-way asymptotic equivalence between the $n$-copy model in
an $n^{-1/2}$-neighborhood of $\rho_p$ and
$\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}$, with $h$ represented classically and
$z$ by displaced thermal bosonic modes.

For \(z=(z_{jk})_{j<k}\in\C^{r_d}\), define the anti-Hermitian matrix
\[
  X_p(z)
  =\sum_{j<k}
  \frac{z_{jk}E_{kj}-\bar z_{jk}E_{jk}}
       {\sqrt{p_j-p_k}}.
\]
The normalization is chosen so that
\[
  [X_p(z),\rho_p]_{kj}=\sqrt{p_j-p_k}\,z_{jk}.
\]
Writing $E_{jk}^{(r)}$ for $E_{jk}$ on copy $r$, the rotation-to-oscillator
approximation of
\cite[Lemmas~6.3--6.4; see also Secs.~7.3 and~7.6]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}
gives the LAN correspondence
\[
  \frac{\sum_{r=1}^nE_{jk}^{(r)}}{\sqrt{n(p_j-p_k)}}\rightsquigarrow a_{jk},
  \qquad
  \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{r=1}^nX_p(z)^{(r)}
  \rightsquigarrow\sum_{j<k}(z_{jk}a_{jk}^*-\bar z_{jk}a_{jk}).
\]
Thus the normalization gives
$[a_{jk},a_{\ell m}^*]=\delta_{j\ell}\delta_{km}I$.  Exponentiating the second
correspondence identifies
$\exp(n^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^nX_p(z)^{(r)})$ with
$D(z)=\bigotimes_{j<k}D(z_{jk})$; hence the limiting $(j,k)$-mode state is
$D(z_{jk})\tau_{p_k/p_j}D(z_{jk})^*$.

For \(h\in\mathsf H\), set
\[
  g_{n,z}=\exp\!\left(\frac{X_p(z)}{\sqrt n}\right),
  \qquad
  \rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}
  =g_{n,z}\diag\!\left(p+\frac{h}{\sqrt n}\right)g_{n,z}^*.
\]
Since \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\), the vector \(p+h/\sqrt n\) belongs to
\(\Delta_d^\circ\), uniformly for \(h\) in any bounded subset of
\(\mathsf H\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).
Thus \(\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}\) is a full-rank state with simple spectrum.
Uniformly for bounded \((h,z)\), moreover,
\[
  \rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}
  =\rho_p+\frac1{\sqrt n}
    \bigl(\diag(h)+[X_p(z),\rho_p]\bigr)+O(n^{-1})
\]
in operator norm.
Set
\[
  \Sigma_p=\left.(\diag(p)-p p^{\mathsf T})\right|_{\mathsf H}>0.
\]

The next theorem is Kahn and Gu\c{t}\u{a}'s finite-dimensional LAN theorem,
restricted to compact local parameter sets and written for the local family
$\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}$ \cite[Theorem~4.3]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}.
The existence and estimates for the LAN channels are imported from that
theorem.  The proof below records the coordinate identification and derives
the \(m_n\)-copy scaling used later.  The qubit case was proved earlier in
\cite{GutaKahn2006LANQubits}.

\begin{theorem}[Full mixed-state LAN; Kahn--Gu\c{t}\u{a}]
\label{thm:LAN-full}
Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  There are channels
\[
  T_n^{\mathrm{full}}:
  \cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot n})
  \longrightarrow
  L^1\!\left(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\right),
\]
\[
  S_n^{\mathrm{full}}:
  L^1\!\left(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\right)
  \longrightarrow
  \cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot n})
\]
such that, for every compact \(K\subset\mathsf H\times\C^{r_d}\),
\begin{align}
  \sup_{(h,z)\in K}
  \left\|
    T_n^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left(
      (\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}
    \right)
    -\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}
  \right\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-full-forward}\\
  \sup_{(h,z)\in K}
  \left\|
    S_n^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left(
      \mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}
    \right)
    -(\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}
  \right\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-full-reverse}
\end{align}
If \(m_n/n\to\gamma\), then the forward channels at sample size \(m_n\)
also satisfy, uniformly on every compact
\(K\subset\mathsf H\times\C^{r_d}\),
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{(h,z)\in K}
  \left\|
    T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left(
      (\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}
    \right)
    -\mathsf N_{\sqrt{\gamma}\,h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}
  \right\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-full-target}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \eqref{eq:LAN-full-forward}--\eqref{eq:LAN-full-reverse}, apply
\cite[Theorem~4.3]{KahnGuta2009LANFiniteDimensional}.  Relabel their spectral
coordinate as \((h_1,\ldots,h_{d-1})\), with
\(h_d=-\sum_{i<d}h_i\).  The identification
\[
  (u_1,\ldots,u_{d-1})
  \longmapsto
  \left(u_1,\ldots,u_{d-1},-\sum_{i<d}u_i\right)
\]
has Jacobian \(\sqrt d\) and sends their covariance
\((p_i\delta_{ij}-p_ip_j)_{i,j<d}\) to \(\Sigma_p\).  For
\(R\in L^1(\R^{d-1};\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))\), the corresponding density is
\[
  \widehat R(h):=d^{-1/2}R(h_1,\ldots,h_{d-1}),
  \qquad
  \|\widehat R\|_{L^1(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))}
  =\|R\|_{L^1(\R^{d-1};\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))}.
\]
Thus \(R\mapsto\widehat R\) is an \(L^1\)-isometric channel isomorphism,
and their classical Gaussian becomes \(\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\).

For each \(j<k\), the two real tangent generators used in that theorem
combine as
\[
  \mathrm i\,\frac{\Re z_{jk}\,\mathrm i(E_{jk}-E_{kj})
             +\Im z_{jk}(E_{jk}+E_{kj})}
           {\sqrt{p_j-p_k}}
  =\frac{z_{jk}E_{kj}-\bar z_{jk}E_{jk}}
         {\sqrt{p_j-p_k}}.
\]
Thus, in their notation, \(U(z/\sqrt n)=g_{n,z}\), and
Equations~(4.3)--(4.5) give exactly
\[
  U\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt n}\right)
  \diag\!\left(p+\frac h{\sqrt n}\right)
  U\!\left(\frac z{\sqrt n}\right)^*
  =\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}.
\]
The LAN correspondence stated before the theorem identifies the oscillator
component with $\Phi_z^{(p)}$ in \eqref{eq:Phi-family-def}.

For compact \(K\), its coordinate image eventually lies in the growing
parameter sets of the cited theorem.  Composing their forward channel with
\(R\mapsto\widehat R\) and their reverse channel with its inverse preserves
the errors and proves
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-forward}--\eqref{eq:LAN-full-reverse}.  For
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-target}, since
\(\sqrt{\gamma_n}/\sqrt{m_n}=1/\sqrt n\) and \(X_p\) is real-linear,
\[
  \rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}
  =\rho_{p,\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,h,\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,z}^{(m_n)}.
\]
Choose \(S<\infty\) with \(\sqrt{\gamma_n},\sqrt\gamma\le S\), and set
\(K'=\{(th,tz):t\in[0,S],\ (h,z)\in K\}\).  Since \(K'\) is compact and
\(m_n\to\infty\), \eqref{eq:LAN-full-forward} gives
\[
  \sup_{(h,z)\in K}
  \left\|
    T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{full}}\!\left((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}\right)
    -\mathsf N_{\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,h,\Sigma_p}
     \ot\Phi_{\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,z}^{(p)}
  \right\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
Gaussian translation is \(L^1\)-continuous and Weyl conjugation is trace-norm
continuous.  Hence
\((t,h,z)\mapsto\mathsf N_{th,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_{tz}^{(p)}\) is uniformly
\(L^1\)-continuous on \([0,S]\times K\), so \(\gamma_n\to\gamma\) gives
\[
  \sup_{(h,z)\in K}
  \left\|
    \mathsf N_{\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,h,\Sigma_p}
      \ot\Phi_{\sqrt{\gamma_n}\,z}^{(p)}
    -\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma\,h,\Sigma_p}
      \ot\Phi_{\sqrt\gamma\,z}^{(p)}
  \right\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
The triangle inequality and
\(\Phi_{\sqrt\gamma z}^{(p)}=\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\) prove
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-target}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-spectrum LAN]\label{cor:LAN-orb}
Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\).  The full model restricted to \(h=0\), with the
parameter-independent classical Gaussian register discarded, yields channel
sequences
\[
  T_n^{\mathrm{orb}}:\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot n})\longrightarrow\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}),
  \qquad
  S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}:\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\longrightarrow\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot n})
\]
such that, uniformly for \(z\) in every compact subset of \(\C^{r_d}\),
\begin{align}
  \bigl\|T_n^{\mathrm{orb}}(\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot n})-\Phi_z^{(p)}\bigr\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0,
  \label{eq:LAN-known-forward}\\
  \bigl\|S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Phi_z^{(p)})-\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot n}\bigr\|_1
  &\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-known-reverse}
\end{align}
If \(m_n/n\to\gamma\), then, uniformly on compact \(z\)-sets,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}^{\ot m_n})
       -\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\bigr\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:LAN-known-target}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T_n^{\mathrm{full}},S_n^{\mathrm{full}}\) be the channels from
\cref{thm:LAN-full}.  Define the channels
\[
  \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{cl}}(R)=\int_{\mathsf H}R(x)\,\dd x,
  \qquad
  \iota_p(\sigma)(x)=\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\,\sigma.
\]
The integral is a Bochner integral.  The first channel discards the classical
register, while the second adjoins the normalized, parameter-independent
Gaussian state.  Set
\[
  T_n^{\mathrm{orb}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{cl}}\circ T_n^{\mathrm{full}},
  \qquad
  S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}=S_n^{\mathrm{full}}\circ\iota_p.
\]
Since \(\rho_{p,0,z}^{(n)}=\rho_{p,g_{n,z}}\) and
\(\iota_p(\Phi_z^{(p)})=\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)}\),
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-reverse} gives \eqref{eq:LAN-known-reverse}.  The bound
\(\|\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{cl}}(R)\|_1\le\|R\|_1\), applied at \(h=0\) to
\eqref{eq:LAN-full-forward} and \eqref{eq:LAN-full-target}, gives
\eqref{eq:LAN-known-forward} and \eqref{eq:LAN-known-target}.
\end{proof}

The converse proofs use the following direct fidelity-transfer consequence of
LAN.

\begin{lemma}[LAN fidelity transfer]\label{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer}
Let \(\{\rho_{n,\theta}\}_{\theta\in K}\) and
\(\{\sigma_{n,\theta}\}_{\theta\in K}\) be finite-dimensional input and
target models on a compact parameter set \(K\).  Let \(\{\Phi_\theta\}\)
and \(\{\Psi_\theta\}\) be limiting models, and suppose channels
\(S_n,T_n\) satisfy
\[
  \sup_{\theta\in K}\|S_n(\Phi_\theta)-\rho_{n,\theta}\|_1\to0,
  \qquad
  \sup_{\theta\in K}\|T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta})-\Psi_\theta\|_1\to0.
\]
For any sequence of finite-dimensional channels \(\cM_n\), define
\[
  \Gamma_n=T_n\circ\cM_n\circ S_n.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LAN-transfer-main}
  \inf_{\theta\in K}
  B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_\theta),\Psi_\theta)
  \ge
  \inf_{\theta\in K}
  B(\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),\sigma_{n,\theta})
  -o(1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
  \delta_n=
  \sup_{\theta\in K}\|S_n(\Phi_\theta)-\rho_{n,\theta}\|_1,
  \qquad
  \eta_n=
  \sup_{\theta\in K}\|T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta})-\Psi_\theta\|_1.
\]
Since \(T_n\circ\cM_n\) is a channel,
\[
  \|\Gamma_n(\Phi_\theta)-T_n\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta})\|_1
  \le\|S_n(\Phi_\theta)-\rho_{n,\theta}\|_1
  \le\delta_n.
\]
By continuity of root fidelity from \cref{lem:fid-cont},
\[
  B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_\theta),\Psi_\theta)
  \ge
  B(T_n\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta}))
  -\sqrt2\,(\sqrt{\delta_n}+\sqrt{\eta_n}).
\]
Monotonicity under the channel \(T_n\) gives
\[
  B(T_n\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),T_n(\sigma_{n,\theta}))
  \ge
  B(\cM_n(\rho_{n,\theta}),\sigma_{n,\theta}).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\theta\in K\) proves \eqref{eq:LAN-transfer-main}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Gaussian amplification bounds}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian flat-prior optimum]\label{thm:gaussian-flat-prior}
Let \(\Phi_z=\Phi_z^{(p)}\) and \(\Psi_z=\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}\) be the
multimode Gaussian input and target families in
\eqref{eq:Phi-family-def}--\eqref{eq:Psi-family-def}.  For the centered cubes
\[
  \mathsf Z_L
  =\{z\in\C^{r_d}:|\Re z_{ij}|,|\Im z_{ij}|\le L\text{ for all }i<j\}.
\]
Writing $|\mathsf Z_L|$ for its volume, one has
\[
  \lim_{L\to\infty}
  \sup_\Lambda
  \int_{\mathsf Z_L}B(\Lambda(\Phi_z),\Psi_z)\frac{\dd z}{|\mathsf Z_L|}
  =\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
Here the supremum is over all completely positive trace-preserving maps
\[
  \Lambda:\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}})\longrightarrow\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}).
\]
The product quantum-limited amplifier attains the value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The product quantum-limited amplifier maps each input mode to
\(\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}\) with the correct displacement and therefore has
constant fidelity \(\Borb(\gamma,p)\) by
\cref{eq:thermal-root-fid,eq:Borb-def}.  The matching upper bound is
\cref{prop:orbital-flat-prior-upper}.
\end{proof}

For a positive definite covariance \(\Sigma\) on \(\mathsf H\) and any
\(u\in\mathsf H\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-gaussian-factor}
  B(\mathsf N_{u,\gamma\Sigma},\mathsf N_{u,\Sigma})
  =\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
  B(\mathsf N_{u,\Sigma_1},\mathsf N_{u,\Sigma_2})
  =\frac{\det(\Sigma_1)^{1/4}\det(\Sigma_2)^{1/4}}
  {\det((\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2)/2)^{1/2}},
\]
where the determinants are taken on \(\mathsf H\).
For the spectral LAN model, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Buniv-def}
  \Bcl(\gamma,d)
  =\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(d-1)/2},
  \qquad
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)=\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
Let
\[
  \Omega_L
  =\left\{(h,z)\in\mathsf H\times\C^{r_d}:
  \|h\|_\infty\le L,\ |\Re z_{ij}|,|\Im z_{ij}|\le L
  \text{ for all }i<j\right\}.
\]
Give $\Omega_L$ the product of these measures and write $|\Omega_L|$ for its
volume.  Set
\[
  \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}=\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_z^{(p)},
  \qquad
  \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}
  =\mathsf N_{\sqrt{\gamma}\,h,\Sigma_p}\ot\Psi_z^{(p,\gamma)}.
\]
A hybrid channel is a classical--quantum channel between these $L^1$
state spaces, in the sense fixed in \cref{sec:preliminaries}.

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid Gaussian flat-prior optimum]\label{thm:hybrid-gaussian}
For every $p\in\Delta_d^\circ$,
\[
  \lim_{L\to\infty}
  \sup_\Lambda\frac1{|\Omega_L|}\int_{\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(\Lambda(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
  \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}\right)\,\dd h\,\dd z
  =\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
Here the supremum is over all hybrid channels.  The product of the classical
dilation $x\mapsto\sqrt{\gamma}\,x$ and the product quantum-limited amplifier
attains the value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The dilation $x\mapsto\sqrt\gamma\,x$ sends
$\mathsf N_{h,\Sigma_p}$ to $\mathsf N_{\sqrt\gamma h,\gamma \Sigma_p}$, and the
product quantum-limited amplifier sends $\Phi_z^{(p)}$ to the correctly
displaced product of the thermal states $\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}$.
Multiplicativity of root fidelity and
\cref{eq:classical-gaussian-factor,eq:thermal-root-fid} therefore give the
constant value $\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p)$.  The matching upper bound is
\cref{prop:hybrid-flat-prior-upper}; its proof does not assume that a competing
hybrid channel factors.
\end{proof}

\section{Known-spectrum cloning}\label{sec:known}

Fix \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\); only the orbit element \(g\) is unknown.  The
Schur transform separates the classical Young label from the
irreducible \(\U(d)\)-sector on which the Cartan channel acts.

\subsection{Protocol}

The known-spectrum channel $\cC_n^p$ performs the Schur transform, measures
the input Young label $\mu$, traces out the input Specht factor, and samples
an output label
\[
  \nu\sim P_{m_n,p}
\]
independently of $\mu$.  If $\nu-\mu$ is a partition, it applies the sector
channel $\cC_{\mu,\nu}$ to the state on $\cH_\mu$.  Otherwise it applies the
constant channel
\[
  X\longmapsto \Tr(X)\frac{I_{\cH_\nu}}{\dim\cH_\nu}.
\]
Finally, it adjoins the maximally mixed Specht factor in the $\nu$-block and
applies the inverse Schur transform.  Both conditional operations are
channels and are $\U(d)$-covariant, so $\cC_n^p$ is a covariant channel.
Because $\nu$ is sampled directly from $P_{m_n,p}$, its output Young law is
exactly the target Young law for every $n$.

\subsection{Optimality}

\begin{theorem}[Known-spectrum asymptotic optimum]
\label{thm:known-optimum}
For every \(p\in\Delta_d^\circ\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-optimum}
  \lim_{n\to\infty}
  \sup_{\cM_n}\inf_{g\in\U(d)}
  B\!\left(
    \cM_n(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),
    \rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}
  \right)
  =\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
Here the supremum is over all completely positive trace-preserving maps
\[
  \cM_n:\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot n})
  \longrightarrow\cT_1((\C^d)^{\ot m_n}).
\]
The Schur--Cartan cloner \(\cC_n^p\) attains the lower bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Achievability.}
By covariance, take $g=I$.  Under the protocol, the input and output labels
have the independent joint law
\[
  \Pi_{n,p}(\mu,\nu)=P_{n,p}(\mu)P_{m_n,p}(\nu),
\]
and the output marginal is exactly $P_{m_n,p}$.  Let
\[
  G_{n,p}=\mathsf T_n(p)\times\mathsf T_{m_n}(p).
\]
Young concentration at the fixed spectrum $p$ gives
$\Pi_{n,p}(G_{n,p}^c)=o(1)$.  For $(\mu,\nu)\in G_{n,p}$,
\[
  (\nu_i-\mu_i)-(\nu_{i+1}-\mu_{i+1})
  \ge (m_n-n)(p_i-p_{i+1})-2m_n^{2/3}-2n^{2/3}>0,
\]
and
\[
  \nu_d-\mu_d
  \ge (m_n-n)p_d-m_n^{2/3}-n^{2/3}>0
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.  Hence every pair in $G_{n,p}$ is
Cartan-admissible.

Choose a compact neighborhood of $p$ in $\Delta_d^\circ$ and put
\[
  \delta_n=n^{-1/3}+m_n^{-1/3}+|\gamma_n-\gamma|.
\]
On $G_{n,p}$, the hypotheses in the uniform part of
\cref{prop:thermal-sector} hold with error at most $\delta_n\to0$.  Hence
there are $\eta_n\to0$ such that
\[
  B\!\left(
    \cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),
    \rho_{p,\nu}
  \right)
  \ge\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n
\]
uniformly on $G_{n,p}$.  For all sufficiently large $n$, the right side
belongs to $[0,1]$.  The actual and target states have the same maximally
mixed Specht factor in every output block.  Applying
\cref{lem:block-good-set} with output law $Q=P_{m_n,p}$ gives
\[
  B\!\left(\cC_n^p(\rho_p^{\ot n}),\rho_p^{\ot m_n}\right)
  \ge
  \bigl(\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n\bigr)
  \bigl(1-\sqrt{\Pi_{n,p}(G_{n,p}^c)}\bigr)
  =\Borb(\gamma,p)-o(1).
\]

\emph{Converse.}
Let \(u_n(p)\) denote the finite-\(n\) minimax value on the left of
\eqref{eq:known-optimum}, and choose \(\cM_n\) with worst-case fidelity at
least \(u_n(p)-1/n\).  For fixed \(L\), let \(S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}\) and \(T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{orb}}\) be the
inverse input and forward output LAN channels from \cref{cor:LAN-orb}, and
set
\[
  \Gamma_n=T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{orb}}\circ\cM_n\circ S_n^{\mathrm{orb}}.
\]
Since the local orbit \(g_{n,z}=\exp(X_p(z)/\sqrt n)\),
\(z\in\mathsf Z_L\), is contained in the full unitary orbit,
\cref{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer} gives
\[
  u_n(p)
  \le
  \inf_{z\in\mathsf Z_L}B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_z),\Psi_z)+o(1)
  \le
  \int_{\mathsf Z_L}
  B(\Gamma_n(\Phi_z),\Psi_z)\frac{\dd z}{|\mathsf Z_L|}+o(1).
\]
Taking \(\limsup_n\), then the supremum over Gaussian channels, and finally
letting \(L\to\infty\), \cref{thm:gaussian-flat-prior} gives
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}u_n(p)\le\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
Together with achievability, this proves \eqref{eq:known-optimum}.
\end{proof}

\section{Unknown-spectrum cloning}\label{sec:unknown}

The Young label carries the spectral fluctuation, while the irreducible
$\U(d)$-sector carries the orbital fluctuation.  We dilate the label by a
spectrum-independent dither and apply the sector channel whenever the sampled
label is Cartan-admissible; otherwise we use a fixed fallback state.  The
dither contributes $\Bcl(\gamma,d)$, while
\cref{prop:thermal-sector} contributes $\Borb(\gamma,p)$.  Their product is
$\Buniv(\gamma,p)$ from \eqref{eq:Buniv-def}.

\subsection{Construction and uniform achievability}

Fix the half-open fundamental cell
\[
  \mathsf C=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}t_i(e_i-e_d):-\frac12\le t_i<\frac12\right\}
  \subset\mathsf H
\]
of $\mathsf H\cap\mathbb Z^d$.  Volumes involving $\mathsf C$ are taken with the
induced $(d-1)$-dimensional Lebesgue measure on affine hyperplanes parallel to
$\mathsf H$.  Let
\[
  \mathsf L_N=\left\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:\sum_i x_i=N\right\},
  \qquad
  \nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}=(m_n,0,\ldots,0).
\]
Given $\mu\in\mathsf L_n$, choose $U$ uniformly in $\mathsf C$ and let
$\widetilde\nu\in\mathsf L_{m_n}$ be the unique lattice point such that
\[
  \gamma_n(\mu+U)\in\widetilde\nu+\mathsf C
\]
(up to a null set of cell boundaries).  The induced kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dithered-kernel}
  \mathsf D_n(\widetilde\nu\mid\mu)
  =\frac{|\gamma_n(\mu+\mathsf C)\cap(\widetilde\nu+\mathsf C)|}
  {\gamma_n^{d-1}|\mathsf C|}.
\end{equation}
For $\mu\vdash n$, call $(\mu,\widetilde\nu)$ admissible when
$\widetilde\nu-\mu$ is a partition.  In that case
$\widetilde\nu=\mu+(\widetilde\nu-\mu)\vdash m_n$, so
$\cC_{\mu,\widetilde\nu}$ is defined.  The proof of the following proposition,
including the required Young local limit, is given in \cref{app:young-dither}.

\begin{proposition}[Dithered Young-label kernel]\label{prop:young-dither}
There is a constant $c_{\mathrm{dit}}<\infty$, independent of $n$, such that
\[
  \mathsf D_n(\widetilde\nu\mid\mu)>0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \left\|\frac{\widetilde\nu}{m_n}-\frac\mu n\right\|_\infty
  =\frac{\|\widetilde\nu-\gamma_n\mu\|_\infty}{m_n}
  \le\frac{c_{\mathrm{dit}}}{m_n}.
\]
For every compact $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
if $p\in K$ and $\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)$, then every pair $(\mu,\widetilde\nu)$ with
$\widetilde\nu$ in the support of $\mathsf D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)$ is admissible.

Let $\mu\sim P_{n,p}$ and, conditionally on $\mu$, let
$\widetilde\nu\sim \mathsf D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)$.  Replace $\widetilde\nu$ by
$\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}$ on every nonadmissible pair, and denote the resulting label law
by $Q_{n,p}$.  The replacement probability tends to zero uniformly for
$p\in K$, and
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left|B(Q_{n,p},P_{m_n,p})-\Bcl(\gamma,d)\right|
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{proposition}

The channel $\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}$ performs the Schur transform, measures
$\mu$, traces out the input Specht factor, and samples $\widetilde\nu$ from
$\mathsf D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)$.  On an admissible branch it sets the final label
$\nu=\widetilde\nu$ and applies $\cC_{\mu,\widetilde\nu}$ to the resulting
state on $\cH_\mu$.  Otherwise it sets $\nu=\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}$ and prepares
$I_{\cH_{\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}}}/\dim\cH_{\nu_n^{\mathrm{fb}}}$.  It then adjoins the maximally
mixed Specht factor and applies the inverse Schur transform.
The sampling rule is a Markov kernel, and both conditional operations are
channels.  The kernel is independent of the
spectrum and eigenbasis, the sector maps are \(\U(d)\)-covariant, and the
fallback state is invariant.
Hence $\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}$ is a spectrum-independent,
$\U(d)$-covariant channel.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform spectrum-independent achievability]
\label{thm:unknown-achievability}
For every compact $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$,
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}
  \inf_{\substack{p\in K\\g\in\U(d)}}
  \left[
  B\!\left(
    \cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),
    \rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}
  \right)
  -\Buniv(\gamma,p)
  \right]
  \ge0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By covariance, take \(g=I\).  Uniformly for \(p\in K\),
\[
  P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)=o(1)
\]
by \cref{lem:young-concentration}.  By \cref{prop:young-dither}, every
branch with \(\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)\) is admissible for all sufficiently
large \(n\).  On this event, the support bound gives
\[
  \left\|\frac{\widetilde\nu}{m_n}-p\right\|_\infty
  \le
  \left\|\frac\mu n-p\right\|_\infty+
  \frac{c_{\mathrm{dit}}}{m_n}
  \le n^{-1/3}+\frac{c_{\mathrm{dit}}}{m_n}.
\]
Choose a compact neighborhood $K'\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$ of $K$.  For all
large $n$, the normalized labels on the typical-input event lie in $K'$, and
all three errors required in the uniform statement of \cref{prop:thermal-sector} are bounded
by
\[
  n^{-1/3}+\frac{c_{\mathrm{dit}}}{m_n}+|\gamma_n-\gamma|\longrightarrow0.
\]
That proposition therefore gives numbers $\eta_n\to0$, independent of
$p\in K$ and of the branch with $\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)$, such that
\[
  B\!\left(
    \cC_{\mu,\widetilde\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),
    \rho_{p,\widetilde\nu}
  \right)
  \ge\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n.
\]

Let $Q_{n,p}$ be the final output-label law.  By
\cref{prop:young-dither}, uniformly for $p\in K$,
\[
  B(Q_{n,p},P_{m_n,p})=\Bcl(\gamma,d)+o(1).
\]
The actual and ideal states have the same maximally mixed Specht factor in
each final Schur block.  Since $\Borb(\gamma,\cdot)$ has a positive minimum
on $K$, the number $\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n$ lies in $[0,1]$ for all large
$n$.  Apply \cref{lem:block-good-set} with
$x=(\mu,\widetilde\nu)$, $y=\nu$, and good event $\{\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)\}$.  This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  B\!\left(
    \cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}(\rho_p^{\ot n}),
    \rho_p^{\ot m_n}
  \right)
  &\ge
  \bigl(\Borb(\gamma,p)-\eta_n\bigr)
  \left(B(Q_{n,p},P_{m_n,p})-\sqrt{P_{n,p}(\mathsf T_n(p)^c)}\right)\\
  &=\Buniv(\gamma,p)-o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly for $p\in K$.  This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local and global optimality}

Fix $p\in\Delta_d^\circ$.  For $L>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, let
\[
  v_{n,L}(p)
  =\sup_{\cM_n}\inf_{(h,z)\in\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(
    \cM_n\bigl((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}\bigr),
    (\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}
  \right),
\]
where the supremum is over all channels from $n$ to $m_n$ copies.

\begin{theorem}[Expanding-window local asymptotic minimax value]
\label{thm:unknown-local}
For every $p\in\Delta_d^\circ$,
\[
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)
  =
  \lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)
  =\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $L$ and choose a compact neighborhood $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$ of
$p$.  For all sufficiently large $n$, every spectrum
$p+h/\sqrt n$ with $(h,z)\in\Omega_L$ lies in $K$, and
$\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)}=\rho_{p+h/\sqrt n,g_{n,z}}$.  Thus
\cref{thm:unknown-achievability} gives, uniformly on $\Omega_L$,
\[
  B\!\left(
    \cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}
    \bigl((\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot n}\bigr),
    (\rho_{p,h,z}^{(n)})^{\ot m_n}
  \right)
  \ge
  \Buniv\!\left(\gamma,p+\frac h{\sqrt n}\right)-o(1).
\]
Uniform continuity of $\Buniv(\gamma,\cdot)$ on $K$ yields
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)\ge\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]

For the converse, choose $\cM_n$ with worst-case fidelity at least
$v_{n,L}(p)-1/n$, and form the hybrid channel
\[
  \Gamma_n=T_{m_n}^{\mathrm{full}}\circ\cM_n\circ S_n^{\mathrm{full}}
\]
from the full LAN channels in \cref{thm:LAN-full}.  Applied on the compact
parameter set $\Omega_L$, \cref{lem:LAN-fidelity-transfer} gives
\[
  v_{n,L}(p)
  \le
  \inf_{(h,z)\in\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(
    \Gamma_n(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
    \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}
  \right)+o(1).
\]
The infimum is no larger than the average, and $\Gamma_n$ is an admissible
hybrid channel.  Hence
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}v_{n,L}(p)
  \le
  \sup_\Lambda\frac1{|\Omega_L|}
  \int_{\Omega_L}
  B\!\left(
    \Lambda(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
    \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}}
  \right)\,\dd h\,\dd z.
\]
Letting $L\to\infty$ and applying \cref{thm:hybrid-gaussian} gives the
matching upper bound.  The quantities
$\limsup_n v_{n,L}(p)$ and $\liminf_n v_{n,L}(p)$ are nonincreasing in $L$,
so the outer limits exist.  The lower and upper bounds prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compact-spectrum global worst-case value]
\label{cor:compact-global-unknown}
Assume $0<\kappa<2/[d(d+1)]$ and set
\[
  \Delta_{d,\kappa}
  =\left\{p\in\R^d:p_d\ge\kappa,\ p_i-p_{i+1}\ge\kappa\ (i<d),\
  \sum_i p_i=1\right\}.
\]
Then $\Delta_{d,\kappa}$ is a nonempty compact subset of
$\Delta_d^\circ$ with nonempty interior relative to the probability
hyperplane, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-global-value}
  \lim_{n\to\infty}
  \sup_{\cM_n}
  \inf_{\substack{p\in\Delta_{d,\kappa}\\g\in\U(d)}}
  B\!\left(\cM_n(\rho_{p,g}^{\ot n}),\rho_{p,g}^{\ot m_n}\right)
  =\inf_{p\in\Delta_{d,\kappa}}\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
The channel $\cC_n^{\mathrm{univ}}$ attains the lower bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every $p\in\Delta_{d,\kappa}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chamber-threshold}
  1=d p_d+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}i(p_i-p_{i+1})
  \ge\frac{\kappa d(d+1)}2.
\end{equation}
To show that the strict inequality in the hypothesis is sufficient, choose
$\kappa'$ with
$\kappa<\kappa'<2/[d(d+1)]$ and set
\[
  p_i=\frac1d+\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-i\right)\kappa'
  \qquad(1\le i\le d).
\]
Then every adjacent gap is $\kappa'>\kappa$ and
\[
  p_d=\frac1d-\frac{d-1}{2}\kappa'
  >\frac{2}{d(d+1)}>\kappa.
\]
This point lies in the interior relative to the probability hyperplane.  The
chamber is compact because it is closed in the probability simplex.

Let $w_n$ denote the finite-$n$ minimax value on the left of
\eqref{eq:compact-global-value}.  The uniform achievability theorem gives
\[
  \liminf_{n\to\infty}w_n
  \ge\inf_{p\in\Delta_{d,\kappa}}\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
For the reverse inequality, fix a point $p_0$ in that relative interior.
For every fixed $L$, the local spectra $p_0+h/\sqrt n$ with
$(h,z)\in\Omega_L$ lie in the chamber for all sufficiently large $n$.
The corresponding local orbit family is therefore a subfamily of the
chamber model, and
\[
  w_n\le v_{n,L}(p_0)
\]
for all large $n$.  Letting first $n\to\infty$ and then $L\to\infty$, and
using \cref{thm:unknown-local}, gives
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}w_n\le\Buniv(\gamma,p_0).
\]
This relative interior is dense in the convex chamber.  Taking the infimum
over $p_0$ there and using continuity of $\Buniv(\gamma,\cdot)$ proves
\eqref{eq:compact-global-value}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Boundary cases.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:chamber-threshold} shows that the chamber is empty when
$\kappa>2/[d(d+1)]$.  At $\kappa=2/[d(d+1)]$, equality forces
$p_d=\kappa$ and $p_i-p_{i+1}=\kappa$ for every $i<d$.  The chamber is
then a singleton, so at its unique spectrum $p$ the optimal value is the
known-spectrum value $\Borb(\gamma,p)$ from \cref{thm:known-optimum}, not
$\Buniv(\gamma,p)$.  This is why \cref{cor:compact-global-unknown}
assumes a strict inequality.  Spectral collisions and the simplex boundary
require different LAN models because stabilizers appear or oscillator modes
disappear.

\section{Comparison and discussion}\label{sec:comparison}

\paragraph{Pure-state cloning and random purification.}
For a \(D\)-dimensional pure state, Werner's universal \(n\to m\) cloner has
pure-target overlap (that is, squared root fidelity)
\(\binom{n+D-1}{D-1}/\binom{m+D-1}{D-1}\).  In the root convention used here,
its value is
\[
  \left(
  \frac{\binom{n+D-1}{D-1}}
       {\binom{m+D-1}{D-1}}
  \right)^{1/2}.
\]
The random-purification plus pure-state-cloning protocol of
\cite[Theorem~S3.1 and the paragraph following it]{LiTheilHarrowChuang2026}
therefore gives, for rank \(r\), the root-fidelity guarantee
\[
  \left(
  \frac{\binom{n+dr-1}{dr-1}}
       {\binom{m+dr-1}{dr-1}}
  \right)^{1/2}
  \longrightarrow\gamma^{-(dr-1)/2}.
\]
This is a guarantee on the larger universal rank-\(r\) model, not an exact
formula for that channel on a fixed orbit.

For every \(0<q<1\) and \(\gamma>1\), the one-root factor in
\eqref{eq:one-mode-b} satisfies
\[
  b_\gamma(q)>\gamma^{-1/2}.
\]
Indeed, after multiplying by the positive denominator and cancelling
\(\sqrt{\gamma}\), the claim is equivalent to
\(\sqrt{q(\gamma-1+q)}>q/\sqrt{\gamma}\), whose square differs by
\(q(\gamma-1)(1+q/\gamma)>0\).  Since \(r_d=\binom d2\),
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)>\gamma^{-r_d/2}.
\]
For full rank, this is strictly larger than the universal guarantee
\(\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/2}\).  Moreover,
\(2\sqrt{\gamma}/(1+\gamma)>\gamma^{-1/2}\), so
\[
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)
  >\gamma^{-(d-1)/4}\gamma^{-d(d-1)/4}
  =\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/4}
  >\gamma^{-(d^2-1)/2}.
\]

\paragraph{Broadcasting versus cloning.}
The target here is the full product \(\rho^{\ot m}\).  Broadcasting only
requires selected output marginals to reproduce the input and permits strong
correlations.  This is why exact multi-input broadcasting or
superbroadcasting results do not conflict with the product-state cloning
bounds proved here
\cite{DArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2005,
BuscemiDArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2005Optimal,
BuscemiDArianoMacchiavelloPerinotti2006,
DArianoPerinottiSacchi2006Continuous}.  Earlier mixed-state cloning bounds
and restricted constructions likewise use different model classes or
figures of merit \cite{Rastegin2003Upper,Rastegin2002Relative,
Rastegin2003StateDependent,Fan2003,FanLiuShi2007,Kazakov2007}.

\paragraph{Limiting regimes.}
For fixed \(p\), as \(\gamma\to\infty\),
\[
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  =
  \gamma^{-d(d-1)/4}
  \prod_{i<j}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{p_j}{p_i}}\right)
  \left(1+O(\gamma^{-1})\right).
\]
Near the identity, write \(\gamma=1+\eps\).  Then
\[
  b_{1+\eps}(q)
  =1-\frac{\eps^2}{8q}+O(\eps^3),
\]
so the known-spectrum loss is quadratic in \(\gamma-1\).  In contrast,
the quoted full-rank random-purification guarantee has a linear loss:
\[
  (1+\eps)^{-(d^2-1)/2}
  =1-\frac{d^2-1}{2}\eps+O(\eps^2).
\]

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Natural extensions include quantitative finite-\(n\) bounds, degenerate or
rank-deficient spectra, finite-sector optimality of the Cartan channel, and
efficient implementation of the Cartan intertwiners.  Degenerate and boundary
spectra require a separate analysis because stabilizers appear and some
oscillator modes collapse.

\section{PBW normal form and the Cartan-sector limit}\label{app:sector-limit}

This appendix proves \cref{prop:thermal-sector}.  It constructs a
cutoff-independent PBW basis whose fixed-excitation Gram matrices converge to
the identity.  In that basis the Cartan inclusion has asymptotic
beam-splitter coefficients, and a cutoff argument gives trace-norm convergence
of the thermal sector.

\subsection{PBW Gram normal form}

Identify the positive roots of $\U(d)$ with
\[
  \mathsf R_+=\{e_i-e_j:1\le i<j\le d\},
  \qquad |\mathsf R_+|=r_d.
\]
For $\alpha=e_i-e_j\in\mathsf R_+$, set
\[
  F_\alpha=E_{ji},
  \qquad E_\alpha=E_{ij},
  \qquad H_\alpha=[E_\alpha,F_\alpha]=E_{ii}-E_{jj},
  \qquad \Delta_\alpha^\lambda=\lambda_i-\lambda_j.
\]
Fix a total order on $\mathsf R_+$.  For a multi-index
$\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}$, write
\[
  F^{\mathbf r}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}^{\prec}F_\alpha^{r_\alpha},
  \qquad |\mathbf r|=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha.
\]
The PBW theorem implies that the vectors $F^{\mathbf r}v_\lambda$ span
$\cH_\lambda$ \cite{FultonHarris1991}.

For a positive-root combination $\eta$, define the finite set
\[
  \mathsf P(\eta)
  =\left\{\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}:
  \sum_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}r_\alpha\alpha=\eta\right\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[PBW Gram normal form]\label{lem:PBW-normal-form}
Let $\lambda=\lambda^{(N)}$ range over dominant weights satisfying
\[
  cN\le\Delta_\alpha^\lambda\le CN
  \qquad(\alpha\in\mathsf R_+)
\]
for fixed $0<c<C<\infty$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-prebasis}
  \widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda
  =
  \prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}^{\prec}
  \frac{F_\alpha^{r_\alpha}}
       {\sqrt{r_\alpha!}(\Delta_\alpha^\lambda)^{r_\alpha/2}}
  v_\lambda.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $M$, uniformly for $|\mathbf r|,|\mathbf s|\le M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-Gram}
  \langle\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda,
         \widetilde e_{\mathbf s}^\lambda\rangle
  =\delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}+O_M(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}

Fix the induced order on each $\mathsf P(\eta)$.  Whenever its full Gram
matrix $G_{\lambda,\eta}$ is invertible, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-block-orthonormalization}
  (e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda)_{\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)}
  :=
  (\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda)_{\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)}
  G_{\lambda,\eta}^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $L$, all full blocks meeting
$\{|\mathbf r|\le L\}$ are invertible for sufficiently large $N$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PBW-orthonormal-close}
  e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda
  =\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda+O_L(N^{-1/2})
  \qquad(|\mathbf r|\le L).
\end{equation}
The definition uses the entire exact-weight block and is therefore
independent of the cutoff used in an estimate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\operatorname{ht}$ be simple-root height.  If
$\mathbf r\in\mathsf P(\eta)$, then
$|\mathbf r|\le\operatorname{ht}(\eta)$, so $\mathsf P(\eta)$ is finite.
If the block contains an index with $|\mathbf r|\le L$, then every
$\mathbf s\in\mathsf P(\eta)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-block-cutoff-bound}
  |\mathbf s|\le\operatorname{ht}(\eta)\le(d-1)L.
\end{equation}
Thus every full block meeting a fixed cutoff lies in a larger fixed cutoff.

Put
\[
  \widehat F_\alpha=\frac{F_\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda}},
  \qquad
  \widehat E_\alpha=\frac{E_\alpha}{\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda}}.
\]
On every fixed cutoff these operators are uniformly bounded.  Indeed,
decompose into $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\alpha)$-strings.  A vector in the cutoff lies
$O_L(1)$ steps below the top of a string whose highest weight is
$\Delta_\alpha^\lambda+O_L(1)$; the standard string formulas then give
$\|F_\alpha\|+\|E_\alpha\|=O_L(\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda})$.  Every word of
at most $2M$ root operators stays within simple-root height $2M(d-1)$, so one
fixed enlargement contains all intermediate vectors used below.

The matrix-unit commutator formula gives, on the enlarged cutoff,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-root-commutator}
  [\widehat E_\alpha,\widehat F_\beta]
  =\delta_{\alpha\beta}I+O_L(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=\beta$, this follows from
$[E_\alpha,F_\alpha]=H_\alpha$ and
$H_\alpha/\Delta_\alpha^\lambda=I+O_L(N^{-1})$ on the relevant weight
spaces.  For $\alpha\ne\beta$, the commutator is either zero or one root
operator of norm $O_L(\sqrt N)$ divided by
$\sqrt{\Delta_\alpha^\lambda\Delta_\beta^\lambda}\asymp N$, hence is
$O_L(N^{-1/2})$.

Write
$\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^\lambda
=c_{\mathbf r}\widehat F^{\mathbf r}v_\lambda$ with
$c_{\mathbf r}=\prod_\alpha(r_\alpha!)^{-1/2}$.
Fix $M$.  For a word
$\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_k)$ with $k\le M$,
\eqref{eq:mixed-root-commutator} gives, on the fixed enlargement,
\begin{align*}
  \widehat E_\alpha\widehat F_{\beta_1}\cdots\widehat F_{\beta_k}
  &={}
  \widehat F_{\beta_1}\cdots\widehat F_{\beta_k}\widehat E_\alpha
  +\sum_{j=1}^k\delta_{\alpha\beta_j}
  \widehat F_{\beta_1}\cdots\widehat F_{\beta_{j-1}}
  \widehat F_{\beta_{j+1}}\cdots\widehat F_{\beta_k}
  +R_{\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta},\\
  \|R_{\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta}\|
  &\le C_MN^{-1/2}.
\end{align*}
The remainder is a sum of at most $M$ products of uniformly bounded
normalized root operators containing one commutator error.  Since
$\widehat E_\alpha v_\lambda=0$, induction on the number of raising operators
gives
\[
  \langle v_\lambda,(\widehat F^{\mathbf r})^*
  \widehat F^{\mathbf s}v_\lambda\rangle
  =\delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}\prod_\alpha r_\alpha!
   +O_M(N^{-1/2}).
\]
The error-free terms are precisely the complete same-root contractions and
give the factorials; unmatched terms vanish by the highest-weight property
or weight orthogonality.  The number of terms and all bounds depend only on
$M,d,c,C$.  Multiplying by $c_{\mathbf r}c_{\mathbf s}$ proves
\eqref{eq:PBW-Gram} uniformly in $\lambda$.

By \eqref{eq:full-block-cutoff-bound}, every full block meeting
$\{|\mathbf r|\le L\}$ has Gram matrix $I+O_L(N^{-1/2})$.  It is therefore
positive definite for large $N$, and functional calculus gives
$G_{\lambda,\eta}^{-1/2}=I+O_L(N^{-1/2})$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:PBW-orthonormal-close} follows, as does cutoff independence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Thermal Weyl-sector states}

For a vector $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)$, write
\[
  p^a=\prod_{i=1}^d p_i^{a_i}.
\]


\paragraph{Uniform character normalization.}
Let $K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ$, let $\delta_N\to0$, and suppose that
$p\in K$ and $\lambda\vdash N$ satisfy
$\|\lambda/N-p\|_\infty\le\delta_N$.  Uniformly over this family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-character-normalization}
  \frac{p^\lambda}{s_\lambda(p)}
  =\prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)+o(1).
\end{equation}
To prove this, write the numerator in the Weyl character formula as
\[
  \sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_d}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)A_\sigma,
  \qquad
  A_\sigma=\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+d-\sigma(i)}.
\]
For every $\sigma\ne\mathrm{id}$,
\[
  \frac1N\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{id}}}{A_\sigma}
  =\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i}{N}\log p_i
   -\sum_i\frac{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}{N}\log p_i+O(N^{-1}).
\]
At $\lambda/N=p$, strict rearrangement makes the right side positive.
Compactness of $K$, continuity, and finiteness of $\mathfrak S_d$ give a
uniform positive lower bound when $\lambda/N$ is sufficiently close to
$p$.  Hence every nonidentity term is exponentially smaller than the
identity term.  Dividing the identity term by
\[
  \det(p_i^{d-j})_{i,j=1}^d
  =\prod_i p_i^{d-i}\prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)
\]
gives \eqref{eq:uniform-character-normalization}.

The next lemma records the zero-displacement fixed-sector thermal limit in
the PBW normalization used for the Cartan-sector cloning construction.

\begin{lemma}[Thermal limit of a Weyl-sector Gibbs state]
  \label{lem:sector-thermal}
  Let $\lambda_N\vdash N$ satisfy
  $\lambda_N/N\to p$, where
  \[
          p_1>\cdots>p_d>0\,.
  \]
  For $\alpha=e_i-e_j\in\mathsf R_+$, set
  \[
          q_\alpha:=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
  \]
  Index the standard number basis of \(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}\) by
  \(\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}\), and denote it by
  \(\{\ket{\mathbf r}\}\).  Then there exist isometries
  \[
          J_N:\cH_{\lambda_N}\longrightarrow \cF_{\mathrm{orb}}
  \]
  such that
  \[
          \left\|
          J_N\rho_{p,\lambda_N}J_N^*
          -
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0\,.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Set
  \[
          \tau:=
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          =
          \sum_{\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathsf R_+}}
          \left[
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}(1-q_\alpha)q_\alpha^{r_\alpha}
          \right]
          |\mathbf r\rangle\langle\mathbf r|,
  \]
  and
  \[
          P_{\le L}:=
          \sum_{|\mathbf r|\le L}|\mathbf r\rangle\langle\mathbf r|
          \qquad(L=0,1,2,\ldots).
  \]
  The proof is a cutoff argument.  We first construct a single sequence
  \(J_N\), once and for all, using the fixed full-block Gram prescription in
  \cref{lem:PBW-normal-form}.

  By \cref{lem:PBW-normal-form} and a diagonal choice, there are integers
  \(L_N\to\infty\) such that every full Gram block meeting
  \(\{|\mathbf r|\le L_N\}\) is positive definite.  Apply
  \eqref{eq:full-block-orthonormalization} to the finite union of all these
  blocks.  The resulting vectors are orthonormal: vectors in the same block
  were orthonormalized together, while vectors in distinct blocks have
  different weights.  Map each such \(e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda_N}\) to
  \(|\mathbf r\rangle\), and extend this map arbitrarily to an isometric
  embedding
  \[
    J_N:\cH_{\lambda_N}\longrightarrow\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}.
  \]  For every
  fixed \(L\), one has
  \[
    J_N e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda_N}=|\mathbf r\rangle
    \qquad(|\mathbf r|\le L)
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(N\), because then \(L_N\ge L\).

  Write
  \[
          \rho_{p,\lambda}
          =
          \frac{\pi_\lambda(\rho_p)}{s_\lambda(p)},
          \qquad
          \rho_p=\diag(p_1,\ldots,p_d),
          \qquad
          s_\lambda(p)=\Tr\pi_\lambda(\rho_p).
  \]
  If \(|\mathbf r|\le L_N\), then \(e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda_N}\) has weight
  \(\lambda_N-\sum_\alpha r_\alpha\alpha\).  For a weight \(\zeta\),
  \(\pi_{\lambda_N}(\rho_p)\) acts by the scalar \(p^\zeta\).  Since
  \(p^{-\alpha}=p_j/p_i=q_\alpha\) for \(\alpha=e_i-e_j\),
  \[
          \rho_{p,\lambda_N}e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda_N}
          =
          c_N
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{r_\alpha}
          e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda_N},
          \qquad
          c_N:=\frac{p^{\lambda_N}}{s_{\lambda_N}(p)}\,.
  \]
  By \eqref{eq:uniform-character-normalization},
  \[
          c_N=\frac{p^{\lambda_N}}{s_{\lambda_N}(p)}
          \longrightarrow
          \prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)
          =\prod_{\alpha\in\mathsf R_+}(1-q_\alpha).
  \]

  Let
  \[
          \widehat\rho_N:=J_N\rho_{p,\lambda_N}J_N^*\,.
  \]
  For fixed \(L\) and all sufficiently large \(N\) such that
  \(L_N\ge L\),
  \[
          P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N P_{\le L}
          =
          \sum_{|\mathbf r|\le L}
          c_N
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{r_\alpha}
          |\mathbf r\rangle\langle\mathbf r|,
  \]
  while
  \[
          P_{\le L}\tau P_{\le L}
          =
          \sum_{|\mathbf r|\le L}
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}(1-q_\alpha)q_\alpha^{r_\alpha}
          |\mathbf r\rangle\langle\mathbf r|.
  \]
  Therefore, for every fixed \(L\),
  \[
          \left\|
          P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N P_{\le L}
          -
          P_{\le L}\tau P_{\le L}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0,
          \qquad
          \Tr[P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N]
          \longrightarrow
          \Tr[P_{\le L}\tau].
  \]

  Now fix \(\eps>0\).  Choose \(L\) such that
  \[
          \Tr[(I-P_{\le L})\tau]<\eps.
  \]
  For all sufficiently large \(N\),
  \[
          \left|
          \Tr[P_{\le L}(\widehat\rho_N-\tau)]
          \right|<\eps,
          \qquad
          \left\|
          P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N P_{\le L}
          -
          P_{\le L}\tau P_{\le L}
          \right\|_1
          <\eps\,.
  \]
  Since \(\Tr\widehat\rho_N=\Tr\tau=1\),
  \[
          \Tr[(I-P_{\le L})\widehat\rho_N]
          =
          \Tr[(I-P_{\le L})\tau]
          -
          \Tr[P_{\le L}(\widehat\rho_N-\tau)]
          <2\eps.
  \]
  The gentle-measurement estimate
  \[
          \|\sigma-P\sigma P\|_1
          \le
          2\sqrt{\Tr[(I-P)\sigma]}
  \]
  applied with \(P=P_{\le L}\) gives
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
          \|\widehat\rho_N-\tau\|_1
          &\le
          \|\widehat\rho_N-P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N P_{\le L}\|_1
          +
          \left\|
          P_{\le L}\widehat\rho_N P_{\le L}
          -
          P_{\le L}\tau P_{\le L}
          \right\|_1
          +
          \|\tau-P_{\le L}\tau P_{\le L}\|_1                 \\
          &<
          2\sqrt{2\eps}
          +
          \eps
          +
          2\sqrt{\eps}.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Since \(\eps\) is arbitrary, \(\|\widehat\rho_N-\tau\|_1\to0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the thermal Cartan-sector limit}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:thermal-sector}]
  It is enough to prove the compact-uniform assertion at the end of the
  statement.  Indeed, the pointwise assertion follows by choosing a compact
  \(K\) in the strict chamber which contains \(p\) and, for all large \(n\),
  also contains \(\mu_n/n\) and \(\nu_n/m_n\), and then taking
  \[
          \delta_n
          =
          \sup_{k\ge n}
          \max\left\{
          \left|\frac{m_k}{k}-\gamma\right|,
          \left\|\frac{\mu_k}{k}-p\right\|_\infty,
          \left\|\frac{\nu_k}{m_k}-p\right\|_\infty
          \right\}.
  \]

  We therefore fix \(K\) and \(\delta_n\) as in the uniform statement.
  Throughout the remainder of the proof, every \(o(1)\) estimate is uniform over the allowed
  \(p,m,\mu,\nu\) at the fixed \(n\); the notation \(o_L(1)\) permits the
  rate to depend on the fixed cutoff \(L\).  Each
  $\sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}$ is restricted to this allowed family.
  Put \(\omega=\nu-\mu\).  Compactness of
  \(K\Subset\Delta_d^\circ\) gives a uniform positive lower bound for
  \(x_d\) and every adjacent gap \(x_i-x_{i+1}\), \(x\in K\).
  Since \(|m/n-\gamma|\le\delta_n\), \(\gamma>1\), and \(\delta_n\to0\),
  the denominators \(m/n-1\) are bounded below by \((\gamma-1)/2\) for all
  large \(n\).  Hence, using
  \(\|\mu/n-p\|_\infty,\|\nu/m-p\|_\infty\le\delta_n\),
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
          \left\|\frac{\omega}{m-n}-p\right\|_\infty
          &=
          \left\|
          \frac{m/n}{m/n-1}\left(\frac{\nu}{m}-p\right)
          -
          \frac1{m/n-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{n}-p\right)
          \right\|_\infty  \\
          &\le
          \frac{m/n+1}{m/n-1}\,\delta_n
          =O(\delta_n)=o(1).
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Since the coordinates and adjacent gaps are uniformly positive on $K$,
  \(\|\omega/(m-n)-p\|_\infty=o(1)\) implies
  \(\omega\vdash m-n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  For
  \(\alpha=e_i-e_j\in\mathsf R_+\), define the splitting fraction
  \[
          s_{\alpha,n}
          :=\frac{\Delta_\alpha^\mu}
               {\Delta_\alpha^\nu}
          =
          \frac{n}{m}
          \frac{\mu_i/n-\mu_j/n}{\nu_i/m-\nu_j/m}
          =
          \frac1\gamma+o(1),
          \qquad
          1-s_{\alpha,n}
          =
          \frac{\Delta_\alpha^\omega}
               {\Delta_\alpha^\nu}.
  \]
  The uniform estimates $\mu/n=p+o(1)$, $\nu/m=p+o(1)$, and
  $\omega/(m-n)=p+o(1)$, together with $p\in K$ and
  $m/n\to\gamma>1$, give constants
  \(0<c_K<C_K<\infty\), depending only on \(K\) and \(\gamma\), such that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-sector-gaps}
          c_Kn
          \le
          \Delta_\alpha^\mu,
          \Delta_\alpha^\nu,
          \Delta_\alpha^\omega
          \le
          C_Kn
          \qquad(\alpha\in \mathsf R_+)
  \end{equation}
  for all large \(n\).

  \paragraph{Finite-cutoff Cartan coefficients.}
  Let \(V=V_{\mu,\omega}:\cH_\nu\to\cH_\mu\ot\cH_\omega\) be the Cartan
  inclusion from \eqref{eq:cartan-inclusion}.  Write
  \(F_\alpha^{(\lambda)}=d\pi_\lambda(F_\alpha)\) for the root operator
  in \(\cH_\lambda\).  Since \(V\) is an intertwiner and sends
  \(v_\nu\) to \(v_\mu\ot v_\omega\),
  \[
          VF_\alpha^{(\nu)}
          =
          \bigl(F_\alpha^{(\mu)}\ot I+I\ot F_\alpha^{(\omega)}\bigr)V\,.
  \]
  Recall the normalized PBW vectors from \eqref{eq:PBW-prebasis}: for
  \(\lambda=\mu,\nu,\omega\),
  \[
          \widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda}
          =
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}^{\prec}
          \frac{(F_\alpha^{(\lambda)})^{r_\alpha}}
               {\sqrt{r_\alpha!}
                (\Delta_\alpha^\lambda)^{r_\alpha/2}}
          v_\lambda\,.
  \]
  Expanding each root block gives the exact identity
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
          V\widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu}
          &=
          \sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
          \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
          \widetilde e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}
          \ot
          \widetilde e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega},\\
          \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
          &:=
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}
          \sqrt{\binom{r_\alpha}{a_\alpha}
          \bigl(s_{\alpha,n}\bigr)^{a_\alpha}
          \bigl(1-s_{\alpha,n}\bigr)^{r_\alpha-a_\alpha}}.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  This identity is exact: within each root block the two tensor-leg
  operators commute, and multiplying the blocks in the fixed PBW order
  produces the same PBW order in each tensor factor.
  Use the full weight-block orthonormalizations constructed in
  \cref{lem:PBW-normal-form}.  By \cref{lem:PBW-normal-form} and
  \eqref{eq:uniform-sector-gaps}, for every fixed \(L\),
  \[
          e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda}
          =
          \widetilde e_{\mathbf r}^{\lambda}+o_L(1),
          \qquad
          \lambda\in\{\mu,\nu,\omega\},\quad |\mathbf r|\le L\,.
  \]
  Hence, uniformly for \(|\mathbf r|\le L\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cartan-beamsplitter-cutoff}
          Ve_{\mathbf r}^{\nu}
          =
          \sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
          \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
          e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}
          \ot
          e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega}
          +o_L(1).
  \end{equation}

  \paragraph{Limiting diagonal weights.}
  Set
  \[
          \sigma_n:=\cC_{\mu,\nu}(\rho_{p,\mu}),
          \qquad
          \widehat\sigma_n:=J_\nu\sigma_n J_\nu^*\,.
  \]
  By the sector-channel formula \eqref{eq:sector-channel-formula},
  \[
          \sigma_n
          =
          \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
          V^*(\rho_{p,\mu}\ot I_{\cH_\omega})V.
  \]
  By \eqref{eq:Young-prob-and-Weyl-state},
  \(\rho_{p,\mu}=\pi_\mu(\rho_p)/s_\mu(p)\).  The vector
  \(e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}\) has weight
  \(\mu-\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\alpha\).  Therefore
  \[
          \rho_{p,\mu}e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu}
          =
          c_\mu
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{a_\alpha}
          e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu},
          \qquad
          c_\mu:=\frac{p^\mu}{s_\mu(p)}\,.
  \]
  Using \eqref{eq:sector-channel-formula} and \eqref{eq:cartan-beamsplitter-cutoff},
  with \(\dim\cH_\mu/\dim\cH_\nu=O_K(1)\) from the Weyl dimension formula and
  \eqref{eq:uniform-sector-gaps}, gives
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu},
          \sigma_n e_{\mathbf s}^{\nu}
          \right\rangle
          &=
          \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
          \sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r,\ \mathbf b\le\mathbf s}
          \beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}
          \beta_{\mathbf s,\mathbf b}^{(n)}
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu},
          \rho_{p,\mu}e_{\mathbf b}^{\mu}
          \right\rangle
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega},
          e_{\mathbf s-\mathbf b}^{\omega}
          \right\rangle
          +o_L(1)  \\
          &=
          \delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}
          \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
          c_\mu
          \sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
          \bigl(\beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}\bigr)^2
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{a_\alpha}
          +o_L(1).
    \end{aligned}
    \]
  Here
  \[
          \left\langle e_{\mathbf a}^{\mu},
          \rho_{p,\mu}e_{\mathbf b}^{\mu}\right\rangle
          =
          \delta_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}
          c_\mu\prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{a_\alpha},
          \qquad
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a}^{\omega},
          e_{\mathbf s-\mathbf b}^{\omega}
          \right\rangle
          =
          \delta_{\mathbf r-\mathbf a,\mathbf s-\mathbf b}.
  \]
  The sum over $\mathbf a\le\mathbf r$ factors over roots:
  \[
          \sum_{\mathbf a\le\mathbf r}
          \bigl(\beta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf a}^{(n)}\bigr)^2
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}q_\alpha^{a_\alpha}
          =
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}
          \sum_{a=0}^{r_\alpha}
          \binom{r_\alpha}{a}
          \bigl(s_{\alpha,n}q_\alpha\bigr)^a
          \bigl(1-s_{\alpha,n}\bigr)^{r_\alpha-a}.
  \]
  Hence
  \[
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu},
          \sigma_n e_{\mathbf s}^{\nu}
          \right\rangle
          =
          \delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}
          \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
          c_\mu
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}
          \left(
          1-s_{\alpha,n}
          +
          s_{\alpha,n}q_\alpha
          \right)^{r_\alpha}
          +o_L(1)
  \]
  for \(|\mathbf r|,|\mathbf s|\le L\).

  The uniform character normalization
  \eqref{eq:uniform-character-normalization} gives
  \[
          c_\mu
          =\frac{p^\mu}{s_\mu(p)}
          =\prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}(1-q_\alpha)+o(1).
  \]
  The Weyl dimension formula gives
  \[
          \frac{\dim\cH_\mu}{\dim\cH_\nu}
          =
          \prod_{\alpha=e_i-e_j\in\mathsf R_+}
          \frac{\Delta_\alpha^\mu+j-i}
               {\Delta_\alpha^\nu+j-i}
          =
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}
          \left(s_{\alpha,n}+O_K(n^{-1})\right)
          =
          \gamma^{-r_d}+o(1).
  \]
  Therefore, for \(|\mathbf r|,|\mathbf s|\le L\),
  \[
          \left\langle
          e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu},
          \sigma_n e_{\mathbf s}^{\nu}
          \right\rangle
          =
          \delta_{\mathbf r,\mathbf s}
          \prod_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}
          \bigl(1-q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}\bigr)
          \bigl(q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}\bigr)^{r_\alpha}
          +o_L(1),
  \]
  because \(1-q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}=(1-q_\alpha)/\gamma\).

  \paragraph{Removing the cutoff.}
  Let
  \[
          \tau^{(\gamma)}
          :=\bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}},
          \qquad
          P_{\le L}:=\sum_{|\mathbf r|\le L}|\mathbf r\rangle\langle\mathbf r|.
  \]
  Choose the isometries \(J_\nu\) by the construction in
  \cref{lem:sector-thermal}, with the thresholds chosen from the uniform gap
  bounds \eqref{eq:uniform-sector-gaps}.  Then, for each fixed \(L\) and all
  large \(n\),
  \[
          J_\nu e_{\mathbf r}^{\nu}=|\mathbf r\rangle
          \qquad(|\mathbf r|\le L).
  \]
  Since \(P_{\le L}\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}\) is finite-dimensional, the established
  entrywise convergence of $J_\nu\sigma_nJ_\nu^*$ to $\tau^{(\gamma)}$ on
  this subspace implies, for each fixed \(L\),
  \[
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left\|
          P_{\le L}\widehat\sigma_n P_{\le L}
          -
          P_{\le L}\tau^{(\gamma)}P_{\le L}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The thermal parameters \(q_\alpha\) and \(q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}\) range in a
  compact subset of \((0,1)\), so for every \(\eps>0\) one can choose \(L\)
  such that
  \[
          \sup_{p\in K}
          \Tr[(I-P_{\le L})\tau^{(\gamma)}]<\eps,
          \qquad
          \sup_{p\in K}
          \Tr\!\left[
          (I-P_{\le L})
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          \right]<\eps\,.
  \]
  For this \(L\), finite-cutoff convergence also gives
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left|
          \Tr[P_{\le L}(\widehat\sigma_n-\tau^{(\gamma)})]
          \right|
          &\le
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left\|
          P_{\le L}\widehat\sigma_n P_{\le L}
          -
          P_{\le L}\tau^{(\gamma)}P_{\le L}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0\,.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Applying the cutoff argument of \cref{lem:sector-thermal}, with the supremum
  carried through the estimates, gives
  \[
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left\|
          \widehat\sigma_n-\tau^{(\gamma)}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0\,.
  \]
  This is the uniform form of \eqref{eq:thermal-sector-limit}.  The same
  cutoff argument applied to the target sector gives
  \[
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left\|
          J_\nu\rho_{p,\nu}J_\nu^*
          -
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          \right\|_1
          \longrightarrow0\,.
  \]
  Root fidelity is invariant under the common isometry \(J_\nu\), and
  \cref{lem:fid-cont} gives
  \[
          \sup_{p,m,\mu,\nu}
          \left|
          B(\sigma_n,\rho_{p,\nu})
          -
          B\!\left(
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}},
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          \right)
          \right|
          \longrightarrow0\,.
  \]
  By the one-mode thermal fidelity formula \eqref{eq:thermal-root-fid},
  \[
          B\!\left(
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha^{(\gamma)}},
          \bigotimes_{\alpha\in \mathsf R_+}\tau_{q_\alpha}
          \right)
          =
          \Borb(\gamma,p).
  \]
  This proves the uniform form of \eqref{eq:sector-fidelity-limit}.
\end{proof}

\section{Young local limit and dithered dilation}\label{app:young-dither}
This appendix proves \cref{prop:young-dither}.  We retain the notation
$\mathsf C$ and $\mathsf L_N$ from \cref{sec:unknown}.  All label laws are
viewed on the full affine lattice $\mathsf L_N$; a Young law is extended by
zero away from partitions.  For
$\lambda\in\mathsf L_N$, define
\[
  \mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda-Np+\mathsf C}{\sqrt N},
  \qquad
  (\mathcal E_{N,p}R)(x)=\frac{N^{(d-1)/2}}{|\mathsf C|}R(\lambda)
  \quad(x\in\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)).
\]
The cells $\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)$ tile $\mathsf H$ up to null
boundaries, and $\mathcal E_{N,p}$ preserves both $\ell^1$-distance and
classical root fidelity.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:young-dither}]
\smallskip\noindent\emph{Dither geometry and admissibility.}
The cells $\widetilde\nu+\mathsf C$, $\widetilde\nu\in\mathsf L_{m_n}$, tile the
target affine hyperplane up to null boundaries.  Their intersections with
$\gamma_n(\mu+\mathsf C)$ therefore partition that dilated cell, whose volume is
$\gamma_n^{d-1}|\mathsf C|$.  Thus the values in \eqref{eq:dithered-kernel} sum to one.
If $\mathsf D_n(\widetilde\nu\mid\mu)>0$, then
$\gamma_n(\mu+u)=\widetilde\nu+v$ for some $u,v\in\mathsf C$, and hence
\[
  \widetilde\nu-\gamma_n\mu=\gamma_n u-v.
\]
Since $\gamma_n\to\gamma$, the constant
\[
  c_{\mathrm{dit}}=\left(1+\sup_n\gamma_n\right)
  \sup_{u\in\mathsf C}\|u\|_\infty
\]
is finite and gives the asserted support bound.

Compactness of $K$ gives $c_0>0$ such that, for all large $n$, every
$p\in K$ and $\mu\in\mathsf T_n(p)$ satisfy
\[
  \mu_d\ge c_0n,
  \qquad
  \mu_i-\mu_{i+1}\ge c_0n\quad(i<d).
\]
For every raw label in the support of $\mathsf D_n(\cdot\mid\mu)$,
\[
  (\widetilde\nu-\mu)_d
  \ge(\gamma_n-1)\mu_d-c_{\mathrm{dit}},
\]
and
\[
  (\widetilde\nu_i-\mu_i)
  -(\widetilde\nu_{i+1}-\mu_{i+1})
  \ge(\gamma_n-1)(\mu_i-\mu_{i+1})-2c_{\mathrm{dit}}.
\]
These quantities are positive for all large $n$, uniformly for $p\in K$,
because $\gamma_n\to\gamma>1$.  Thus $\widetilde\nu-\mu$ is a partition.
Consequently, redirection can occur only when
$\mu\notin\mathsf T_n(p)$, so \cref{lem:young-concentration} makes its
probability $o(1)$ uniformly on $K$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Uniform Young local limit.}
We first establish
\begin{equation}\label{eq:young-local-limit}
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|\mathcal E_{N,p}P_{N,p}-\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\right\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Let $M_{N,p}$ be the multinomial law on nonnegative integer
compositions of $N$, extended by zero to $\mathsf L_N$.  For $\lambda\vdash N$,
\[
  P_{N,p}(\lambda)=\dim\cS_\lambda\,s_\lambda(p),
  \qquad
  \dim\cS_\lambda
  =N!\frac{\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i)}
  {\prod_i(\lambda_i+d-i)!},
\]
and the Weyl character formula gives
\[
  s_\lambda(p)
  =\frac{\det[p_i^{\lambda_j+d-j}]_{i,j}}
  {\prod_{i<j}(p_i-p_j)}.
\]
Set $\mathsf W_{N,p}=\{\lambda\in\mathsf L_N:\|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{2/3}\}$.
Uniform strict separation on $K$ implies, for all large $N$,
\[
  \lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}
  \ge N(p_i-p_{i+1})-2N^{2/3}>0
  \qquad(\lambda\in \mathsf W_{N,p}),
\]
so every point of $\mathsf W_{N,p}$ in the support of $M_{N,p}$ is a partition.

On $\mathsf W_{N,p}\cap\operatorname{supp}M_{N,p}$, both
$(\lambda_i+d-i)/N$ and $\log p_i$ are uniformly strictly decreasing.
The strict rearrangement inequality, compactness of
$K$, and finiteness of the permutation group therefore give $c_1>0$ such
that, for every $\sigma\ne\mathrm{id}$,
\[
  \sum_i\frac{\lambda_i+d-i}{N}\log p_i
  -\sum_i\frac{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+d-\sigma(i)}{N}\log p_i
  \ge c_1.
\]
Thus every nonidentity term in the determinant is exponentially smaller
than the identity term, uniformly on
$\mathsf W_{N,p}\cap\operatorname{supp}M_{N,p}$, and
\[
  s_\lambda(p)
  =\frac{\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_i+d-i}}
  {\prod_{i<j}(p_i-p_j)}\bigl(1+O(e^{-c_1N})\bigr).
\]
Comparing with
$M_{N,p}(\lambda)=N!\prod_i p_i^{\lambda_i}/\prod_i\lambda_i!$ gives
\[
  \frac{P_{N,p}(\lambda)}{M_{N,p}(\lambda)}
  =\bigl(1+O(e^{-c_1N})\bigr)
  \prod_{i<j}\frac{\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i}{N(p_i-p_j)}
  \prod_i\frac{\lambda_i!(Np_i)^{d-i}}
  {(\lambda_i+d-i)!}.
\]
Every factor on the right tends uniformly to one on
$\mathsf W_{N,p}\cap\operatorname{supp}M_{N,p}$, and therefore
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}\sup_{\lambda\in \mathsf W_{N,p}\cap\operatorname{supp}M_{N,p}}
  \left|\frac{P_{N,p}(\lambda)}{M_{N,p}(\lambda)}-1\right|
  \longrightarrow0.
\]
The $P_{N,p}$-mass of $\mathsf W_{N,p}^c$ tends uniformly to zero by
\cref{lem:young-concentration}; the same holds for $M_{N,p}$ by
Hoeffding's inequality.  Since every central multinomial point is a
partition, splitting the $\ell^1$-sum over $\mathsf W_{N,p}$ and its complement
gives
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}\|P_{N,p}-M_{N,p}\|_{\ell^1}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Fix $\beta_0\in(1/2,2/3)$ and put
$x_\lambda=(\lambda-Np)/\sqrt N$.  On
$\|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{\beta_0}$, compactness of $K$ gives
$\lambda_i\asymp_K N$, and uniform Stirling expansion followed by a
third-order Taylor expansion gives
\[
  \log M_{N,p}(\lambda)
  =-\frac{d-1}{2}\log(2\pi N)-\frac12\sum_i\log p_i
  -\frac12\sum_i\frac{x_{\lambda,i}^2}{p_i}
  +O_K(N^{3\beta_0-2}).
\]
For $x\in\mathsf H$,
$\langle x,\Sigma_p^{-1}x\rangle=\sum_i x_i^2/p_i$.  Moreover, for
$b_i=e_i-e_d$, $\det[\langle b_i,b_j\rangle]=d$, so
$|\mathsf C|=\sqrt d$.  A principal cofactor of $\Sigma_p$ equals
$\prod_i p_i$, while $\ker\Sigma_p=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$;
hence $\det_{\mathsf H}\Sigma_p=d\prod_i p_i$.  Since
$3\beta_0-2<0$, the expansion therefore yields, uniformly on the window,
\[
  \frac{N^{(d-1)/2}}{|\mathsf C|}M_{N,p}(\lambda)
  =\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x_\lambda)(1+o(1)).
\]
Here $\Sigma_p$ is the multinomial covariance restricted to $\mathsf H$.
Let
\[
  V_{N,p}=\{\lambda\in\mathsf L_N:
  \|\lambda-Np\|_\infty\le N^{\beta_0}\},
  \qquad
  U_{N,p}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in V_{N,p}}\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda).
\]
Hoeffding's inequality and the uniform eigenvalue bounds for $\Sigma_p$ give
constants $c_K,C_K>0$ such that
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}\left[
  M_{N,p}(V_{N,p}^c)
  +\int_{U_{N,p}^c}\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\,\dd x
  \right]
  \le C_Ke^{-c_KN^{2\beta_0-1}}.
\]
On the window, the cells have diameter $O(N^{-1/2})$, while
$\|\nabla\log\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\|=O(1+\|x\|)$ uniformly on $K$.
Consequently, the relative oscillation of the Gaussian density across a
cell is
\[
  \exp\bigl(O(N^{-1/2}+N^{\beta_0-1})\bigr)-1=o(1).
\]
The tail bound, the local approximation, and this oscillation estimate give
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \|\mathcal E_{N,p}M_{N,p}-\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\|_1
  \le{}&M_{N,p}(V_{N,p}^c)
  +\int_{U_{N,p}^c}\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)\,\dd x\\
  &+\sup_{\substack{\lambda\in V_{N,p}\\
                     x\in\mathsf C_{N,p}(\lambda)}}
  \left|
  \frac{N^{(d-1)/2}M_{N,p}(\lambda)}
       {|\mathsf C|\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(x)}-1
  \right|=o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly for $p\in K$.  Thus
$\sup_{p\in K}\|\mathcal E_{N,p}M_{N,p}
-\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\|_1\to0$,
and combining this with
$\sup_{p\in K}\|P_{N,p}-M_{N,p}\|_{\ell^1}\to0$ proves
\eqref{eq:young-local-limit}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Dithered label limit.}
Let $\widetilde Q_{n,p}=P_{n,p}\mathsf D_n$ be the raw output-label law before fallback.  For a
density $f$ on $\mathsf H$, set
\[
  (\operatorname{dil}_a f)(y)=a^{-(d-1)/2}f(y/\sqrt a),
\]
and let $\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}$ be the cell-averaging operator on the target cells:
\[
  (\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}f)(x)
  =\frac{m_n^{(d-1)/2}}{|\mathsf C|}
  \int_{\mathsf C_{m_n,p}(\nu)}f(y)\,\dd y,
  \qquad x\in\mathsf C_{m_n,p}(\nu).
\]
Multiplication by $\gamma_n$ in the unscaled affine hyperplane becomes
multiplication by $\sqrt{\gamma_n}$ in central-limit coordinates, and
$\mathsf D_n$ records the target cell hit by the dilated point.  Hence
\[
  \mathcal E_{m_n,p}\widetilde Q_{n,p}
  =\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}\operatorname{dil}_{\gamma_n}
  \bigl(\mathcal E_{n,p}P_{n,p}\bigr).
\]
The operator $\operatorname{Av}_{m_n,p}$ is an $L^1$-contraction.  By
\eqref{eq:young-local-limit} and the $L^1$-isometry of
$\operatorname{dil}_{\gamma_n}$,
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|
  \operatorname{dil}_{\gamma_n}(\mathcal E_{n,p}P_{n,p})
  -\varphi_{0,\gamma_n \Sigma_p}
  \right\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
The compact family $\varphi_{0,\gamma_n \Sigma_p}$ converges uniformly in
$L^1$ to $\varphi_{0,\gamma \Sigma_p}$.  Since the target cells have diameter
$O(m_n^{-1/2})$ and the same Gaussian family is uniformly
translation-continuous in $L^1$, cell averaging changes the limit by
$o(1)$.  Thus
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|\mathcal E_{m_n,p}\widetilde Q_{n,p}
  -\varphi_{0,\gamma \Sigma_p}\right\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
Applying \eqref{eq:young-local-limit} with $N=m_n$ also gives
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}
  \left\|\mathcal E_{m_n,p}P_{m_n,p}
  -\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}\right\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]

If $\varepsilon_{n,p}$ is the fallback probability at spectrum $p$, then
\[
  \sup_{p\in K}\|Q_{n,p}-\widetilde Q_{n,p}\|_{\ell^1}
  \le2\sup_{p\in K}\varepsilon_{n,p}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The inequalities
\[
  |B(Q,P)-B(Q',P)|\le\|Q-Q'\|_{\ell^1}^{1/2},
  \qquad
  |B(f,g)-B(f',g')|
  \le\|f-f'\|_1^{1/2}+\|g-g'\|_1^{1/2}
\]
follow from $\|\sqrt f-\sqrt{f'}\|_2^2\le\|f-f'\|_1$.  The convergences
$\mathcal E_{m_n,p}\widetilde Q_{n,p}\to\varphi_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}$ and
$\mathcal E_{m_n,p}P_{m_n,p}\to\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}$ in $L^1$, the estimate
$\|Q_{n,p}-\widetilde Q_{n,p}\|_{\ell^1}\to0$, and preservation of root
fidelity by the cell embedding imply, uniformly for $p\in K$,
\[
  B(Q_{n,p},P_{m_n,p})
  \longrightarrow
  B(\mathsf N_{0,\gamma \Sigma_p},\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p})
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted Gaussian converses}\label{app:gaussian-converse}

This appendix proves the upper bounds in
\cref{thm:gaussian-flat-prior,thm:hybrid-gaussian}.  We first average over
large parameter cubes, but apply a compact product witness before taking any
limit.  The proof therefore uses only a completely positive map on oscillator
trace class and, in the hybrid case, one scalar convolution kernel.

\subsection{Weighted fidelity and compact limits}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted root-fidelity bound]\label{lem:weighted-fidelity}
Let $R,T$ be positive trace-class operators, and let $W$ be bounded,
positive, and injective.  Define
\[
  \Tr(TW^{-1})
  :=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
  \Tr\bigl(T(W+\varepsilon I)^{-1}\bigr)\in[0,\infty].
\]
If this quantity is finite, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-quantum-fidelity}
  B(R,T)^2\le \Tr(RW)\,\Tr(TW^{-1}).
\end{equation}

More generally, let
$R\in L^1(\mathsf H;\cT_1(\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}))$ be positive, let $g$ be a
probability density on $\mathsf H$, and let $\chi>0$ be measurable.  Whenever
the three factors on the right are finite,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-hybrid-fidelity}
  B(R,gT)^2
  \le
  \left(\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)\Tr[R(y)W] \,\dd y\right)
  \left(\int_{\mathsf H}\frac{g(y)}{\chi(y)}\,\dd y\right)
  \Tr(TW^{-1}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\varepsilon>0$, put $W_\varepsilon=W+\varepsilon I$.  The Schatten
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
  B(R,T)
  =\|R^{1/2}T^{1/2}\|_1
  \le
  \|R^{1/2}W_\varepsilon^{1/2}\|_2
  \|W_\varepsilon^{-1/2}T^{1/2}\|_2.
\]
Squaring and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves
\eqref{eq:weighted-quantum-fidelity} by the definition of $\Tr(TW^{-1})$
and monotone convergence.

By the direct-integral definition of classical--quantum fidelity,
\[
  B(R,gT)=\int_{\mathsf H}\sqrt{g(y)}\,B(R(y),T)\,\dd y.
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:weighted-quantum-fidelity} pointwise and then use
Cauchy--Schwarz in $y$ after writing
\[
  \sqrt{g(y)\Tr[R(y)W]}
  =\sqrt{\chi(y)\Tr[R(y)W]}
   \sqrt{\frac{g(y)}{\chi(y)}}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:weighted-hybrid-fidelity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-observable limits of completely positive maps]
\label{lem:compact-cp-limit}
Let $\cH_0,\cH_1$ be separable Hilbert spaces and let
$\Gamma_j:\cT_1(\cH_0)\to\cT_1(\cH_1)$ be completely positive maps whose
trace-norm operator norms are uniformly bounded.  There are a subsequence and
a completely positive map $\Gamma:\cT_1(\cH_0)\to\cT_1(\cH_1)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-map-convergence}
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma(X)K]
\end{equation}
for every $X\in\cT_1(\cH_0)$ and every compact operator
$K\in\mathcal K(\cH_1)$.  If
$\Tr\Gamma_j(X)\le c\Tr X$ for all $X\ge0$, then
$\Tr\Gamma(X)\le c\Tr X$.

Suppose also that $U_g$ and $V_g$ are unitary representations on
$\cH_0$ and $\cH_1$ and, for every fixed $g$ and $X$,
\[
  \|\Gamma_j(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
Then $\Gamma(U_gXU_g^*)=V_g\Gamma(X)V_g^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restrict the adjoints $\Gamma_j^*$ to $\mathcal K(\cH_1)$ and choose a
countable norm-dense $\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)$-linear $*$-subspace there.
Banach--Alaoglu and a diagonal argument give a subsequence converging
pointwise weak-* on this subspace.  The uniform bounds extend the limit and
the convergence to a bounded complex-linear map
$\Gamma^*:\mathcal K(\cH_1)\to\mathcal B(\cH_0)$.  At every matrix level the
positive cone is weak-* closed, so $\Gamma^*$ is completely positive.  Define
$\Gamma(X)\in\cT_1(\cH_1)=\mathcal K(\cH_1)^*$ by
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma(X)K]=\Tr[X\Gamma^*(K)].
\]
This defines a completely positive map and gives
\eqref{eq:compact-map-convergence}.

Let $P_N\uparrow I$ be finite-rank projections on $\cH_1$.  For $X\ge0$,
\[
  \Tr\Gamma(X)
  =\sup_N\Tr[\Gamma(X)P_N]
  =\sup_N\lim_j\Tr[\Gamma_j(X)P_N]
  \le c\Tr X.
\]
For the covariance assertion, pair the assumed trace-norm defect with a
compact $K$, pass to the limit, and use that $V_g^*KV_g$ is compact.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The multimode least-noise moment}

For $s\ge1$, choose $q_1,\ldots,q_s\in(0,1)$ and set
\[
  q_\ell^{(\gamma)}=1-\frac{1-q_\ell}{\gamma},
  \qquad
  \tau=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^s\tau_{q_\ell},
  \qquad
  \tau^{(\gamma)}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^s\tau_{q_\ell^{(\gamma)}}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Least-noise moment]
\label{prop:least-noise-moment}
Let $\Gamma$ be a completely positive trace-nonincreasing map on the
$s$-mode trace class.  Assume that it is displacement covariant with gain
$\sqrt\gamma$:
\[
  \Gamma(D(z)XD(z)^*)
  =D(\sqrt\gamma\,z)\Gamma(X)D(\sqrt\gamma\,z)^*
  \qquad(z\in\C^s).
\]
If
\[
  W(\widehat{\mathbf N})
  =\prod_{\ell=1}^s w_\ell(\widehat N_\ell),
\]
where every $w_\ell$ is bounded, nonnegative, and decreasing on
$\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:least-noise-moment}
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W(\widehat{\mathbf N})]
  \le
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)]\,
  \Tr[\tau^{(\gamma)} W(\widehat{\mathbf N})].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_s)\), define the independent
phase rotation
\[
  R_\theta=\exp\left(\mathrm i\sum_{\ell=1}^s
  \theta_\ell\widehat N_\ell\right).
\]
Replace \(\Gamma\) by
\[
  \Gamma^{\mathrm{ph}}(X)
  =\frac1{(2\pi)^s}\int_{[0,2\pi]^s}
    R_{-\theta}\Gamma(R_\theta X R_\theta^*)R_{-\theta}^*\,\dd\theta.
\]
This twirl preserves displacement covariance and leaves both traces in
\eqref{eq:least-noise-moment} unchanged.  We may therefore assume that $\Gamma$ is phase covariant in
every mode.

For one mode, the positive functional $X\mapsto\Tr\Gamma(X)$ has the form
$\Tr(AX)$ for a bounded $0\le A\le I$.  Displacement covariance implies that
$A$ commutes with every Weyl displacement.  Irreducibility gives $A=cI$,
where $0\le c\le1$.  If $c=0$, both sides of
\eqref{eq:least-noise-moment} vanish.  If $c>0$, then $c^{-1}\Gamma$ is a
phase-insensitive, displacement-covariant channel of gain
$\sqrt\gamma$.  The Weyl-operator characterization of covariant amplifiers in
\cite{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning}, with
$(\sqrt2,1)$ replaced by $(\sqrt\gamma,\sqrt{\gamma-1})$, shows that every
such channel satisfies
\[
  (c^{-1}\Gamma)^*(D(\xi))
  =\Tr[\sigma D(-\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,\bar\xi)]D(\sqrt\gamma\,\xi)
\]
for an idler state $\sigma$; phase covariance makes $\sigma$ diagonal in the
number basis.  Gu\c{t}\u{a} and Matsumoto's stochastic-order argument
\cite[Definition~2.2 and Lemma~2.3]{GutaMatsumoto2006MixedGaussianCloning},
with the arbitrary-gain extension stated in their Sec.~III,\footnote{For gain
$\sqrt\gamma$, their reduction uses
$\cosh\widetilde t=\sqrt\gamma\cosh t$ and
$\sinh^2\widetilde t=\gamma\sinh^2t+\gamma-1$; the subsequent number-state
calculation is unchanged.} shows that the output photon-number law on
$\tau_q$ stochastically dominates the geometric law of
$\tau_{q^{(\gamma)}}$.  Hence every bounded decreasing photon-number function
has no larger expectation for the former law.  Multiplying by $c$ proves
\eqref{eq:least-noise-moment} for $s=1$.

For $s>1$, if some $w_\ell$ vanishes identically, both sides of
\eqref{eq:least-noise-moment} vanish.  Otherwise divide each weight by its
positive supremum, so that $0\le w_\ell\le1$.  Put
\[
  W_{>1}=\prod_{\ell=2}^s w_\ell(\widehat N_\ell),
  \qquad
  \tau_{>1}=\bigotimes_{\ell=2}^s\tau_{q_\ell},
\]
and define the one-mode completely positive trace-nonincreasing map
\[
  \Gamma_1(X)
  =\Tr_{2,\ldots,s}\!\left[
    (I\ot W_{>1}^{1/2})
    \Gamma(X\ot\tau_{>1})
    (I\ot W_{>1}^{1/2})
  \right].
\]
It inherits displacement and phase covariance in the first mode.  The
one-mode result therefore gives
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W]
  \le
  \Tr[\tau_{q_1^{(\gamma)}}w_1(\widehat N_1)]
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W_{>1}].
\]
Repeating the same argument for modes $2,\ldots,s$ proves
\eqref{eq:least-noise-moment}.
\end{proof}

For the Gaussian families in the paper, write
\[
  \tau:=\Phi_0,
  \qquad
  \tau^{(\gamma)}:=\bigotimes_{i<j}\tau_{q_{ij}^{(\gamma)}}.
\]
In their joint number basis, let
\[
  \tau=\sum_{\mathbf k}t_{\mathbf k}\proj{\mathbf k},
  \qquad
  \tau^{(\gamma)}=\sum_{\mathbf k}t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}\proj{\mathbf k},
\]
and define
\[
  W_\gamma
  =\sum_{\mathbf k}
  \sqrt{\frac{t_{\mathbf k}}{t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}}
  \proj{\mathbf k}.
\]
The eigenvalues of $W_\gamma$ are positive, bounded, and form a product of
decreasing geometric sequences; in particular, $W_\gamma$ is compact and
injective.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantum-witness-identities}
  \Tr(\tau^{(\gamma)} W_\gamma)
  =\Tr(\tau W_\gamma^{-1})
  =\sum_{\mathbf k}\sqrt{t_{\mathbf k}t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}
  =\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Orbital flat-prior bound}

\begin{proposition}[Orbital flat-prior upper bound]
\label{prop:orbital-flat-prior-upper}
With the notation of \cref{thm:gaussian-flat-prior},
\[
  \limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda
  \int_{\mathsf Z_L}B(\Lambda(\Phi_z),\Psi_z)
  \frac{\dd z}{|\mathsf Z_L|}
  \le\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a channel $\Lambda$, define its finite F\o lner average
\[
  \overline\Lambda_L
  =\frac1{|\mathsf Z_L|}\int_{\mathsf Z_L}
  \operatorname{Ad}_{D(-\sqrt\gamma z)}\circ\Lambda\circ
  \operatorname{Ad}_{D(z)}\,\dd z.
\]
Joint concavity and displacement covariance of the Gaussian families give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbital-folner-concavity}
  \int_{\mathsf Z_L}B(\Lambda(\Phi_z),\Psi_z)
  \frac{\dd z}{|\mathsf Z_L|}
  \le B(\overline\Lambda_L(\tau),\tau).
\end{equation}
Choose $L_j\to\infty$ and channels approaching the left-hand limsup, and set
$\Gamma_j=\overline\Lambda_{L_j}$.  Translation of the averaging cube gives,
for every fixed $w\in\C^{r_d}$,
\[
  \|\Gamma_j(D(w)XD(w)^*)
  -D(\sqrt\gamma w)\Gamma_j(X)D(\sqrt\gamma w)^*\|_1
  \le
  \frac{|(\mathsf Z_{L_j}+w)\triangle\mathsf Z_{L_j}|}
       {|\mathsf Z_{L_j}|}\,\|X\|_1.
\]
By \cref{lem:compact-cp-limit}, after passing to a subsequence there is a
completely positive trace-nonincreasing, exactly displacement-covariant map
$\Gamma$ such that
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma(X)K]
\]
for every trace-class $X$ and compact $K$.

Apply \cref{lem:weighted-fidelity} with $W=W_\gamma$.  Since $W_\gamma$ is
compact, \cref{prop:least-noise-moment,eq:quantum-witness-identities} yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \limsup_j B(\Gamma_j(\tau),\tau)^2
  &\le
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)W_\gamma]\,
  \Tr(\tau W_\gamma^{-1})\\
  &\le
  \Tr[\Gamma(\tau)]\,
  \Tr(\tau^{(\gamma)} W_\gamma)\,
  \Borb(\gamma,p)\\
  &\le \Borb(\gamma,p)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:orbital-folner-concavity}, this proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hybrid flat-prior bound}

\begin{lemma}[Translation-covariant scalar kernels]\label{lem:scalar-convolution}
Let $\mathcal R:L^1(\mathsf H)\to C_0(\mathsf H)^*$ be a positive contraction and
suppose
\[
  \mathcal R(u(\,\cdot-h))(E)=\mathcal R(u)(E-\sqrt\gamma h)
  \qquad(h\in\mathsf H)
\]
for every Borel set $E$.
Then there is a finite positive measure $\mu$, with $\mu(\mathsf H)\le1$,
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-convolution}
  \mathcal R(u)(E)
  =\int_{\mathsf H}u(x)\,\mu(E-\sqrt\gamma x)\,\dd x
\end{equation}
for every $u\in L^1(\mathsf H)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a nonnegative approximate identity $u_j\in C_c(\mathsf H)$ with
$\int u_j=1$.  The measures $\mathcal R(u_j)$ have norm at most one.  Since
$C_0(\mathsf H)$ is separable, a subsequence converges weak-* to a positive
measure $\mu$ with $\mu(\mathsf H)\le1$.  For $u\in L^1(\mathsf H)$, $u*u_j\to u$ in $L^1$,
while covariance and bounded linearity give, as finite measures,
\[
  \mathcal R(u*u_j)(E)
  =\int_{\mathsf H}u(x)\,\mathcal R(u_j)(E-\sqrt\gamma x)\,\dd x.
\]
Pairing with an arbitrary function in $C_0(\mathsf H)$ and using dominated
convergence proves \eqref{eq:scalar-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hybrid flat-prior upper bound]
\label{prop:hybrid-flat-prior-upper}
With the notation of \cref{thm:hybrid-gaussian},
\[
  \limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_\Lambda
  \frac1{|\Omega_L|}\int_{\Omega_L}
  B(\Lambda(\Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{in}}),
    \Theta_{h,z}^{\mathrm{tar}})\,\dd h\,\dd z
  \le\Buniv(\gamma,p).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
  f_1=\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p},
  \qquad
  f_\gamma=\varphi_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}.
\]
For a trace-class operator $X$, write $f_1X$ for the density
$x\mapsto f_1(x)X$.  The classical witness
\[
  \chi(y)=\sqrt{\frac{f_1(y)}{f_\gamma(y)}}
  =\gamma^{(d-1)/4}
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}
  \langle y,\Sigma_p^{-1}y\rangle\right]
\]
is bounded, strictly positive, and belongs to $C_0(\mathsf H)$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-witness-identity}
  \int_{\mathsf H}\frac{f_1(y)}{\chi(y)}\,\dd y
  =\int_{\mathsf H}\sqrt{f_1(y)f_\gamma(y)}\,\dd y
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\end{equation}

For $\xi=(h,z)$, define
\[
  (\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}R)(x)
  =D(z)R(x-h)D(z)^*,
  \qquad
  (\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}R)(y)
  =D(\sqrt\gamma z)R(y-\sqrt\gamma h)D(\sqrt\gamma z)^*.
\]
For a hybrid channel $\Lambda$, set
\[
  \overline\Lambda_L
  =\frac1{|\Omega_L|}\int_{\Omega_L}
  \mathcal U_{-\xi}^{\mathrm{out}}\circ\Lambda\circ
  \mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}\,\dd\xi.
\]
Since
$\Theta_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}(f_1\tau)$ and
$\Theta_\xi^{\mathrm{tar}}=\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}(f_1\tau)$,
joint concavity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hybrid-folner-concavity}
  \frac1{|\Omega_L|}\int_{\Omega_L}
  B(\Lambda(\Theta_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}),
    \Theta_\xi^{\mathrm{tar}})\,\dd\xi
  \le B(\overline\Lambda_L(f_1\tau),f_1\tau).
\end{equation}

Choose $L_j\to\infty$ and channels approaching the left-hand limsup, and
write $\Lambda_j=\overline\Lambda_{L_j}$.  The compact quantum witness
$W_\gamma$ controls the oscillator noise, while $\chi$ controls the classical
translation noise.  Put
\[
  R_j=\Lambda_j(f_1\tau),
  \qquad
  M_j=\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)\Tr[R_j(y)W_\gamma]\,\dd y.
\]
By
\cref{lem:weighted-fidelity,eq:classical-witness-identity,eq:quantum-witness-identities},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hybrid-fidelity-to-moment}
  B(R_j,f_1\tau)^2
  \le M_j\,\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}

Define
\[
  \Gamma_j^\chi(X)
  =\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)[\Lambda_j(f_1X)](y)\,\dd y.
\]
These maps are completely positive and satisfy
$\Tr\Gamma_j^\chi(X)\le\|\chi\|_\infty\Tr X$ for $X\ge0$.  Translation of the
averaging cube gives, for every fixed $\xi$ and every hybrid trace-class
density $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hybrid-approx-covariance}
  \|\Lambda_j(\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{in}}R)
  -\mathcal U_\xi^{\mathrm{out}}\Lambda_j(R)\|_1
  \le
  \frac{|(\Omega_{L_j}+\xi)\triangle\Omega_{L_j}|}
       {|\Omega_{L_j}|}\,\|R\|_1.
\end{equation}
Taking $h=0$ gives, for every fixed $w\in\C^{r_d}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\|\Gamma_j^\chi(D(w)XD(w)^*)
    -D(\sqrt\gamma w)\Gamma_j^\chi(X)D(\sqrt\gamma w)^*\|_1\\
  &\qquad\le
  \|\chi\|_\infty
  \frac{|(\Omega_{L_j}+(0,w))\triangle\Omega_{L_j}|}
       {|\Omega_{L_j}|}\,\|X\|_1
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
By \cref{lem:compact-cp-limit}, after passing
to a subsequence there is a completely positive, exactly
displacement-covariant map $\Gamma^\chi$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-map-limit}
  \Tr[\Gamma_j^\chi(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(X)K]
\end{equation}
for every trace-class $X$ and compact $K$.  In particular, since
$W_\gamma$ is compact,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-limit}
  M_j=\Tr[\Gamma_j^\chi(\tau)W_\gamma]
  \longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau)W_\gamma].
\end{equation}

For $u\in L^1(\mathsf H)$, let $\mathcal R_j(u)$ be the classical trace
measure of $\Lambda_j(u\tau)$:
\[
  \mathcal R_j(u)(E)=\int_E\Tr[\Lambda_j(u\tau)(y)]\,\dd y.
\]
The maps $\mathcal R_j$ are positive contractions from $L^1(\mathsf H)$ to
the finite Radon measures.  Passing to a further subsequence, a diagonal extraction on
countable dense subsets of $L^1(\mathsf H)$ and $C_0(\mathsf H)$ makes all
corresponding pairings converge.  The contraction bound then extends the
limit to a positive contraction $\mathcal R$ with
$\mathcal R_j(u)\to\mathcal R(u)$ weak-* for every
$u\in L^1(\mathsf H)$.  Taking $z=0$ in
\eqref{eq:hybrid-approx-covariance}, pairing with functions in
$C_0(\mathsf H)$, and passing to the limit gives
\[
  \mathcal R(u(\,\cdot-h))(E)
  =\mathcal R(u)(E-\sqrt\gamma h)
  \qquad(h\in\mathsf H)
\]
for every Borel set $E$.  Hence \cref{lem:scalar-convolution} applies; let
$\mu$ be the resulting convolution measure.

Let $P_N\uparrow I$ be finite-rank projections on $\cF_{\mathrm{orb}}$.
Using \eqref{eq:weighted-map-limit}, positivity, the weak-* convergence of
$\mathcal R_j(f_1)$, and $\chi\in C_0(\mathsf H)$ gives
\begin{align*}
  \Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau)
  &=\sup_N\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau)P_N]
    \le\liminf_j\Tr\Gamma_j^\chi(\tau)\\
  &=\lim_j\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)\,\mathcal R_j(f_1)(\dd y)
    =\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)\,\mathcal R(f_1)(\dd y).
\end{align*}
By \eqref{eq:scalar-convolution},
\[
  \int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)\,\mathcal R(f_1)(\dd y)
  =\int_{\mathsf H}c_\gamma(r)\,\mu(\dd r),
  \qquad
  c_\gamma(r)=\int_{\mathsf H}\chi(y)f_\gamma(y-r)\,\dd y.
\]
Completing the square on $\mathsf H$ gives
\[
  c_\gamma(r)
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d)
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma(\gamma+1)}
  \langle r,\Sigma_p^{-1}r\rangle\right]
  \le\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\]
Since $\mu(\mathsf H)\le1$,
\[
  \Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau)\le\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\]
The trace functional of $\Gamma^\chi$ is represented by a bounded positive
operator commuting with every multimode Weyl displacement.  Irreducibility
of the multimode Weyl representation therefore gives
\[
  \Tr\Gamma^\chi(X)=c\Tr X,
  \qquad
  c=\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau)\le\Bcl(\gamma,d)\le1.
\]
Thus $\Gamma^\chi$ is trace nonincreasing.  Applying
\cref{prop:least-noise-moment,eq:quantum-witness-identities} to
\eqref{eq:moment-limit},
\[
  \lim_j M_j
  =\Tr[\Gamma^\chi(\tau)W_\gamma]
  \le\Tr\Gamma^\chi(\tau)\,\Tr(\tau^{(\gamma)} W_\gamma)
  \le\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:hybrid-fidelity-to-moment}, this gives
\[
  \limsup_j B(R_j,f_1\tau)
  \le\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\]
Combining this estimate with \eqref{eq:hybrid-folner-concavity} proves the
proposition.
\end{proof}
