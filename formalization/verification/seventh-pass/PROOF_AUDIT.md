# Mathematical dependency audit

Reference: `reference/cloning.tex`, supplied manuscript, 4,820 lines.

This document records an independent reading of the manuscript to identify what a Lean formalization must actually establish. It is not a certificate that the entire manuscript has been formalized. The companion Lean modules and their build output determine which statements are machine checked. A theorem proved from an explicit mathematical hypothesis does **not** establish that hypothesis for the manuscript's quantum channels.

## Scope and result of this review

The detailed review covered Appendix A (the exact-weight PBW calculation, lines 2564–3076), Appendix C (the Gaussian converse, lines 3327–3882), Appendix D (PCT comparison, lines 3884–4480), the LAN transfer and minimax completion (lines 1526–1795), and the projector argument (lines 1797–2233). Appendix B and the Young concentration estimate were also checked for their connections to these arguments.

**No definite mathematical error was established in the reviewed arguments.** This is an informal audit, not a claim that all external inputs or every estimate have been verified. The original constructive LAN and bosonic dilation references were located and their relevant statements checked during the fifth pass, but their proofs were not independently reconstructed in Lean. The substantial unformalized dependencies below should not be presented as false statements or as proof gaps solely because no Lean implementation has been supplied.

## Appendix A: exact-weight PBW argument

Relevant labels: `lem:PBW-fixed-height` (line 2619), `lem:uniform-character-normalization` (2752), `eq:cartan-PBW-exact` (2854), `eq:output-fixed-height-convergence` (2967), and `eq:uniform-thermal-height-tail` (3023).

The manuscript works with **complete exact-weight spaces**, not arbitrary finite sets of PBW monomials. This matters in two places:

1. The raising operator on a complete height cutoff is the adjoint of the lowering operator from the preceding cutoff. This supports the recurrence bounding its restricted operator norm. A formal proof must retain the domains and codomains of these restrictions; the operators do not preserve one fixed cutoff.
2. Orthonormalization is performed separately inside each weight space. Since the Gibbs-type representation state is scalar on a weight space, it remains diagonal in the resulting basis, even when the weight has multiplicity greater than one.

The normalized cross-root commutators are small on each fixed cutoff. Reordering a bounded-length word then gives the asymptotic Gram matrix. Formalizing this requires a finite-word induction with explicit bounds, finite-dimensional matrix functional calculus for `G^(-1/2)`, and the PBW spanning theorem for the concrete highest-weight representation. These inputs cannot be replaced by merely declaring an orthonormal Fock basis.

The Cartan coproduct expansion is exact before orthonormalization and becomes asymptotically binomial afterwards. All output comparisons use a frame depending on the output label `ν`, independent of the input label `μ`. That independence is what permits arbitrary mixtures over `μ`.

The final tail argument needs only tightness of the **target** state: the complementary fidelity is bounded by the square root of its target mass because the output trace is at most one. A separate uniform trace-norm convergence theorem for the entire PRV/Cartan channel is not used. The order of limits is fixed height first, then height tending to infinity. A Lean statement must preserve that quantifier order.

## Appendix C: Gaussian converse

Relevant labels: `lem:weighted-fidelity` (3346), `lem:compact-cp-limit` (3398), `lem:covariant-amplifier-representation` (3483), `prop:least-noise-moment` (3529), and `prop:flat-prior-converse` (3659).

### Compact-observable limit

The limit in `lem:compact-cp-limit` is against compact output operators. The construction uses separability, a diagonal extraction, the duality between compact operators and trace-class operators, and matrix-level positivity. It permits loss of trace. The manuscript correctly derives only the trace bound `Tr Γ(X) ≤ c Tr X`, not equality.

The proof does not require a diagonal argument over all displacements: the extracted subsequence converges for **every** trace-class input and compact observable; the covariance identity can subsequently be passed to the limit for each fixed displacement. A formal implementation should likewise avoid imposing countability on the displacement group.

A formal theorem here requires a genuine infinite-dimensional trace-class API, the relevant predual/dual identifications, and compatibility of complete positivity with these limits. Finite-dimensional matrix compactness alone does not establish this result.

### Least-noise moment

The amplifier representation allows a correlated, nondiagonal idler state. In the output **number probabilities**, tracing out the idler eliminates its off-diagonal number matrix elements. The remaining conditional distribution is a product of negative-binomial laws, mixed by the possibly correlated idler occupation law. Coupling each coordinate to the first geometric summand proves the monotone moment inequality.

A scalar product-monotonicity lemma formalizes the last inequality only. The quantum-to-probability reduction additionally needs Weyl irreducibility, a dilation theorem with nondegenerate symplectic defect, the two-mode-squeezer expansion, convergence of the infinite series, and the negative-binomial convolution identity.

### Flat-prior averaging

The Følner estimate gives approximate covariance uniformly in the chosen channel. Weighting the classical output by `χ` produces quantum maps with a sharper limiting trace bound `c_k`, obtained by the Gaussian translation identity and an approximate identity on the classical input. Compactness of the witness `W_γ` then passes its moment through the compact-observable limit. The moment inequality and weighted fidelity yield the desired bound.

The witness inverse is unbounded in general. The manuscript defines its trace through regularization by `(W + εI)^(-1)` and requires finiteness. A formal weighted-fidelity lemma with a bounded inverse would need an additional limiting argument before it applies here.

The remaining infrastructure includes Bochner integration of channels, matrix-amplified positivity under integration, strong continuity of translations/Weyl conjugations, Gaussian integration, and the polytope symmetric-difference estimate. The proof does not assume that arbitrary competing channels are Gaussian, product, or exactly covariant.

## Appendix D: PCT comparison

Relevant labels: `lem:Werner-Fock` (3933), `eq:pct-product-mixture` (4038), `eq:LAN-coordinate-map` (1538), `eq:pct-lan-output` (4161), `prop:grassmann-pct-comparison` (4241), and `prop:pct-projector-small-error` (4381).

The occupation law converges pointwise to a normalized product of geometric laws; discrete Scheffé gives the required trace-norm convergence. The coherent/product approximation is then integrated using a trace-distance bound of two, so no estimate uniform over unbounded Gaussian tangent vectors is necessary.

For the full-rank result, each fixed tangent vector is first expressed in **exact** LAN coordinates via the inverse function theorem. The coordinates approach the linearized values. This avoids the generally unjustified shortcut of tensoring a one-copy `O(1/L)` trace-distance approximation `L` times.

The covariance calculation gives the classical factor `(2γ−1)Σ_p` and mean photon number `((γ−1)p_i + γp_j)/(p_i−p_j)`. The strict scalar comparison relies on `0 < q < 1` and `γ > 1`; both are essential. These algebraic parts are suitable self-contained Lean targets, but their verification does not prove that the actual PCT output has that limit.

Both forward and reverse LAN approximations are used to identify the limiting fidelity. A forward approximation alone would supply only one inequality.

For projector PCT, the proof uses weak convergence of count distributions only to obtain an **upper bound** on their Hellinger affinity through finite partitions. It does not claim that weak convergence implies convergence of affinity. The bound is therefore compatible with the discreteness of the finite count laws.

For the small-error bound, the manuscript computes the purity of the explicit mixture approximation and transfers only the resulting **fidelity bound** to the true output. It does not transfer dimension-rescaled purity through trace-norm convergence, which would be invalid without further estimates. The condition `δ < 1/(2r)` supplies the stated domination for the Gaussian integral. The conclusion concerns liminf/limsup envelopes and does not assert existence of the PCT fidelity limit at each fixed `δ` in this projector regime.

## Boundaries that must remain visible in a formalization

- Root fidelity is used throughout; squared fidelity has different exponents.
- The simple full-rank chamber assumption and compact subsets bounded away from collisions/boundaries are indispensable to the uniform PBW, character, and local-limit estimates.
- The unknown-spectrum compact-set converse requires the set to be the closure of its full relative interior. It does not apply to a singleton or an arbitrary lower-dimensional spectral set.
- The fixed-gain assumption is `m_n/n → γ > 1`. An `O(1/n)` convergence rate relative to the final projector value additionally requires `m_n = γn + O(1)`, as the manuscript explicitly states near line 2225.
- The final section's degenerate-orbit and fixed-rank local formulas, `eq:conjectured-degenerate-orbit` and `eq:conjectured-fixed-rank-local`, are **conjectures**, not missing proof obligations of the stated theorems.

## What an end-to-end certificate would still need

An end-to-end Lean proof must instantiate every abstract hypothesis with concrete density operators and quantum channels; establish the representation-theoretic and infinite-dimensional analytic constructions; formalize the stated LAN external theorem with the exact conventions; and connect the resulting results to the original minimax expressions. No `axiom`, `sorry`, or structure field merely restating one of those substantive conclusions should be counted as a proof of it.

### Concrete fidelity and deletion audit

The `MatrixFidelity` modules define fidelity on actual complex matrices by `Re Tr sqrt(sqrt(B) A sqrt(B))`. For positive inputs they prove equality with `matrixTraceNorm (sqrt(A) sqrt(B))`, where `matrixTraceNorm X := Re Tr sqrt(X†X)` is the spectral nuclear norm. This is not identified with Lean's other matrix norm instances.

Proved laws include positivity and self-fidelity; scalar homogeneity; dependent direct-sum additivity; tensor multiplicativity and normalized-ancilla cancellation; diagonal classical affinity; rectangular-isometry invariance; symmetry; canonical trace normalization including zero blocks; and upper bound one for subnormalized positive matrices. The maximally mixed state explicitly requires a nonempty finite dimension.

The dimension-independent trace bound is proved without assuming polar decomposition or Schatten inequalities. For invertible positive `B`, set `C = sqrt B`, `R = sqrt(C A C)` and `D = C⁻¹ R`. Concrete matrix algebra gives `C†D = R` and `Tr(D†D) = Tr A`; Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz proves `F(A,B)² ≤ Tr A Tr B`. Positive-definite regularization extends the result to singular `B`.

Trace-square-root subadditivity follows from inverse-square-root order: for positive definite `X,Y`, pair `X` and `Y` with `(X+Y)⁻¹/²`, compare to their individual inverse square roots using positive trace pairings, then add. Regularizing both inputs extends the bound to all PSD matrices. A product of PSD matrices is never incorrectly assumed PSD; only its trace is shown nonnegative through a positive sandwich.

Fidelity symmetry is proved using the explicit polar isometry `U = X (sqrt(X†X))⁻¹` for invertible `X`, isometric square-root invariance, and trace cyclicity. Simultaneous positive regularization removes the invertibility hypothesis. General perturbation control uses `H = CFC.abs(A-B)`, with `A ≤ B+H`, `B ≤ A+H` and `Tr H = matrixTraceNorm(A-B)`. Monotonicity, subadditivity and the trace bound give first-argument continuity; symmetry and the triangle inequality on real fidelities give the full `√ε + √η` estimate. No quantum data-processing inequality is used.

**The former `hquantum` condition is now proved for the concrete model.** `MatrixFidelityDeletion` shows `|F(G+B,T)-F(G,T)| ≤ sqrt(Tr B Tr T)` for arbitrary positive summands, even with overlapping support. Its lifted theorem gives exactly the canonical retained-block sum and square root of discarded trace. `MatrixFidelityAsymptotics` invokes this theorem in the scalar assembly, so the actual matrix factorization and growing-dimension convergence have no `hquantum` hypothesis. The old generic `Main` interface retains a parameter for abstract uses.

`MatrixLiftedChannel` constructs positive output blocks from genuine CPTP sector channels, proves marginal traces, and assembles a normalized state from a probability input law and stochastic transition rule. `MatrixLiftedTrimming` applies an arbitrary mask depending on both input and output labels. The full block minus retained block is exactly the discarded positive sum, and its trace is exactly the discarded joint mass. This supplies the positive-domination hypotheses automatically for the actual channel outputs. Neither module claims the full measurement/transition/ancilla procedure has been packaged as one channel on arbitrary inputs or identified with the physical Schur construction.

The regularization arguments work at each fixed finite dimension, but their conclusions are universally quantified and have dimension-independent constants. In the asymptotic theorems, label sets, sector dimensions and spectator dimensions all depend explicitly on sample size. No uniform regularization assumption is smuggled into the growing-dimension limit.

### One-sided achievability audit

The matrix variance identity proves operator concavity of square root: for nonnegative `a,b` summing to one, the difference between `a A+b B` and `(a sqrt A+b sqrt B)²` is the positive matrix `ab (sqrt A-sqrt B)²`. Square-root monotonicity then gives concavity, and tracing the target sandwich gives concavity of fidelity in one argument. Finite Jensen and probability-mixture lower bounds are proved, not postulated.

`MatrixTransitionAchievability` identifies each normalized retained transition block with the explicit conditional mixture having weights `keptJoint(i,j) / retainedMarginal(j)`. At positive retained marginal these weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Individual retained positive-weight output lower bounds therefore imply the normalized mixture lower bound. Zero retained marginals require no approximation.

`MatrixFidelityAchievability` derives `F_actual ≥ c * Affinity(q,p) - η - sqrt δ`. The loss is `η + sqrt δ`, rather than the two-sided factorization's `η + 2 sqrt δ`: deleting a positive contribution costs nothing for a lower bound by monotonicity; only the classical marginal replacement costs `sqrt δ`. The hypotheses `0 ≤ c ≤ 1`, probability target weights, total output mass at most one, and `η ≥ 0` are explicit.

`lifted_matrix_eventual_achievability` concludes that for every positive `ε`, eventually `F_actual > c*l - ε`, with sample-dependent matrix dimensions. It does not claim an upper bound or exact convergence of mixture fidelities. Concavity alone would not justify that stronger statement: two orthogonal pure states each have root fidelity `1/sqrt 2` with `I₂/2`, while their equal mixture has fidelity one.

For the lower-bound route, the remaining achievability inputs are uniform **individual good-pair** fidelity lower bounds with vanishing error, vanishing discarded joint mass, the classical affinity limit, and physical Schur/Cartan identification. The exact factorization theorem still needs two-sided retained-sector approximation. The converse still requires the representation and infinite-dimensional LAN constructions and Gaussian optimization. Finite quantum-channel data processing and trace-distance contractivity are now proved.

### Finite channel laws and transfer audit

`MatrixFidelityWitness` explicitly constructs the positive-definite geometric witness `H = A^(-1/2) sqrt(sqrt(A) B sqrt(A)) A^(-1/2)`. It proves positivity, `H A H = B`, and `Re Tr(A H) = F(A,B)`. The rectangular congruence `[I;H] A [I,H]` then gives an attaining positive block matrix with off-diagonal block `A H`. No commutativity of the two density matrices is assumed.

`MatrixFidelityBlockBound` tests an arbitrary positive block against `[H;-I]` and pairs the resulting positive matrix with `H^(-1)`. This gives `2 Re Tr X <= Tr(AH) + Tr(BH^(-1))`. The optimal constructed witness proves `Re Tr X <= F(A,B)` for strictly positive diagonal blocks. Adding `epsilon I` to both diagonal blocks preserves block positivity without changing `X`; continuity removes strict positivity. The helper with an optimal-witness premise is therefore discharged, not used as an unproved final assumption.

`MatrixChannelFidelity` transports the feasible block using the channel's actual two-dimensional amplification. The output's Hermitian symmetry supplies the conjugate off-diagonal relation. Trace preservation keeps the objective fixed, proving data processing for positive-definite inputs. Simultaneous input regularization and finite-dimensional linear continuity handle arbitrary PSD inputs. The channel is not assumed unital or positivity improving, and its output may be singular. Input and output matrix dimensions may differ.

`MatrixTraceNormOrder` proves that the trace norm of a Hermitian matrix is the sum of the absolute eigenvalues. Evaluating any positive-minus-positive decomposition in that eigenbasis bounds this sum by the two positive traces. The spectral Jordan decomposition attains that bound. `MatrixChannelTraceNorm` applies positivity and trace preservation to the two Jordan parts, proving contraction for every Hermitian input and hence for differences of states. This uses the full spectral nuclear norm `Tr sqrt(X^*X)`, not an operator norm, Hilbert–Schmidt norm, or half-normalized trace distance.

`MatrixDiscardLabel` is a CPTP channel on arbitrary matrix inputs: it sums the diagonal classical-label blocks, including for inputs with nonzero off-diagonal blocks. Its amplification is explicitly a sum of positive submatrices. Combining this channel with data processing and the proved direct-sum law yields strong concavity with independent nonnegative weights and joint concavity with matching weights. Empty label sets cause no inconsistency: there are no normalized states in the zero-dimensional domain, and the mixture inequality reduces to zero.

`MatrixWeightedFidelity` proves the noncommutative finite-dimensional inequality `F(A,B)^2 <= Re Tr(AW) * Re Tr(BW^(-1))` for arbitrary PSD inputs and positive-definite `W`. Testing a positive block with `[sW;-I]` and pairing with `W^(-1)` gives a real quadratic nonnegative for every real `s`; its nonpositive discriminant bounds the squared off-diagonal trace. The explicit attaining witness and regularization then give the fidelity inequality. No commutativity or normalization is assumed. This does not establish the infinite-dimensional unbounded-inverse witness estimate in Appendix C.

`MatrixLANTransfer` constructs channel composition and channel images of states. It proves the finite-matrix transfer error `sqrt(delta)+sqrt(eta)` from only the two trace-norm approximation hypotheses, then integrates the uniform comparison to the genuine minimax/Bayes bound. Fidelity positivity, upper bound one, data processing, contractivity, and continuity are all supplied by proved matrix theorems. The Bayes supremum runs over actual finite-dimensional channels. This is not yet a realization of the manuscript's infinite-dimensional Gaussian comparison experiment; no Fock-space limit or truncation theorem is implicitly assumed proved.

### Fifth-pass representation, Gaussian and countable-limit audit

`MatrixPartialTraceCovariance` proves partial-trace covariance by concrete tensor-matrix algebra. `MatrixCovariantBalance` converts a matrix commuting with the actual irreducible representation into an intertwining endomorphism and invokes Mathlib's algebraically closed Schur lemma. The retained dimension is explicitly positive. Trace preservation of partial trace and the isometry identity determine the scalar as `dim C / dim A`. The final channel constructor assumes actual representation laws, unitarity, irreducibility, and an isometric intertwiner; neither scalarity nor balance is assumed. The physical highest-weight representations and their Cartan inclusion are still to be constructed.

`GaussianAffinity` integrates the normalized density `sqrt(a/pi) exp(-a x²)` for positive `a`; its variance is `1/(2a)`. Replacing `a` by `a/gamma` therefore dilates variance by `gamma`. Finite-product Fubini produces root fidelity `(2 sqrt(gamma)/(1+gamma))^(k/2)`, and `k=d-1` yields the manuscript's classical factor. No squared-fidelity convention is substituted. The coordinate-change theorem uses the pushed-forward reference measure under a common measurable equivalence; it does not claim a covariance-matrix density formula relative to unadjusted Lebesgue measure. The Young local limit, actual covariance identification, and discretization bridge remain separate obligations.

`CountableScheffe` proves ℓ¹ convergence from pointwise convergence and exact normalization of nonnegative laws. Its proof controls the overlap `min(p_n,q)` by the summable target law `q`; it does not assume the desired ℓ¹ convergence or a separate uniform tail estimate. Countable Cauchy–Schwarz then proves continuity of Hellinger affinity and convergence of the squared ℓ² distance between square-root coefficients. The geometric product target is genuinely normalized. Werner binomial-ratio asymptotics, Poisson pointwise limits, and the identification of these coefficients with quantum occupation operators are not supplied by this upgrade theorem.

### Infinite-dimensional foundations and external-source audit

The Physlib trace-class development has been adapted from the pinned revision recorded in `EXTERNAL_RESOURCES.json`, retaining authorship, source blob identifiers and the Apache license. Its definitions act on arbitrary complete complex Hilbert spaces. `IsTraceClass` is summability of the positive diagonal of the actual operator absolute value in a Hilbert basis; `trace` is the absolutely summable complex diagonal and `traceNorm` is the positive diagonal sum of the absolute value.
