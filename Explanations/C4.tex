\large The hybrid Gaussian product bound}

\begin{abstract}
Proposition C.4 proves the converse bound for the hybrid Gaussian model.  It
shows that an arbitrary exactly covariant hybrid operation cannot trade
classical error against quantum error to beat the product of the separate
Gaussian and thermal-state fidelities.  The proof uses the residual
subinstrument from Lemma C.3, a branchwise stochastic-order estimate from
Lemma D.4, and a carefully chosen positive witness.  This note develops the
required background and explains every step of the product estimate.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem at the origin}

Fix $\gamma>1$ and a strictly ordered spectrum $p$.  Let
$s=d-1=\dim\Hc$ and enumerate the oscillator modes by
$\ell=1,\ldots,r_d$.  Write
\[
  q_\ell\in(0,1),
  \qquad
  \tau_{q_\ell}=(1-q_\ell)\sum_{k\geq0}q_\ell^k\proj{k},
\]
and set
\[
  \Phi_0=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{r_d}\tau_{q_\ell}.
\]
At parameter $(h,z)=(0,0)$, both the hybrid input and the desired target are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:origin-state}
  \Theta_0
  =\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p}\ot\Phi_0.
\end{equation}
They are the same state at this one parameter value.  Nevertheless, an
admissible map in the converse must be covariant with gain $\sqrt\gamma$ for
\emph{all} translations and displacements.  The identity map has the wrong
gain and is not an admissible competitor.  Amplification necessarily adds
noise, which lowers the fidelity even at the origin.

The natural reference operation consists of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the classical dilation $x\mapsto\sqrt\gamma x$, which sends
  $\mathsf N_{0,\Sigma_p}$ to $\mathsf N_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}$; and
  \item the product quantum-limited amplifier, which sends
  $\tau_{q_\ell}$ to $\tau_{q_\ell^{(\gamma)}}$, where
  \[
    q_\ell^{(\gamma)}=1-\frac{1-q_\ell}{\gamma}.
  \]
\end{itemize}
Its fidelity with \eqref{eq:origin-state} is
\[
  \Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p),
\]
where
\begin{align*}
  \Bcl(\gamma,d)
  &=\left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{s/2},\\
  \Borb(\gamma,p)
  &=\prod_{\ell=1}^{r_d}
    \B(\tau_{q_\ell^{(\gamma)}},\tau_{q_\ell}).
\end{align*}
Proposition C.4 proves that even a hybrid operation coupling the classical
and quantum components cannot do better.

\section{Background I: phase twirling and diagonal states}

Let $\widehat N_\ell$ be the photon-number operator of mode $\ell$ and
\[
  U_\theta^{\mathrm{ph}}
  =\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{r_d}
    e^{\mathrm i\theta_\ell\widehat N_\ell}.
\]
Both the origin input and target are invariant under these independent phase
rotations.  Given a map $\Gamma$, average it as
\[
  \Gamma^{\mathrm{ph}}
  =\int
  \vartheta_{-\theta}^{\mathrm{out}}
  \circ\Gamma\circ
  \vartheta_\theta^{\mathrm{in}}\dd\theta,
\]
using normalized Haar measure on the torus.  Concavity and unitary invariance
of root fidelity imply
\[
  \B(\Gamma^{\mathrm{ph}}(\Theta_0),\Theta_0)
  \geq \B(\Gamma(\Theta_0),\Theta_0).
\]
The twirl preserves the required classical translation and quantum
displacement covariance.  Therefore an upper bound may assume phase
covariance from the outset.

If an operator is invariant under every independent phase rotation, it is
diagonal in the joint number basis
$\{\proj{\mathbf k}:\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{r_d}\}$.  This reduces
the final root fidelity to a classical Hellinger sum inside the classical
integral.

\section{Background II: the reference distributions and witness}

Write the target and amplified-reference densities as
\[
  f_1(y)=\varphi_{0,\Sigma_p}(y),
  \qquad
  f_\gamma(y)=\varphi_{0,\gamma\Sigma_p}(y).
\]
The joint photon-number laws of $\Phi_0$ and of the amplified reference are
\begin{align*}
  t_{\mathbf k}
  &=\prod_\ell(1-q_\ell)q_\ell^{k_\ell},\\
  t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}
  &=\prod_\ell
    (1-q_\ell^{(\gamma)})
    (q_\ell^{(\gamma)})^{k_\ell}.
\end{align*}
Define the square-root likelihood ratios
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witnesses}
  \chi(y)=\sqrt{\frac{f_1(y)}{f_\gamma(y)}},
  \qquad
  w_{\mathbf k}
  =\sqrt{\frac{t_{\mathbf k}}{t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}}.
\end{equation}
Because $\gamma>1$, the Gaussian ratio $\chi$ is positive and bounded.
The operator
\[
  W(\Nhat)=\sum_{\mathbf k}w_{\mathbf k}\proj{\mathbf k}
\]
is bounded.  Moreover,
\[
  w_{\mathbf k}=\prod_\ell w_\ell(k_\ell),
\]
where each factor is positive and decreasing because
$q_\ell<q_\ell^{(\gamma)}$.  This places $W$ exactly within the scope of
Lemma D.4.

The ratios in \eqref{eq:witnesses} are chosen so that both reference overlaps
factor:
\begin{align}
  \int \frac{f_1(y)}{\chi(y)}\dd y
  &=\int\sqrt{f_1(y)f_\gamma(y)}\dd y
    =\Bcl(\gamma,d),
  \label{eq:classical-factor}\\
  \sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{t_{\mathbf k}}{w_{\mathbf k}}
  &=\sum_{\mathbf k}
    \sqrt{t_{\mathbf k}t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}
    =\Borb(\gamma,p).
  \label{eq:quantum-factor}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-witness}
  \sum_{\mathbf k}\int
  \frac{f_1(y)t_{\mathbf k}}
       {\chi(y)w_{\mathbf k}}\dd y
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}

\section{Background III: the branchwise conic bound}

Lemma D.4 says that if $\Lambda$ is a completely positive,
trace-nonincreasing multimode map with gain-$\sqrt\gamma$ displacement
covariance and independent phase covariance, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D4}
  \Tr[\Lambda(\Phi_0)W(\Nhat)]
  \leq
  \Tr[\Lambda(\Phi_0)]
  \Tr[\tau^{(\gamma)}W(\Nhat)],
\end{equation}
where
\[
  \tau^{(\gamma)}
  =\bigotimes_\ell\tau_{q_\ell^{(\gamma)}}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:quantum-factor}, the final trace in \eqref{eq:D4} is
$\Borb(\gamma,p)$.  The estimate is called \emph{conic} because it applies
to subnormalized outputs: the right side is scaled by the output trace.

The one-mode result behind this estimate has the usual least-noise
stochastic-order interpretation.  For a possibly correlated multimode map,
however, Lemma D.4 asserts only the product-test inequality
\eqref{eq:D4}, not a full multivariate stochastic order.  This is sufficient
here because $W$ is precisely a product of decreasing one-mode weights.

\section{Statement of Proposition C.4}

\begin{propC}
Every exactly covariant completely positive trace-nonincreasing hybrid map
$\Gamma$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:claim}
  \B\bigl(\Gamma(\Theta_0),\Theta_0\bigr)
  \leq
  \Buniv(\gamma,p)
  :=\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
\end{equation}
Consequently, the flat-prior hybrid Gaussian payoff is at most
$\Buniv(\gamma,p)$.
\end{propC}

In the paper the input and target at the origin are denoted
$\Theta_0^{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\Theta_0^{\mathrm{tar}}$.  They coincide as in
\eqref{eq:origin-state}; the two labels are retained there to emphasize their
different roles in the covariant families.

\section{Proof, step by step}

\subsection{Step 1: phase twirl and apply Lemma C.3}

By the phase-twirling argument, assume $\Gamma$ is phase covariant without
decreasing its origin payoff.  Lemma C.3 then gives a residual subinstrument
$E\mapsto\mathcal I_E$.  Every branch is displacement covariant with gain
$\sqrt\gamma$ and phase covariant, so Lemma D.4 applies separately to every
$\mathcal I_E$.

Define two finite positive measures on the residual classical space:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu-def}
  \mu_1(E)=\Tr[\mathcal I_E(\Phi_0)],
  \qquad
  \mu_W(E)=\Tr[\mathcal I_E(\Phi_0)W(\Nhat)].
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:D4} to the branch $\mathcal I_E$ gives, for every Borel
set $E$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:measure-order}
  \mu_W(E)
  \leq\Borb(\gamma,p)\mu_1(E).
\end{equation}
Thus $\mu_W\leq\Borb\mu_1$ as measures.  Since the residual instrument is
trace nonincreasing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
  \mu_1(\Hc)\leq1.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Step 2: control a shifted classical Gaussian overlap}

The residual representation convolves the ideal dilated Gaussian
$f_\gamma$ with the residual outcome $r$.  Introduce
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-def}
  c_\gamma(r)
  =\int_{\Hc}\chi(y)f_\gamma(y-r)\dd y.
\end{equation}
A direct completion of the square, using the inverse of $\Sigma_p$ on
$\Hc$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-formula}
  c_\gamma(r)
  =\Bcl(\gamma,d)
  \exp\left[
    -\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma(\gamma+1)}
    \langle r,\Sigma_p^{-1}r\rangle
  \right].
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-max}
  0<c_\gamma(r)\leq c_\gamma(0)=\Bcl(\gamma,d).
\end{equation}
This says that residual classical displacement can only reduce the overlap
with the centered target.  The maximum occurs when no residual displacement
is added.

\subsection{Step 3: bound the witness expectation of the actual output}

Let
\[
  \Xi=\Gamma(\Theta_0).
\]
The Gaussian convolution in Lemma C.3 makes the classical output absolutely
continuous even if the residual measure itself is singular.  Phase
covariance makes its operator density diagonal:
\[
  X(y)=\sum_{\mathbf k}x_{\mathbf k}(y)\proj{\mathbf k}.
\]
Use the test formula of Lemma C.3 with the classical function $\chi$ and the
quantum observable $W$.  Equations \eqref{eq:mu-def} and \eqref{eq:c-def}
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output-identity}
  \sum_{\mathbf k}\int
  \chi(y)w_{\mathbf k}x_{\mathbf k}(y)\dd y
  =\int_{\Hc}c_\gamma(r)\mu_W(\dd r).
\end{equation}
Now apply the measure domination \eqref{eq:measure-order}, the pointwise
bound \eqref{eq:c-max}, and the mass bound \eqref{eq:mass}:
\begin{align}
  \int c_\gamma(r)\mu_W(\dd r)
  &\leq\Borb(\gamma,p)
    \int c_\gamma(r)\mu_1(\dd r)\notag\\
  &\leq\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p).
  \label{eq:output-witness}
\end{align}
This is the crucial joint estimate.  It shows that classical residual noise
and quantum residual noise cannot be correlated in a way that exceeds the
product benchmark.

\subsection{Step 4: convert the witness bounds into fidelity}

By the hybrid fidelity formula from Lemma C.1 and diagonality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fidelity-diagonal}
  \B(\Xi,\Theta_0)
  =\sum_{\mathbf k}\int
    \sqrt{x_{\mathbf k}(y)f_1(y)t_{\mathbf k}}\dd y.
\end{equation}
For positive $u,v,A$, the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amgm}
  \sqrt{uv}\leq\frac12\left(Au+\frac vA\right).
\end{equation}
Apply it pointwise with
\[
  u=x_{\mathbf k}(y),
  \qquad
  v=f_1(y)t_{\mathbf k},
  \qquad
  A=\chi(y)w_{\mathbf k}.
\]
After summing and integrating, the first term is bounded by
\eqref{eq:output-witness}; the second is exactly
\eqref{eq:target-witness}.  Hence
\begin{align*}
  \B(\Xi,\Theta_0)
  &\leq\frac12\left[
    \Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p)
    +\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p)
  \right]\\
  &=\Bcl(\gamma,d)\Borb(\gamma,p),
\end{align*}
which proves \eqref{eq:claim}.

Finally, Lemma C.2 says that the large-cube average payoff of arbitrary
hybrid channels is bounded by the best origin payoff among exactly covariant
trace-nonincreasing maps.  Applying \eqref{eq:claim} to that limiting map
gives the flat-prior upper bound.

\section{Why this particular witness works}

The choice $A=\chi w$ in \eqref{eq:amgm} is not arbitrary.  Equality in
\eqref{eq:amgm} occurs when $Au=v/A$, or
\[
  A=\sqrt{v/u}.
\]
For the reference output
\[
  u=f_\gamma(y)t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)},
  \qquad
  v=f_1(y)t_{\mathbf k},
\]
this optimal ratio is exactly
\[
  \sqrt{\frac{f_1(y)t_{\mathbf k}}
                   {f_\gamma(y)t_{\mathbf k}^{(\gamma)}}}
  =\chi(y)w_{\mathbf k}.
\]
Thus the witness is the tangent functional to root fidelity at the product
quantum-limited output.  The rest of the proof establishes that every
admissible output lies on the correct side of this tangent hyperplane.

\section{Dependencies and logical role}

The proof uses the following results.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Lemma C.1 supplies the integral formula for fidelity of hybrid
  operator-valued measures.
  \item Lemma C.3 represents an exactly covariant hybrid map by a residual
  subinstrument and gives the Gaussian convolution formula.
  \item Lemma D.4 gives the branchwise multimode conic stochastic-order
  estimate.  Its proof tensorizes the one-mode estimate in Lemma D.3, which
  in turn uses the constant-trace reduction in Lemma D.1 and the
  least-noise theorem for Gaussian amplifiers.
  \item Joint concavity and unitary invariance justify phase twirling.
  \item A Gaussian completion of the square and the elementary inequality
  \eqref{eq:amgm} finish the estimate.
\end{itemize}

Proposition C.4 is the final analytic step in the upper bound for the hybrid
Gaussian flat-prior theorem.  Lemma C.2 reduces arbitrary channels to an
exactly covariant limiting map, and C.4 bounds that map by $\Buniv$.  The
explicit product of the classical and orbital factors is therefore a
consequence of the witness argument, not an assumption that the competing
channel itself factors.

\section{Minimal prerequisite checklist}

To follow the numerical part of the proof, it is enough to know Gaussian
densities, geometric photon-number laws, root fidelity for commuting states,
and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality.  The structural reduction
also requires phase twirling, covariant completely positive maps, quantum
subinstruments, and the stochastic-order estimate in Lemma D.4.  The central
idea is that the square-root likelihood ratio of the product reference state
simultaneously tests the classical and quantum components and rules out any
advantage from coupling them.
