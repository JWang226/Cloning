\begin{abstract}
Lemma C.2 is the compactness device used in the unified Gaussian converse proof.
It extracts a completely positive limit from a uniformly bounded sequence of
completely positive maps, even though trace-norm compactness is unavailable
in infinite dimension.  Convergence is tested only with compact output
observables.  This note explains the choice of topology, the diagonal
argument on the adjoints, the possible loss of trace, and the passage from
asymptotic to exact covariance.
\end{abstract}

\section{What the lemma does}

For a Hilbert space $\Hc$, we use the operator classes
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Bop(\Hc)
  &:=\{A:\Hc\to\Hc:A\text{ is linear and bounded}\},\\
  \K(\Hc)
  &:=\overline{\{A\in\Bop(\Hc):\operatorname{rank}A<\infty\}}^{\|\cdot\|},\\
  \T(\Hc)
  &:=\{X\in\K(\Hc):\Tr|X|<\infty\},
  \qquad \|X\|_1:=\Tr|X|,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $|X|=(X^*X)^{1/2}$.  Thus $\Bop(\Hc)$, $\K(\Hc)$, and
$\T(\Hc)$ are respectively the bounded, compact, and trace-class
operators, with
$\T(\Hc)\subset\K(\Hc)\subset\Bop(\Hc)$.

Let
\[
  \Gamma_j:\T(\Hc_0)\longrightarrow\T(\Hc_1)
\]
be completely positive maps that are uniformly bounded as maps between the
two trace-class spaces: for some $M<\infty$,
\[
  \sup_j\|\Gamma_j\|_{1\to1}
  :=\sup_j\sup_{X\ne0}\frac{\|\Gamma_j(X)\|_1}{\|X\|_1}
  \leq M.
\]
For each fixed $X$, this bound only places the outputs $\Gamma_j(X)$ in a
bounded subset of the trace class, and such subsets are not generally
precompact in trace norm in infinite dimension (orthogonal rank-one states,
for example, remain pairwise trace distance two).  Lemma C.2 instead uses the
weaker topology obtained by testing outputs against compact operators.  Since
$\T(\Hc_1)=\K(\Hc_1)^*$, Banach--Alaoglu makes every closed norm-bounded ball
weak-* compact; moreover, separability of $\K(\Hc_1)$ makes this topology
metrizable on such a ball, so every bounded sequence has a weak-* convergent
subsequence.  This allows one to extract a map $\Gamma$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-convergence}
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma(X)K]
\end{equation}
for every trace-class input $X$ and every compact output observable $K$.

This is exactly the convergence needed later.  The quantum witness
$W_\gamma$ appearing in Proposition C.5 is compact, so its moment
passes to the limit by \eqref{eq:weak-convergence}.

The lemma also records two stability properties:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a common trace bound passes to the limit, although equality of traces
  need not;
  \item if the maps become covariant in trace norm, the limiting map is
  exactly covariant.
\end{itemize}

\section{The topology behind the statement}

\subsection{Trace class is the dual of the compact operators}

For a separable Hilbert space $\Hc$,
\[
  \K(\Hc)^*=\T(\Hc),
  \qquad
  \langle X,K\rangle=\Tr(XK).
\]
Consequently, bounded sets of trace-class operators are compact in the
weak-* topology induced by compact observables.  A sequence $X_j$ converges
to $X$ in this topology precisely when
\[
  \Tr(X_jK)\longrightarrow\Tr(XK)
  \qquad(K\in\K(\Hc)).
\]

This topology is weaker than the usual weak topology obtained by testing
against all bounded operators.  In particular, the identity is not compact
when $\Hc$ is infinite-dimensional, so weak-* convergence on $\K(\Hc)$ does
not force convergence of traces.

For example, if $(e_n)$ is an orthonormal basis, then
\[
  |e_n\rangle\!\langle e_n|\stackrel{w^*}{\longrightarrow}0
  \quad\text{on }\K(\Hc),
\]
where $w^*$ is read ``weak star'' and means convergence after pairing with
every compact operator.  Indeed,
\[
  \Tr\!\left(|e_n\rangle\!\langle e_n|K\right)
  =\langle e_n,Ke_n\rangle\longrightarrow0
  \qquad(K\in\K(\Hc)),
\]
even though every operator $|e_n\rangle\!\langle e_n|$ has trace one.
Compact observables cannot see a unit amount of mass moving to arbitrarily
high basis vectors.  This is the ``escape to infinity'' allowed by Lemma C.2.

\subsection{Why the proof works with adjoints}

The Banach adjoint of $\Gamma_j$ is
\[
  \Gamma_j^*:\Bop(\Hc_1)\longrightarrow\Bop(\Hc_0),
  \qquad
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)A]=\Tr[X\Gamma_j^*(A)].
\]
Restricting $\Gamma_j^*$ to $\K(\Hc_1)$ turns the problem into extracting a
pointwise weak-* limit in $\Bop(\Hc_0)=\T(\Hc_0)^*$.  Banach--Alaoglu gives
the required compactness on bounded sets of this dual space.

\section{Statement of Lemma C.2}

\begin{lemmaC}
Let $\Hc_0,\Hc_1$ be separable Hilbert spaces and let
$\Gamma_j:\T(\Hc_0)\to\T(\Hc_1)$ be completely positive maps whose
induced norms $\|\Gamma_j\|_{1\to1}$ are uniformly bounded.  Then a
subsequence and a completely positive map $\Gamma$ exist such that
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma_j(X)K]\longrightarrow\Tr[\Gamma(X)K]
\]
for all $X\in\T(\Hc_0)$ and $K\in\K(\Hc_1)$.

If, for every positive $X$,
\[
  \Tr\Gamma_j(X)\leq c\Tr X,
\]
then $\Tr\Gamma(X)\leq c\Tr X$.  Suppose $U_g$ on $\Hc_0$ and $V_g$ on
$\Hc_1$ are families of unitaries indexed by a set $G$ and satisfy
\[
  \|\Gamma_j(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\|_1\longrightarrow0
\]
for every fixed $g$ and $X$, then
\[
  \Gamma(U_gXU_g^*)=V_g\Gamma(X)V_g^*.
\]
\end{lemmaC}

\section{Proof walkthrough}

\subsection*{Step 1: diagonalize on a countable family of observables}

Because $\Hc_1$ is separable, $\K(\Hc_1)$ is separable in operator norm.
Choose a countable norm-dense $\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)$-linear $*$-algebra
$\mathcal D\subset\K(\Hc_1)$, where
$\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)=\{a+\mathrm ib:a,b\in\mathbb Q\}$ is the countable
dense subfield of Gaussian rationals in $\mathbb C$.  Using this field keeps
$\mathcal D$ countable while preserving addition, products, adjoints, and
enough scalar multiplication to extend the limiting map uniquely to a
complex-linear map by continuity.

For every fixed $K\in\mathcal D$, the operators $\Gamma_j^*(K)$ form a
bounded subset of $\Bop(\Hc_0)$.  Banach--Alaoglu gives a weak-* convergent
subsequence because the predual $\T(\Hc_0)$ is separable and the bounded
weak-* compact sets are therefore metrizable.  Applying this successively to
the countably many elements of $\mathcal D$ and taking the diagonal
subsequence gives
\[
  \Gamma_j^*(K)\stackrel{w^*}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma^*(K)
  \qquad(K\in\mathcal D).
\]

The common norm bound makes the limiting assignment bounded.  It therefore
extends uniquely from $\mathcal D$ to a bounded complex-linear map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-adjoint}
  \Gamma^*:\K(\Hc_1)\longrightarrow\Bop(\Hc_0),
\end{equation}
and the same bound extends convergence to every compact $K$.

\subsection*{Step 2: complete positivity survives the limit}

Complete positivity must be checked at every matrix level.  Fix
$A\geq0$ in $M_r(\K(\Hc_1))$.  The square root $A^{1/2}$ is compact.
Choose $B_\ell\in M_r(\mathcal D)$ with
$B_\ell\to A^{1/2}$ in norm.  Complete positivity of every
$\Gamma_j^*$ gives
\[
  \Gamma_j^{*(r)}(B_\ell^*B_\ell)\geq0.
\]
First let $j\to\infty$ and then let $\ell\to\infty$.  The positive cone is
weak-* closed, so $\Gamma^{*(r)}(A)\geq0$.  Since this holds for every $r$,
the map in \eqref{eq:limit-adjoint} is completely positive.

\subsection*{Step 3: return to a map on trace class}

For $X\in\T(\Hc_0)$, define a functional on $\K(\Hc_1)$ by
\[
  K\longmapsto\Tr[X\Gamma^*(K)].
\]
It is bounded, hence it is represented by a unique trace-class operator
$\Gamma(X)$ because $\K(\Hc_1)^*=\T(\Hc_1)$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:predual-definition}
  \Tr[\Gamma(X)K]=\Tr[X\Gamma^*(K)].
\end{equation}
The complete positivity of $\Gamma^*$ implies complete positivity of
$\Gamma$.  Combining the construction of $\Gamma^*$ with
\eqref{eq:predual-definition} proves the desired convergence
\eqref{eq:weak-convergence}.

\subsection*{Step 4: recover the trace inequality}

Let $P_N$ be increasing finite-rank projections on $\Hc_1$ with
$P_N\uparrow I$ strongly.  For a positive trace-class operator $Y$,
\[
  \Tr Y=\sup_N\Tr(YP_N).
\]
Therefore, for $X\geq0$,
\begin{align*}
  \Tr\Gamma(X)
  &=\sup_N\Tr[\Gamma(X)P_N]\\
  &=\sup_N\lim_j\Tr[\Gamma_j(X)P_N]\\
  &\leq\liminf_j\Tr\Gamma_j(X)\\
  &\leq c\Tr X.
\end{align*}
The third line uses
$\sup_N\lim_j a_{j,N}\leq\liminf_j\sup_N a_{j,N}$ with
$a_{j,N}=\Tr[\Gamma_j(X)P_N]$; here
$\sup_N a_{j,N}=\Tr\Gamma_j(X)$ because $\Gamma_j(X)\geq0$ and
$P_N\uparrow I$.  Thus the trace bound passes to the limit even though the
traces themselves need not converge.
The argument gives an inequality rather than equality because the identity
is approximated from below only after the limit has been taken.  Escaped
mass contributes to $\Tr\Gamma_j(X)$ but to none of the fixed compact
projections $P_N$.

\subsection*{Step 5: asymptotic covariance becomes exact}

Fix $g$, $X$, and $K\in\K(\Hc_1)$.  Pair the covariance defect with $K$:
\begin{align*}
 &\left|\Tr\left[
   \bigl(\Gamma_j(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\bigr)K
  \right]\right|\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \|\Gamma_j(U_gXU_g^*)-V_g\Gamma_j(X)V_g^*\|_1\,\|K\|.
\end{align*}
The right side tends to zero.  On the second term, cyclicity replaces $K$
by $V_g^*KV_g$, which is still compact.  Passing to the limit gives
\[
  \Tr[\Gamma(U_gXU_g^*)K]
  =\Tr[V_g\Gamma(X)V_g^*K].
\]
Compact operators separate trace-class operators, so the two outputs are
equal.  This proves exact covariance for every fixed $g$.

\section{Points worth keeping straight}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The lemma does not assert trace-norm convergence of outputs.  It
  asserts convergence only after testing with a compact observable.
  \item A sequence of channels may converge to a trace-nonincreasing map.
  This is a genuine infinite-dimensional phenomenon, not a defect in the
  proof.
  \item Complete positivity is not obtained from positivity alone; the
  closed-cone argument must be made at every matrix level.
  \item The covariance hypothesis is pointwise in $g$ and $X$.  That is
  enough because the conclusion is also proved one fixed $g$ at a time.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Role in Appendix C}

In the orbital specialization of Proposition C.5, F\o lner-averaged channels
are asymptotically displacement covariant.  Lemma C.2 extracts an exactly
covariant trace-nonincreasing limit, and compactness of $W_\gamma$ lets its
weighted moment pass to that limit.  In the full classical--quantum
specialization, the same lemma is applied to the weighted quantum maps
$\Gamma_j^\chi$.  Their initial trace bound is only $\|\chi\|_\infty$; the
classical convolution argument later improves the limit to a
trace-nonincreasing map.  Thus Lemma C.2 supplies the quantum compactness and
exact covariance common to both cases of the unified proposition.
