From PBW frames to uniform Cartan-sector fidelity

Paper: Lemma A.1 · Proposition 2.3 · §2.2 · Appendix A · §2.1

The representation-theoretic core compares actual normalized lowering words with a Fock basis. Fixed-height matrix estimates identify a particular orthonormal frame and its rate; thermal tail control then gives the sector fidelity used by the physical cloners.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Unpacks the Appendix A proof of the sector-fidelity proposition in Section 2.2 into PBW Gram and frame estimates, Cartan coefficients, and thermal cutoff removal. Lean provides the named quantitative auxiliaries alongside the qualitative Cartan transition limits used by the concrete physical protocols; neither asserts a rate over the whole unbounded Fock basis.

Setting and assumptions

  • The PBW height cutoff R is fixed, and positive-root gaps have a linear lower bound cN with c>0.
  • For sector limits, γ>1, m(n)/n→γ, and spectra range over a compact positive simple set.
  • The coordinate windows may shrink at any rate δ_n→0. Mixtures share a common target sector and output frame.
01

Prove quantitative independence of lowering words

Normalize each lowering word by the relevant root-gap scale and compute its exact-weight Gram matrix. At every fixed height the Gram matrix is O(N^(−1/2)) from identity, uniformly under the root-gap bound. This proves invertibility, rather than assuming that the chosen words already form an orthonormal family.

Paper: Lemma A.1

theorem eventually_weightGram_rate (R : ℕ) (c : ℝ) (hc : 0<c) :
    ∃ C : ℝ,0≤C ∧ ∀ᶠ (N : ℕ) in atTop,
      ∀ (mu : Fin d→ℕ) (hmu : Antitone mu),
      (∀ a : PositiveRoot d,c*(N:ℝ)≤rootGap mu a) →
      ∀ (η : Fin d→ℤ),
      IsUnit (Matrix.gram ℂ (weightRawFrame mu hmu R η)) ∧
      ∀ i j : WeightOccupation d R η,
        ‖⟪weightRawFrame mu hmu R η i,weightRawFrame mu hmu R η j⟫_ℂ-
          (if i=j then 1 else 0 : ℂ)‖≤C/Real.sqrt (N:ℝ)
02

Choose and identify the frame

Apply the symmetric inverse square root of the Gram matrix. The resulting vectors are an orthonormal basis of the complete exact-weight space and stay O(N^(−1/2)) from the normalized raw words. Specifying this construction makes coefficient comparisons meaningful across the different input and output representations.

03

Compute the physical Cartan coefficients

The Cartan inclusion sends a highest-weight vector to the tensor product of highest-weight vectors. Expanding lowering operators across the tensor product gives product-binomial coefficients. The proof compares these coefficients in the constructed symmetric frames at the actual finite root-gap fractions, with the same fixed-height rate.

Paper: §A.2 · Character normalization and fixed-height matrices

theorem eventually_cartan_symmetricFrame_product_rate (R : ℕ) (c : ℝ) (hc : 0<c) :
    ∃ C : ℝ,0≤C ∧ ∀ᶠ (N : ℕ) in atTop,
      ∀ (mu nu : Fin d→ℕ) (hmu : Antitone mu) (hnu : Antitone nu),
      (∀ a : PositiveRoot d,c*(N:ℝ)≤rootGap mu a) →
      (∀ a : PositiveRoot d,c*(N:ℝ)≤rootGap nu a) →
      ∀ k : HeightOccupation d R,
      ‖cartanAmbientInclusion mu nu hmu hnu
          (blockSymmetricSectorFrame (fun a=>mu a+nu a)
            (sumPartition_antitone mu nu hmu hnu) R k) -
        ∑uv∈(loweringSplits (canonicalWord k.val)).toFinset,
          ((∏a : PositiveRoot d,Cloning.Occupation.splitAmplitude
            (canonicalWord k.val |>.count a) (uv.1.count a) (rootFraction mu nu a) : ℝ) : ℂ) •
            tensorJoin (wordSymmetricFrame mu hmu R uv.1)
              (wordSymmetricFrame nu hnu R uv.2)‖≤C/Real.sqrt (N:ℝ)
04

Remove the cutoff and allow mixtures

Character normalization supplies the thermal normalization, and tail estimates allow the fixed-height cutoff to grow after taking the large-sample limit. Compactness upgrades the sequential conclusion to arbitrary shrinking windows. The mixture theorem quantifies over every finite cardinality and every probability law, which is essential when many source sectors contribute to one output sector.

Paper: Proposition 2.3 · §A.2 · Character normalization and fixed-height matrices

05

Derive admissibility rather than assuming it

Strict spectral gaps and γ>1 imply that the target and the target-minus-source are eventually dominant and nonnegative. The compatibility theorem starts with signed target lattice coordinates, so the later channel argument does not silently assume that rounding has already produced a valid Young label.

Paper: Proposition 2.3

Scope of this step

  • The C/√N estimates concern fixed heights; they do not assert a uniform estimate over the entire unbounded Fock basis.
  • The arbitrary-mixture theorem uses one common target sector. It is not a fidelity-linearity assertion for unrelated target states.
