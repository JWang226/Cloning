Gaussian amplification against arbitrary competitors

Paper: Theorem 3.1 · Lemma C.1 · Lemma C.2 · Lemma C.3 · Proposition C.4 · Proposition C.5 · §3.1 · Appendix C

The Gaussian converse optimizes over all channels before taking the large-prior limit. Translation averaging and weighted fidelity reduce the problem to a covariant oscillator moment, where a representation theorem and least-noise inequality identify the optimal value.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Moves the Appendix C proof behind the Gaussian amplification theorem of Section 3.1 ahead of the physical LAN application. Lean proves the general Gaussian converse and its score-window instance using arbitrary competitors and possibly trace-losing CP limits; Gaussian optimality is a conclusion, not a restriction on competing channels.

  • Statement §3.1 · Local parametrization and Gaussian amplification
  • Argument Appendix C · Unified Gaussian flat-prior converse

Setting and assumptions

  • Gain γ is greater than one; thermal parameters satisfy 0≤q_i<1, so vacuum modes are included.
  • Flat prior bodies are compact and convex with zero in their interior, and dilate through real radii L→∞.
  • The full model includes D−1 classical coordinates; the orbital model is the zero-classical-coordinate case.
01

Obtain a weighted fidelity witness

A regularized inverse-moment inequality bounds squared fidelity by a weighted output moment. The limit theorem passes from positive regularization to a finite right limit, so the witness is justified even when an inverse is not bounded on the whole space. The hybrid version allows a nonnormalized nonnegative integrable classical weight multiplying an arbitrary positive trace-class target.

Paper: Lemma C.1

02

Average and extract a covariant limit

Translate near-optimal competitors over expanding prior bodies and apply the weighted witness. Compact-observable convergence gives a subsequence and a completely positive limit. The trace bound survives as an upper bound, which is enough for the converse even if probability escapes to infinity. This step does not incorrectly promote the limiting map to trace preserving.

Paper: Lemma C.2

03

Represent every covariant amplifier

Weyl covariance forces a squeezing dilation with an auxiliary idler state. The formal result establishes a unique joint idler without assuming Gaussianity, diagonality, or independence between modes. This is why the subsequent bound applies to arbitrary covariant competitors, including correlated ones.

Paper: Lemma C.3

04

Bound the oscillator moment

For nonnegative antitone product observables, the least-noise moment is maximal for the least-noise construction. The CP trace-nonincreasing version keeps the exact output-trace multiplier. The classical translation estimate supplies the classical affinity factor; the oscillator estimate supplies the product of thermal mode factors.

Paper: Proposition C.4

05

Match the bound and recover the manuscript window

The product physical amplifier and classical dilation achieve the calculated value. Whitening transports the trace-zero sup-norm box to the exact score polytope, rather than substituting a rectangular window. Its positive finite measure scale cancels in normalized averages, so the endpoint concerns the manuscript’s actual prior and the supremum over all CPTP maps.

Paper: Theorem 3.1 · Proposition C.5
