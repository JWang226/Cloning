01

The physical problem and its conventions

The formal problem starts with density matrices and actual quantum channels. The payoff compares the whole output state with the full product target. Orbit and spectral uncertainty then enter through explicit infima, before the supremum over channels.

Follow the argument →02

Young labels: concentration and the local Gaussian law

Schur decomposition separates spectral information in a Young label from the conditional representation state. The formal development identifies the resulting law for actual tensor powers, proves a dimension-uniform tail bound, and resolves its lattice-scale Gaussian behavior.

Follow the argument →03

From PBW frames to uniform Cartan-sector fidelity

The representation-theoretic core compares actual normalized lowering words with a Fock basis. Fixed-height matrix estimates identify a particular orthonormal frame and its rate; thermal tail control then gives the sector fidelity used by the physical cloners.

Follow the argument →04

Lifted channels, randomized dilation, and factorization

A stochastic rule on Schur labels becomes a genuine covariant channel by applying the corresponding sector transition. An exact block-fidelity identity and a discarded-mass estimate turn the sector theorem into a uniform statement for arbitrary lifted kernels.

Follow the argument →05

Gaussian amplification against arbitrary competitors

The Gaussian converse optimizes over all channels before taking the large-prior limit. Translation averaging and weighted fidelity reduce the problem to a covariant oscillator moment, where a representation theorem and least-noise inequality identify the optimal value.

Follow the argument →06

Constructed LAN and fidelity transfer

Local asymptotic normality is implemented by channels in both directions between tensor-power states and a classical Gaussian field of thermal oscillator states. The target sample size changes the local displacement by √γ while leaving the reference covariance and thermal parameters fixed.

Follow the argument →07

The known-spectrum optimum

When the spectrum is known, the correct output Young-label law can be sampled directly. The only asymptotic fidelity cost comes from cloning the unknown eigenbasis, and the orbital Gaussian converse proves that no other channel improves its limiting value.

Follow the argument →08

One universal cloner and the compact-set optimum

Without the spectrum, the classical Young-label fluctuations must be amplified along with the eigenbasis information. A single spectrum-independent channel achieves the product of the classical and orbital factors. Its minimax optimality needs spectral neighborhoods inside the allowed set.

Follow the argument →09

Exact rank compression and projector-state optimality

Flat projector states are treated by exact representation identities rather than by taking a singular limit of the simple-spectrum theorem. Rank embeddings compress the physical Cartan channels, identify their fidelity loss, and support an independent finite-dimensional converse.

Follow the argument →10

Purify–clone–trace: exact channels, limits, and guarantees

PCT is a fixed composition: random purification with a chosen rank bound, Werner cloning in the enlarged space, and partial trace of the purifying register. The proofs evaluate that physical construction and compare it with the sharp cloners, while also giving uniform lower guarantees beyond simple spectra.

Follow the argument →11

Small errors, rank boundaries, and noncommuting limits

The small-error results begin with explicit scalar expansions and then transfer them to actual channel fidelities. They also identify where fixed-spectrum asymptotics fail to remain uniform: near rank loss, near purity, and near a repeated eigenvalue.

Follow the argument →12

Spectral order and what is known for all states

The explicit formulas support comparison theorems and rigorous bounds on the harder all-density problem. Ratio-wise monotonicity gives useful spectral order, but neither majorization nor the existing all-state bounds determines the exact global optimum.
