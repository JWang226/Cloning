Lifted channels, randomized dilation, and factorization

A stochastic rule on Schur labels becomes a genuine covariant channel by applying the corresponding sector transition. An exact block-fidelity identity and a discarded-mass estimate turn the sector theorem into a uniform statement for arbitrary lifted kernels.

English explanation · exact Lean excerpts are shown separately.

This chapter in the dependency map →

Setting and assumptions

  • A transition table has nonnegative entries and each source row sums to one.
  • For asymptotic factorization, its joint input-output label law has uniformly vanishing mass outside a common shrinking spectral window.
  • Simple spectra range over a compact set and m(n)/n→γ>1. The kernel may depend on the spectrum in the general factorization theorem.
01

Build the channel before evaluating it

The physical Schur instrument measures a source copy, samples a target copy from the stochastic table, applies the Cartan transition when compatible, and uses a defined fallback otherwise. The construction is CPTP on every input operator. Physical code indexes irreducible copies; ordinary Young-label rules are represented by distributing their multiplicity copies.

02

Use the exact direct-sum formula

After Schur decomposition, each output block is a weighted sum of sector outputs. The exact identity expresses the global physical fidelity through those blocks. This retains the conditional mixtures that would be lost by simply averaging individual sector fidelities.

03

Trim only small joint-label mass

Retain close source-target pairs, normalize every nonzero retained output block, and apply the common-target mixture theorem. Fidelity continuity controls the omitted quantum output and the omitted classical law. The resulting error is at most η+2√b, where b is the actual discarded joint-label mass. Sending η and b to zero proves factorization for arbitrary stochastic kernels satisfying the stated concentration condition.

04

Generate unknown labels by randomized dilation

The universal rule dilates the observed input label by m/n and rounds on the trace-one lattice with bounded error. On the central window the rounded output is eventually compatible; elsewhere a fallback keeps the channel defined. L1 continuity under dilation compares the raw law with a Gaussian of covariance γΣ_p, and Gaussian affinity supplies the classical factor.

Scope of this step

  • An arbitrary stochastic kernel need not concentrate. Vanishing discarded mass is a genuine hypothesis of the general factorization theorem.
  • The raw rounded law and the fallback law are distinct objects; their comparison uses control of the fallback probability.
