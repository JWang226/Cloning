The known-spectrum optimum

Paper: Theorem 1.1 · §1.1 · §2.1 · §2.3 · §3.3

When the spectrum is known, the correct output Young-label law can be sampled directly. The only asymptotic fidelity cost comes from cloning the unknown eigenbasis, and the orbital Gaussian converse proves that no other channel improves its limiting value.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Reunites the known-spectrum theorem in Section 1.1 with its exact target-label rule in Section 2.1, attainment in Section 2.3, and converse in Section 3.3. Lean combines concrete sector and probability assembly with the constructed LAN witness; the final optimum is unconditional, although reusable intermediate interfaces remain available.

At a glance

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\mathcal M_n}\inf_{U\in\mathrm U(D)}F\!\left(\mathcal M_n(\rho_{p,U}^{\otimes n}),\rho_{p,U}^{\otimes m_n}\right)=F_{\mathrm{orb}}(\gamma,p).

Setting and assumptions

  • p is a positive simple spectrum in physical dimension D.
  • m(n) is natural-valued and m(n)/n→γ>1.
  • Compact uniformity concerns compact subsets of the simple positive chamber and every unitary eigenbasis.
01

Sample the target label law exactly

knownSpectrumChannel uses the known spectrum to select target Schur copies with their exact m-copy probabilities. The rule does not need to infer p from the measured label. After summing source probabilities, the output classical marginal is the target law, whose affinity with itself is one.

02

Evaluate the constructed physical cloner

Concentration discards bad pairs with vanishing mass, and the uniform sector-mixture theorem supplies the orbital factor on the retained blocks. Exact covariance removes eigenbasis dependence. The prescribed-channel wrapper also handles early output sizes m≤n by exact restriction, while agreeing eventually with the cloning construction.

theorem eventually_uniform_prescribedKnownSpectrumChannel_payoff {d : ℕ}
    (K : Set (SimpleSpectrum (d+1))) (hK : IsCompact K)
    (m : ℕ → ℕ) (γ : ℝ) (hγ : 1<γ)
    (hgain : Tendsto (fun n => (m n : ℝ)/n) atTop (𝓝 γ)) (ε : ℝ) (hε : 0<ε) :
    ∀ᶠ n in atTop,∀ p∈K,∀ U : unitary (Matrix (Fin (d+1)) (Fin (d+1)) ℂ),
      |spectrumPayoff n (m n) (prescribedKnownSpectrumChannel n (m n) (d+1) p.eigenvalue
        (fun a => (p.positive a).le) p.normalized) p U-orbitalValue γ p|<ε
03

Bound every competitor locally

Choose a bounded orbital displacement window around the fixed reference spectrum. Constructed quantum LAN transfers any physical competitor into a Gaussian competitor with vanishing error. The worst-case orbit payoff is bounded by its average on this local family, and the expanding-prior Gaussian optimum gives the orbital upper bound.

04

Close the minimax limit

The explicit cloner provides the asymptotic lower bound, while the all-channel converse supplies the limsup bound. Payoffs stay in [0,1], so the matching bounds yield actual convergence of the finite-copy supremum-infimum to orbitalValue.

Scope of this step

  • The attaining channel is allowed to depend on p; its eigenbasis remains unknown.
  • This theorem alone does not apply to a repeated-eigenvalue spectrum by continuity in p. The degeneracy chapter explains a concrete obstruction to exchanging limits.
