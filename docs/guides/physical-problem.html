The physical problem and its conventions

The formal problem starts with density matrices and actual quantum channels. The payoff compares the whole output state with the full product target. Orbit and spectral uncertainty then enter through explicit infima, before the supremum over channels.

English explanation · exact Lean excerpts are shown separately.

This chapter in the dependency map →

At a glance

\begin{gathered}F(\rho,\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho},\\f_\gamma(q)=\frac{\sqrt\gamma+\sqrt{q(\gamma-1+q)}}{\gamma+q},\qquad 0\le q<1,\\F_{\mathrm{orb}}(\gamma,p)=\prod_{i<j}f_\gamma(p_j/p_i),\\F_{\mathrm{cl}}(\gamma,D)=\left(\frac{2\sqrt\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right)^{(D-1)/2},\\F_{\mathrm{univ}}(\gamma,p)=F_{\mathrm{cl}}(\gamma,D)F_{\mathrm{orb}}(\gamma,p).\end{gathered}

Setting and assumptions

  • D denotes physical Hilbert-space dimension. Many files write D=d+1 to make positivity of the dimension automatic.
  • A simple spectrum has strictly positive, strictly decreasing entries whose sum is one.
  • The asymptotic cloning regime is a prescribed natural-valued sequence m(n) with m(n)/n → γ>1.
01

Keep the fidelity convention visible

The central quantity is root fidelity F(A,B)=Tr sqrt(sqrt(A) B sqrt(A)), extended to positive trace-class operators. For normalized states it lies between zero and one. The small-error chapters explicitly use 1−F²; squaring an asymptotic root fidelity changes exponents and expansion coefficients.

02

Quantify over genuine channels

QuantumChannel packages an actual linear map on trace-class operators with positivity, trace preservation, and complete positivity. Consequently the physical minimax values quantify over CPTP competitors, rather than a class of symbolic strategies or channels assumed to obey a Gaussian limit.

03

Express the decision problem literally

statePayoff applies a competing channel to ρ tensor n and takes fidelity with ρ tensor m. spectrumPayoff substitutes the unitary orbit of a diagonal spectrum. knownSpectrumValue optimizes against the eigenbasis; unknownSpectrumValue additionally optimizes against the unknown spectrum in a specified set K. The competitor in the latter may depend on K, but cannot depend on the actual spectrum selected by the adversary.

04

Name the limiting factors

Each ordered eigenvalue pair i<j contributes the one-mode factor fγ(q) shown above, with q=p_j/p_i. Their product is orbitalValue. universalValue multiplies this by the classical affinity factor (2√γ/(1+γ))^((D−1)/2) for the D−1 spectral coordinates. These are explicit scalar definitions; identifying them with physical channel limits is the work of the later chapters.
