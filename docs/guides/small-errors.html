Small errors, rank boundaries, and noncommuting limits

Paper: Proposition D.3 · §6.3 · §D.3 · §6.4

The small-error results begin with explicit scalar expansions and then transfer them to actual channel fidelities. They also identify where fixed-spectrum asymptotics fail to remain uniform: near rank loss, near purity, and near a repeated eigenvalue.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Separates the expansions and projector-PCT envelopes in Section 6.3 and Appendix D.3 from the qubit limit-order example discussed in Section 6.4. Lean proves physical target brackets in the stated iterated limits and a concrete qubit obstruction; it does not infer a uniform joint finite-sample rate or prove the general degenerate-spectrum conjectures.

Setting and assumptions

  • The full-rank quadratic expansions fix a positive simple spectrum before taking gain γ=1+δ down to one.
  • The physical target brackets use a fixed slack 0<η<1 and prescribed output sequences with the required gain.
  • The projector envelope uses 0<δ<1/(2r); physical target brackets require a nontrivial complement D−r>0.
01

Expand squared infidelity

Explicit thermal formulas give limits of (1−F(1+δ,p)²)/δ² for known-spectrum, universal, and PCT values. Their positive coefficients determine the √ε scale of the allowable additional-copy ratio at squared-infidelity target ε. Slack factors 1−η and 1+η produce strict scalar brackets.

02

Transfer strict margins to finite samples

At each fixed small error, the channel or minimax fidelity converges to its explicit fixed-gain limit. A strict scalar margin therefore persists for all sufficiently large n. This gives two-sided physical brackets for the known-spectrum optimum, full PCT, and prescribed universal cloner; the universal brackets hold uniformly over eigenbases.

03

Use envelopes for projector PCT

The finite support factor isolates an internal error. Purity estimates control that internal term and yield liminf/limsup envelopes for the physical squared infidelity. Both envelopes have first-order coefficient r(D−r), matching optimal projector cloning, without requiring a fixed-gain PCT fidelity limit.

04

Identify the loss of uniformity at boundaries

If the smallest eigenvalue tends to zero, the extreme eigenvalue ratio tends to zero and a single positive term forces the quadratic coefficients to diverge. A separate near-pure theorem treats the joint scaling q=o(δ). These are different regimes from a fixed-spectrum quadratic expansion.

05

Exhibit noncommuting physical limits

An explicit simple qubit spectrum approaches the maximally mixed state. At fixed sample sizes, a constant preparation channel has orbit-uniform fidelity tending to one and the all-channel optimum follows. Taking the large-sample cloning limit first instead gives the limiting orbital formula. The theorem proves these two iterated limits disagree for the actual physical minimax.

Paper: §6.4 · Conjectures for degenerate and rank-deficient spectra

Scope of this step

  • The iterated asymptotics take n→∞ at fixed positive additional-copy ratio, then send that ratio to zero. Equivalently, target brackets hold for each sufficiently small ε and then for n above an ε-dependent cutoff.
  • No uniform joint finite-sample/small-error rate is inferred.
  • The universal physical brackets describe the specified universal cloner; they do not assert pointwise optimality when p is supplied to a competitor.
