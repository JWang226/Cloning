Spectral order and what is known for all states

Paper: §6.1 · §6.2 · §6.4

The explicit formulas support comparison theorems and rigorous bounds on the harder all-density problem. Ratio-wise monotonicity gives useful spectral order, but neither majorization nor the existing all-state bounds determines the exact global optimum.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Turns the spectral-order observations in Section 6.1 and the all-state bounds in Section 6.2 into separate monotonicity, exact counterexample, geometric-infimum, and minimax-bound results in Lean. The exact all-density optimum, PCT optimality for that problem, and the general conjectures of Section 6.4 remain open.

Setting and assumptions

  • Ratio comparisons use normalized decreasing simple spectra of the same dimension and γ>1.
  • The all-density minimax ranges over every physical density matrix, including rank-deficient and repeated-eigenvalue states.
  • Large-gain statements concern the explicit upper and lower bounds as γ→∞, not a proved exact all-state value.
01

Differentiate the mode factor

The one-mode derivative determines how fidelity changes as each ratio p_j/p_i changes. Multiplying positive factors gives nonstrict and strict comparison results for orbitalValue and universalValue. Ratio-wise order implies every required prefix-sum inequality for majorization, but this implication cannot be reversed for the fidelity comparison.

02

Give an exact majorization counterexample

At gain ten, the spectra (0.8,0.16,0.04) and (0.9,0.06,0.04) have the required majorization relation but violate the proposed fidelity ordering. Rational square-root inequalities certify separation of the scalar values. The proof does not rely on floating-point numerical evaluation.

03

Evaluate the infimum over simple spectra

A normalized geometric family makes every smaller-to-larger eigenvalue ratio tend to zero. The corresponding mode factors approach their boundary values and attain the infimum in the limiting sense. This explicitly evaluates the scalar upper bound used in the all-density converse; existence of such spectra is part of the proof.

04

Combine independent all-density bounds

Restricting the adversarial family to suitable compact spectral sets gives an upper bound for the all-state optimum. The full-rank-bound PCT guarantee supplies a lower bound valid on every density matrix. These give a liminf/limsup sandwich with explicit but generally different quantities, rather than an equality.

theorem all_density_minimax_bounds (d : ℕ) (m : ℕ→ℕ) (γ : ℝ) (hγ : 1<γ)
    (hgain : Tendsto (fun n => (m n:ℝ)/n) atTop (𝓝 γ)) :
    γ^(-(((d+1:ℕ):ℝ)^2-1)/2)≤liminf (fun n => value d n (m n)) atTop ∧
    limsup (fun n => value d n (m n)) atTop≤
      classicalValue γ (d+1)*γ^(-(((d+1:ℕ):ℝ)*(((d+1:ℕ):ℝ)-1))/4)
05

Compare their large-gain scales

Algebraic factorization identifies the upper bound’s leading constant 2^((D−1)/2) and decay exponent (D²−1)/4. The available lower bound has exponent (D²−1)/2. This quantified gap records what is still unknown about the all-density minimax problem.
