Constructed LAN and fidelity transfer

Paper: Theorem 3.2 · Corollary 3.4 · Lemma 3.5 · §3.2 · §3.1 · §3.3

Local asymptotic normality is implemented by channels in both directions between tensor-power states and a classical Gaussian field of thermal oscillator states. The target sample size changes the local displacement by √γ while leaving the reference covariance and thermal parameters fixed.

Where this guide fits in the paper

Expands the LAN theorem, orbital reduction, and fidelity-transfer lemma in Section 3.2, with the coordinates of Section 3.1 and their use in Section 3.3. The paper invokes the cited finite-dimensional LAN construction; Lean constructs genuine forward and reverse channel witnesses internally and then proves target scaling and transfer on fixed compact windows.

Setting and assumptions

  • The reference spectrum is positive and simple.
  • Local spectral directions lie in the full trace-zero hyperplane, and local parameters range over a fixed compact window.
  • Whitening frames and positive-root enumerations are coordinate choices whose existence is proved upstream.
01

Construct both directions of the experiment

The forward channel combines the physical Schur decomposition, the classical label interpolation, and sector embeddings into oscillator coordinates. The reverse channel reconstructs physical blocks and supplies a channel completion away from controlled subspaces. The compact-window theorem constructs a witness containing actual all-input channels and uniform approximation statements.

02

Account for the target sample size

A perturbation of order n^(−1/2) is seen at output size m as a local shift multiplied by √(m/n). Model continuity and m/n→γ replace this finite scale by √γ uniformly on each fixed window. This rescales the mean and displacement, not the reference spectral covariance or thermal state.

03

Transfer every competing channel

Compose a reverse LAN channel, an arbitrary physical competitor, and a forward LAN channel. Trace-norm contraction controls the input error uniformly in the competitor, while fidelity continuity gives an additive √δ+√η error. The comparison therefore survives optimization over all physical channels.

Paper: Lemma 3.5

theorem fidelity_transfer
    (S : HybridToQuantum G A μ) (M : QuantumChannel A B) (T : QuantumToHybrid B H μ)
    (ρ : PositiveTraceClass A) (σ : PositiveTraceClass B)
    (Φ : PositiveL1 G μ) (Ψ : PositiveL1 H μ)
    (hσ : ‖σ.1‖ = 1) (hΦ : ‖Φ.1‖ = 1) {δ η : ℝ}
    (hδ : ‖S.map Φ.1 - ρ.1‖ ≤ δ) (hη : ‖T.map σ.1 - Ψ.1‖ ≤ η) :
    (ρ.map M.toPositiveTracePreservingMap).rootFidelity σ ≤
      (Φ.map (transferred S M T)).rootFidelity Ψ + Real.sqrt δ + Real.sqrt η
04

Restrict to a fixed-spectrum orbit

For known-spectrum cloning, the spectral Gaussian carries no unknown local parameter. Preparing or removing that classical register gives quantum-only comparison channels. The orbital window error theorem provides the reverse-input and forward-target estimates used by the known-spectrum converse.

Paper: Corollary 3.4

theorem known_fixedWindow_errors
    (p : SimpleSpectrum (k+1)) (b : Fin (k+1) → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin (k+1)))
    (e : Fin s ≃ PairIndex (k+1)) (lan : CompactWindowLAN p b e)
    (m : ℕ → ℕ) (g : ℝ) (hg : 0 < g)
    (hratio : Tendsto (fun n => (m n : ℝ)/(n : ℝ)) atTop (𝓝 g)) (L : ℕ) :
    ∃ δ η : ℕ → ℝ, Tendsto δ atTop (𝓝 0) ∧ Tendsto η atTop (𝓝 0) ∧
      ∀ᶠ n in atTop, ∀ ξ ∈ phaseSpaceBox 0 s ((L : ℝ)+1),
        let U := localUnitary p (orbitalParameters e ξ) n
        (‖(removeClassicalReverse (lan.reverse n)).map (translatedPositive ξ (orbitalReference p e)).1 -
          (tensorState (orbitState p U) n).1‖ ≤ δ n) ∧
        (‖(removeClassicalForward (lan.forward (m n))).map (tensorState (orbitState p U) (m n)).1 -
          (translatedPositive (Real.sqrt g • ξ) (orbitalReference p e)).1‖ ≤ η n)

Scope of this step

  • LAN is local on fixed compact windows. It does not assert one approximation uniform over all unbounded local parameters.
  • Some reusable lemmas accept a LAN witness as an argument; the final physical theorems instantiate that argument with the constructed witness.
